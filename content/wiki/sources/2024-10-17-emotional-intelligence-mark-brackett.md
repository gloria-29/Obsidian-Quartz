# Emotional Intelligence with Dr. Mark Brackett

## Summary
Dr. Mark Brackett, professor of psychology at Yale University and director of the Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence, defines EQ as the ability to reason with and about emotions. He introduces the RULER framework: Recognize, Understand, Label, Express, and Regulate emotions. The discussion challenges the oversimplification of emotions through emoji use, emphasizes the importance of emotional granularity (distinguishing anxiety from stress from fear from overwhelm), and highlights how misinterpreting emotional root causes leads to inappropriate responses.

## Core Ideas
- **RULER framework**: five discrete EQ skills—Recognize, Understand, Label, Express, Regulate
- **EQ is not a singular construct** but a set of discrete, learnable skills
- **Emotional granularity matters**: distinguishing similar emotions is crucial for effective regulation
- **Emoji oversimplification** can hinder self-awareness and communication
- **Body awareness** is foundational to emotional recognition

## Key Concepts
- [[Emotional Intelligence]] as intra- and interpersonal skill
- [[RULER framework]] for emotional skill development
- Emotional differentiation: anxiety vs. stress vs. fear vs. overwhelm
- Misattribution of emotions leading to inappropriate responses
- Academic critique vs. online discourse and impact on EQ
