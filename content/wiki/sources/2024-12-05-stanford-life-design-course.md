# Stanford Life Design Course: Rethinking Your Life

## Summary
A deep dive into the Stanford Life Design course methodology, applying design thinking principles to life planning. The approach emphasizes reframing problems (identifying the right problem before solving), accepting "gravity problems" (immovable constraints), and using tools like the Health-Work-Play-Love dashboard and Good Time Journal. A counterintuitive core message: you don't need to find your passion to be happy—passion often emerges after you design a life that regularly puts you in flow states. Prototyping (testing life options in small experiments) is preferred over big leaps.

## Core Ideas
- **Reframe the problem before solving it**: like fixing a leaky faucet when the real issue is a busted pipe
- **Gravity problems**: accept immovable constraints (low pay in certain fields, family obligations) and work around them
- **Passion follows action, not vice versa**: design a life with regular flow states, and passion emerges naturally
- **Prototyping**: test career/life changes in small experiments before committing (shadowing, weekend trials, informational interviews)
- **The Health-Work-Play-Love dashboard**: assess balance across four life domains to spot neglected areas

## Key Concepts
- Work view and Life view as personal compass
- Good Time Journal + AEIOU method (Activities, Environment, Interactions, Objects, Users)
- Flow state ([[Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi]]): total absorption where time flies
- [[Design thinking]] applied to personal life
- Prototyping as a low-risk experimentation strategy
- [[Deep Work]] and the relationship to flow
