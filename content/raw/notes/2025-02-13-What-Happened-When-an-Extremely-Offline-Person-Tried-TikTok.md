_For this week’s Infinite Scroll column, Cal Newport is filling in for Kyle Chayka_.  
本周无限滚动专栏由 Cal Newport 代班 Kyle Chayka。

In 2013, I wrote a blog post titled “Why I Never Joined Facebook.” Social media had grown so ubiquitous that I felt obligated to justify my abstention; I pointed out that I didn’t need it because it didn’t solve any actual problems in my life.  
2013 年，我写了一篇名为“我为何从未加入 Facebook”的博客文章。社交媒体已经变得无处不在，我觉得有必要为自己的抵制行为做出解释；我指出，我不需要它，因为它没有解决我生活中的任何实际问题。  
The post prompted an energetic discussion in the comments section, leading to a series of follow-up essays in which I tried to rebut the arguments for platforms such as Facebook and Twitter. “Fear of missing out . . .  
该帖子在评论区引发了一场热烈的讨论，导致一系列后续文章的发表，我在这些文章中试图反驳 Facebook 和 Twitter 等平台的观点。“害怕错过……  
is not a valid argument for trashing what you already have,” I observed in one earnest passage. In 2016, I consolidated my ideas into a [book](https://www.amazon.com/Deep-Work-Focused-Success-Distracted/dp/0349413681) chapter called “Quit Social Media,” and, when I adapted them into a TEDx talk and a [_Times_ op-ed](https://www.nytimes.com/2016/11/20/jobs/quit-social-media-your-career-may-depend-on-it.html), both went viral.  
“这不是一个有效的论据，用来否定你已有的东西，”我在一段诚挚的段落中观察到。2016 年，我将我的想法整合成了一章书，名为“退出社交媒体”，当我将其改编成 TEDx 演讲和《时代》杂志的社论时，两者都迅速走红。

I had upset many techno-optimists.  
我惹恼了许多技术乐观主义者。  
The technology writer and researcher Alexandra Samuel, for example, joined me on a radio show and said, “I think it’s far more useful to ask yourself how this incredibly powerful medium can actually support you in your own personal goals.” But things changed after the 2016 election, one of many unsettling upheavals shaped in part by social media.  
该技术作家和研究员亚历山德拉·萨缪尔，例如，加入了我的一档广播节目，并表示：“我认为，思考这种极其强大的媒介如何实际上支持你实现个人目标，要远比这更有用。”但在 2016 年选举之后，事情发生了变化，这是由社交媒体部分塑造的许多令人不安的动荡之一。  
Public opinion gradually turned against big-tech platforms, and my offline status no longer seemed so alarming. I moved on, happily disconnected from whatever the world’s five billion social-media users were doing with their online lives.  
公众舆论逐渐转向反对大型科技平台，我的离线状态也不再显得那么令人担忧。我继续前进，快乐地与世界五亿社交媒体用户在线生活的所作所为断绝了联系。

Then, last month, I had surgery and found myself at home for several weeks, recovering. Seeking something both interesting and relatively undemanding to occupy my time, I began revisiting my decade-old arguments for quitting social media and wondering how much they still applied.  
然后，上个月，我做了手术，自己在家里休养了几周，恢复中。为了找些既有趣又不太费力的东西来打发时间，我开始重新审视我十年前关于退出社交媒体的论点，思考它们现在还有多少适用性。  
I was particularly interested in TikTok, which launched in 2017 and quickly displaced Facebook, Twitter, and Instagram for many young people.  
我对 TikTok 特别感兴趣，该平台于 2017 年推出，迅速取代了 Facebook、Twitter 和 Instagram，成为许多年轻人的首选。  
It caught my attention because it was so common among the college students I teach, and the Supreme Court was in the process of deciding whether the U.S. should be allowed to ban it—or else force its Chinese owners to sell.  
它引起了我的注意，因为它在我所教的大学学生中非常普遍，而最高法院正在决定是否应允许美国禁止它——或者强迫其中国所有者出售。  
(A ruling will be released any day now.) So I took a step that would have nauseated an earlier version of myself: I downloaded the TikTok app, while I still could, to find out what all the fuss was about.  
(任何一天都将发布一项裁决。)所以我采取了一步，这可能会让早先的我感到恶心：在我还能做到的时候，我下载了 TikTok 应用，以了解所有这些喧嚣的原因。

When I sign in for the first time, TikTok asks me to choose my interests from a long list illustrated by cheerful emojis. I select “Life Hacks,” “Science and Education,” and “Sports.” Then I’m off.  
当我第一次登录时，TikTok 让我从一张由愉快的表情符号装饰的长列表中选择我的兴趣。我选择了“生活小窍门”、“科学与教育”和“体育”。然后我就开始了。  
The first video shows the Clemson University baseball team playing an exhibition game against the Savannah Bananas, a professional touring squad. The Clemson infield, for some inscrutable reason, starts dancing. I swipe up.  
第一段视频展示了克莱姆森大学棒球队与萨凡纳香蕉队进行的一场表演赛，萨凡纳香蕉队是一支职业巡回队伍。由于某种难以理解的原因，克莱姆森内场球员开始跳舞。我向上滑动。  
A new video begins, showing someone selecting shoes at a store. The video is only ten seconds long; by the time I’ve finished jotting down some notes, it has already started replaying. I hastily swipe again.  
一段新视频开始，显示有人在商店里挑选鞋子。视频只有十秒长；在我匆忙记下一些笔记的时候，它已经开始了重播。我急忙再次滑动。  
The next video plays tranquil music while a car slowly drives toward Yosemite National Park. The algorithm must have noticed that I lingered on the wintry scene: the next video shows someone sweeping snow off a porch with some kind of rotating broom contraption.  
下一视频播放着宁静的音乐，一辆汽车缓缓驶向优胜美地国家公园。算法一定注意到了我停留在冬日景象上的时间：下一个视频显示有人用某种旋转扫帚装置清扫门廊上的雪。  
Then the feed takes a darker turn, which makes me want to scroll even faster.  
然后，内容变得更为阴暗，这让我想更快地滚动。  
I see a news story about a person being pushed onto subway tracks in Manhattan—swipe—a Trump video set to ominous music—swipe—“Top 15 Most Ghetto High Schools in New Jersey”—swipe—and someone making fun of a server’s accent in a restaurant. I shut down the app.  
我看到一条关于曼哈顿有人被推上地铁轨道的新闻——滑动——特朗普的视频配上令人不安的音乐——滑动——“新泽西州前 15 名最贫民窟高中”——滑动——还有人在餐厅里取笑服务员的口音。我关闭了应用程序。

The velocity of the clips and the rawness of their emotion is breathtaking. I immediately feel old, like a grandparent encountering a smartphone for the first time. What I notice most, though, is TikTok’s lack of obvious purpose.  
剪辑的速度和情感的直接性令人叹为观止。我立刻感到自己老了，就像第一次遇到智能手机的祖父母。然而，我注意到最多的却是 TikTok 缺乏明显的目的。  
In a 2013 blog post called “Why I’m (Still) Not Going to Join Facebook,” I described a common argument in favor of legacy social media: that it “makes it possible to maintain lightweight, high-frequency contact with a large number of people.” This is clearly not the function of TikTok, which does not revolve around following friends or posting updates about one’s life.  
在 2013 年的一篇博客文章《为什么我（仍然）不会加入 Facebook》中，我描述了一个支持传统社交媒体的常见论点：它“使得与大量人保持轻量级、高频次的联系成为可能。”这显然不是 TikTok 的功能，TikTok 并不围绕关注朋友或发布个人生活更新来运作。  
When I first signed up, the app didn’t even require me to pick a username; it asked only for my phone number and birthday. According to Pew Research, the typical TikTok user never adds information to their account’s “bio” field.  
当我第一次注册时，这个应用甚至不需要我选择用户名；它只要求我提供电话号码和生日。根据皮尤研究中心的调查，典型的 TikTok 用户永远不会向他们账户的“个人简介”字段添加信息。  
They’re happy to remain anonymous consumers of content. (I’d later learn that many people share TikToks via texts or instant messages.)  
他们乐于保持匿名，作为内容的消费者。（我后来得知许多人通过短信或即时消息分享抖音视频。）

My blog post also considered the once sacrosanct idea that social media provides important “professional benefits.” After I wrote my _Times_ piece, the paper went so far as to publish a [rebuttal from Patrick Gillooly](https://www.nytimes.com/2016/12/03/jobs/dont-quit-social-media-put-it-to-work-for-your-career-instead.html), then the director of digital communications and social media at Monster, a Web site for recruiters and job seekers.  
我的博客文章还考虑了曾经神圣不可侵犯的观点，即社交媒体提供了重要的“职业利益”。在我为《时代》杂志撰写文章之后，该报纸甚至发表了一篇来自帕特里克·吉洛利的反驳文章，当时他是招聘网站 Monster 的数字传播和社交媒体总监。  
“As someone who spends the majority of his work time on social media helping people find careers they’ll love, I disagree with his assessment,” Gillooly said of my piece. “I believe that you should _not_ quit social media—and that doing so will actually damage your career.” Does anyone other than TikTok influencers think that TikTok is somehow beneficial to their career? The opposite possibility, that a social post could get you fired, might be more likely.  
作为一名将大部分工作时间花在社交媒体上帮助人们找到他们热爱的职业的人，我不同意他的评估，”吉尔卢利关于我的作品说。“我认为你不应该放弃社交媒体——而且这样做实际上会损害你的职业生涯。”除了 TikTok 网红，还有谁认为 TikTok 以某种方式对他们的事业有益？相反的可能性，即一条社交媒体帖子可能会让你被解雇，可能更可能。  
(Fittingly, videos about getting fired for posting on TikTok are a popular genre on TikTok.)  
(恰如其分的是，关于因在 TikTok 上发布内容而被解雇的视频在 TikTok 上成为了一种流行类型。)

Then there’s the once popular argument that [social media is an online “town square”](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/cultural-comment/we-dont-need-a-new-twitter)—that a Twitter trend, a widely read Facebook post, or an Instagram meme can become a locus of collective conversation.  
然后是曾经流行的观点，即社交媒体是一个在线“广场”——推特趋势、广泛阅读的 Facebook 帖子或 Instagram 迷因可以成为集体对话的焦点。  
Some legacy platforms, most notably X, still cling to this notion, but TikTok and Reels, the part of Instagram that spotlights short-form video, don’t seem to care about shared experiences.  
一些遗留平台，尤其是 X，仍然坚持这一观念，但 TikTok 和 Reels，即 Instagram 中突出短视频的部分，似乎并不关心共享体验。  
Some videos might get viewed millions of times, but, in general, feeds are customized by each user’s individualized curation algorithm.  
一些视频可能被观看数百万次，但总的来说，内容流是根据每个用户的个性化推荐算法定制的。  
My encounters with dancing baseball players and rotating snow brooms were not grist for the mill of public discussion; this experience was unique to me.  
我的与跳舞的棒球运动员和旋转的扫雪铲的遭遇并不适合成为公共讨论的素材；这种经历对我来说是独一无二的。

I found these realizations dislocating. A decade ago, I understood the arguments in favor of social media, even if I didn’t always agree with them. But I was learning that I had no idea what fuels its current appeal.  
我发现这些认识令人困惑。十年前，我理解了支持社交媒体的论点，即使我并不总是同意它们。但我正在意识到，我对是什么推动了它当前的吸引力一无所知。  
Seeking clarity, I called Zack, a twenty-four-year-old former student of mine. I had a simple question for him: why?  
寻求清晰，我打电话给了我的前学生 Zack，他今年二十四岁。我问他一个简单的问题：为什么？

“I use it pretty much exclusively either to view content that my friends have shared with me,” Zack tells me, “or to look for content to share with my friends and family.” As if on cue, he’s interrupted by a text message from a friend.  
“我几乎完全用它来查看朋友们分享给我的内容，”扎克告诉我，“或者寻找可以与我朋友和家人分享的内容。” 就在这时，他收到了一条来自朋友的短信。  
It links to a TikTok video, which he forwards to me.  
它链接到一个 TikTok 视频，他把它转发给我。  
The clip, which is captioned “Always ready,” opens with a shot of feet on a bedroom carpet, along with the text: “Golf: ‘buddy be ready by 8am.’ ” Expectant music plays as the feet walk toward what appears to be a sleeping figure on a bed.  
该片段标题为“随时待命”，以一双在卧室地毯上的脚的镜头开始，同时显示文字：“高尔夫：‘伙伴，早上 8 点准备好。’”随着脚步走向床上看似熟睡的人物，期待的音乐响起。  
When a hand pulls back the covers, however, the camera suddenly pans up to reveal a young man standing on the bed in full golf attire, holding a club as though ready to swing. The text changes: “me at 7:59 am.” The music swells. Then it’s done. I’m shocked by how short it is.  
当一只手拉起被子时，然而，摄像机突然向上移动，揭示一个穿着全套高尔夫服装的年轻人站在床上，手持球杆，仿佛准备挥杆。文本变为：“我在早上 7:59。”音乐增强。然后结束了。我对它的短暂感到震惊。

“That’s it?” I ask.  “这就完了？”我问。

“It’s funny!” Zack says.  它很有趣！Zack 说。

He forwards me another TikTok that his brother enjoyed.  
他转发给我另一个他兄弟喜欢的 TikTok。  
It shows the Swiss national soccer team playing a match, along with the words, “Me explaining what toblerone is to someone that’s never had 1.” An announcer uses the phrase “neat little Swiss triangles.” Five seconds have passed; the video ends.  
它展示了瑞士国家队正在比赛，同时配文“向从未尝过的人来说解释托布勒 one 是什么。”解说员使用了“小巧的瑞士三角形”这个短语。过去了五秒钟；视频结束。

I sense that funny TikToks take on a special challenge: using visuals to encode as much information as possible in as little time as possible. For the viewer, there’s a joy in decoding them. Zack sends me a calculus gag: a man and a woman are on a date, having a drink.  
我感觉到搞笑的 TikTok 面临着一个特殊的挑战：在尽可能短的时间内，用视觉传达尽可能多的信息。对于观众来说，解码它们是一种乐趣。Zack 给我发了一个微积分笑话：一对男女在约会，喝着饮料。  
A caption floats above the woman: “I’ll change him.” Near her face is d/dx, the mathematical symbol for taking a derivative. Superimposed on the man is the function e
^x. You have to have studied calculus to know that the derivative of e^
x is itself e
^x.  
一个标题浮在女人上方：“我会改变他。”在她脸旁是 d/dx，这是求导的数学符号。在男人身上叠加的是函数 e^x。你必须学过微积分才能知道 e^x 的导数是它本身 e^x。  
I let out an audible chortle. Then I ask myself, How many people could possibly appreciate this? “That meme format also requires context to understand,” Zack says. “Very specific humor for a small subset of people.”  
我发出一声可闻的咯咯笑声。然后我问自己，有多少人可能欣赏这个？“那种梗格式也需要上下文才能理解，”扎克说。“对一小部分人来说是非常具体的幽默。”

Sometimes when Zack hangs out with his brother, they’ll browse clips on TikTok. “We’ll watch them and laugh together,” he explains. His sister, however, is less interested in comedy than in getting glimpses of various activities.  
有时，当 Zack 和他的兄弟一起闲逛时，他们会在 TikTok 上浏览剪辑。“我们会一起看并笑出声，”他解释道。然而，他的妹妹对喜剧的兴趣不如对窥见各种活动的兴趣大。  
“I feel like I’m living in other peoples’ lives,” she recently told him.  
我感觉我像是在过别人的生活，”她最近告诉他。

I ask another of my students, a nineteen-year-old undergraduate named Lizzie, how she uses TikTok.  
我问我的另一个学生，一个名叫 Lizzie 的 19 岁本科生，她如何使用 TikTok。  
She describes a sense of authenticity that comes with the democratic nature of the platform: “anyone can go viral.” She sends me a minute-long montage of soldiers coming home from deployment and surprising their kids, played against patriotic background music.  
她描述了一种与平台民主性质相关的真实感：“任何人都可以走红。”她给我发了一段一分钟的剪辑，展示了士兵们从部署地回家，惊喜地见到孩子们，背景音乐是爱国歌曲。  
It’s not a slick production, but it’s undeniably touching.  
这不是一个光滑的制作，但无疑令人感动。

She also sends a TikTok that shows the making of a Caprese-style sandwich. Watching the basil getting crushed and the crusty bread being sliced is oddly hypnotic. But can someone actually follow these recipes, given how quickly they roll past?  
她还在 TikTok 上发布了一段制作卡普雷塞风三明治的视频。看着罗勒被压碎和硬面包被切片，感觉有点着迷。但考虑到它们滚动的速度，有人真的能按照这些食谱操作吗？  
“People use them for relaxation and learning,” she says.  
人们用它们来放松和学习，她说。

A decade ago, I viewed social media as Manichaean: these platforms could distract and mislead their users, but they could also topple dictators and enable free expression. These competing impulses have always been at war with each other.  
十年前，我把社交媒体看作是善恶二元对立的：这些平台可能会分散和误导用户，但它们也可能推翻独裁者并促进自由表达。这些相互竞争的冲动始终在相互斗争。  
Serious thinkers have written articles and books about how to make social media a force for good, while critics such as [Jaron Lanier](https://www.newyorker.com/science/annals-of-artificial-intelligence/how-to-picture-ai), who wrote a 2018 book titled “[Ten Arguments for Deleting Your Social Media Accounts Right Now](https://www.amazon.com/Arguments-Deleting-Social-Media-Accounts/dp/1250239087),” expressed skepticism about that vision. But much of the content on TikTok, and on comparable services like Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, borders on nihilism. It seems to revel in meaninglessness, sometimes even poking fun at the idea that a video should be useful.  
严肃的思想家们撰写了文章和书籍，探讨如何让社交媒体成为一股正能量，而像 Jaron Lanier 这样的批评者，他于 2018 年出版了一本名为《现在就删除你的社交媒体账号的十个论点》的书，对这种愿景表示怀疑。但 TikTok 以及类似的服务如 Instagram Reels 和 YouTube Shorts 上的大部分内容几乎接近虚无主义。它们似乎在享受无意义，有时甚至嘲笑视频应该有用的想法。  
The most popular platforms are saying the quiet part out loud—that there is no deeply meaningful justification for their digital wares—and their users seem to understand and accept this new agreement.  
最受欢迎的平台大声说出他们内心的话——那就是他们的数字产品没有深刻的、有意义的理由——而他们的用户似乎理解并接受了这个新协议。  
TikTok is “a gold mine for people with short attention spans who crave quick bursts of dopamine,” Lizzie told me. As if to underscore this point, she forwarded me a video of dog barks that sound like human speech. “I love you,” a blue-eyed husky mix appears to growl.  
抖音是“对那些渴望快速多巴胺冲动的注意力短暂的人的‘金矿’，”莉兹告诉我。为了强调这一点，她转发给我一个听起来像人类说话的狗吠视频。“我爱你，”一只蓝眼睛的哈士奇混血狗似乎在咆哮。
