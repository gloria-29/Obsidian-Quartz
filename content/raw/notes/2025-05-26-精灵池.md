As soon as she touched down in Scotland, she believed in fairies. No, as soon as the rock and velvet of Inverness rushed up to her where she was falling, a long way through the hagstone hole of a cloud, and she plunged down into the center of the cloud and stayed there.  
她刚抵达苏格兰，就相信了仙子的存在。不，当她触及因弗内斯的地表，天鹅绒般的岩石和天鹅绒般的地表涌向她，她从云的蛤石洞中跌落，远远地，然后她一头扎进云的中心，并停留在那里。  
You used to set a child out for them, she thought, and was caught in the arms and awoke on the green hillside.  
她想起，人们过去会把孩子放在那里，她心想，然后被抱在怀里醒来，在绿色的山丘上。

“Sco’land,” she heard her mother say, in a voice weak from lack of iced tea—they were barely alive after five hours on the runway in Chicago and another ten in the air. “It’s not the first real day,” her husband kept reminding them.  
“斯科兰，”她听到母亲说，声音因缺少冰茶而显得微弱——他们在芝加哥的秀场上待了五个小时，又在空中飞行了另一个十分钟，几乎奄奄一息。“这不是真正的第一天，”她的丈夫不断提醒他们。  
After a short stay in Inverness that night, they would go on to Skye tomorrow. He jangled a set of keys.  
那天晚上在因弗内斯短暂停留后，他们明天将前往斯凯。他摇响了钥匙串。  
In Ireland, two years before, it had transpired that her husband, a contrarian, was born to drive on the wrong side of the road, while her mother, a worse contrarian—whom she had defeated by marrying the former—planned to drag them all to Hell that way.  
在爱尔兰，两年前发生了一件怪事，她的丈夫，一个逆流而上的人，出生在错误的一侧道路上驾驶，而她的母亲，一个更糟糕的逆流而上的人——她通过嫁给前者来击败了她——计划这样把他们所有人拖进地狱。  
“I’ll drive,” he told her mother now, very loudly, “and you sit on the passenger side and slam your foot to the floor whenever I get too close to a low stone wall.” “Deal,” her mother agreed. She would also provide commentary, and throw in those sharp little gasps for free.  
“我来开车，”他现在大声对母亲说，“你坐在乘客座位上，每当我觉得离低矮的石头墙太近时，就猛踩油门。” “成交，”母亲同意了。她还会提供评论，并且免费加入那些尖锐的小喘息声。

As for her, she was silent and let herself be carried on and on toward the green hillside. The trees corresponded to an obsessive high-school reading of “The White Goddess.” The sheep were spray‑painted according to who owned them.  
至于她，她沉默着，任由自己被带到那片绿色山坡上。树木对应着她对《白女神》的强迫性高中阅读。绵羊是根据谁拥有它们而被喷漆的。  
Actual lambs frisked in the fields, on legs like little girls’. Wait, she thought, am I confused about changelings? You put them out—did you get anything back? Or they were taken from you in the night, and you woke up one morning...  
真实的羊羔在田野里嬉戏，腿像小女孩的。等一下，她想，我对掉包婴孩是不是搞错了？你把它们放出去——你得到什么回来了吗？或者它们在夜里被带走了，你醒来在一个早晨……

Her sister sat apart from her in the rental car. Her head was full of the Child, her child, who’d been lost to them all just that January.  
她的姐姐在租来的车里和她分开坐着。她的脑海里充满了那个孩子，她的孩子，那个在去年一月被所有人失去的孩子。  
From time to time she saw her sister flicking through pictures on her phone with a chipped hot-pink manicure, so quickly that the Child appeared to be alive.  
不时地，她看到姐姐用一块剥落的粉红色指甲油在手机上翻看照片，动作之快，仿佛孩子般生动。  
It was as if her sister were smoothing a forehead, or touching away an eyelash, or wiping milk from a mouth, and once again the whole life was in motion.  
好像姐姐在抚平额头，或是抹去一根睫毛，或是擦去嘴边的牛奶，整个生命再次活跃起来。

“Wide load,” she heard her mother saying to the asses of the sheep, as if they were women.  
她听到母亲对羊的屁股说“超载”，好像它们是女人一样。

Upon arrival at the hotel, instead of taking pictures of the North Sea she took pictures of elk bellowing on the walls and a little guy who appeared to be penetrating his bagpipes. This was her typical routine in other countries—to first take pictures of their pictures.  
到达酒店后，她没有拍照北海，而是拍下了墙上咆哮的麋鹿和一个小伙子似乎在吹奏风笛。这在她去其他国家时的典型做法——先拍下他们的照片。  
It was a way of entering into the spirit, as was eating the complimentary oatcakes that had been left in your room, with its conscious and unconscious plaids: light, shadow, her hand crossed over her sister’s, and that life called through‑the‑window.  
那是一种进入精神世界的方式，就像吃掉留在你房间里的免费燕麦饼一样，带着它有意识和无意识的格子图案：光、阴影、她交叉在姐姐手上的手，以及那透过窗户呼喊的生命。 %%

Between the time her mother had gone into her hotel room and the time she reappeared in the hall, her jeans had become wet. They would not dry for the rest of the trip. The wetness came to represent, in their minds, the iced tea she could never get. “Tea...  
在她母亲进入酒店房间和她重新出现在走廊之间的时间里，她的牛仔裤已经湿了。它们在剩下的旅途中都不会干。在他们心中，这湿漉漉的感觉代表着她永远无法得到的冰茶。“茶……”  
with ice?” she would ask hopefully, making a series of gestures to communicate the concept of iced tea, and would be brought a cup with three cubes in it by someone who looked almost medically concerned.  
“加冰？”她满怀希望地问道，做出一系列手势来表达冰茶的概念，然后有人递给她一杯装有三块冰的杯子，看起来几乎像是出于医疗上的关心。

“Tea... with ice?” she asked at the Jamaican restaurant, holding up her hands like shocked daisies next to her eyes. They had gone to three or four places before finding one that was open. The chef, in a white hat, laughed and entered her mother’s conspiracy.  
“茶……加冰？”她在牙买加餐厅问道，举起手像受惊的雏菊一样放在眼睛旁边。他们找过三四个地方才找到一家开门的餐厅。那位戴白色厨师帽的主厨笑着加入了她母亲的阴谋。

The night flowed like just‑struck oil outside the windows; she tried to fix the details of it in her mind. She never remembered the first night in another country—it was as if the change in altitude threw her off, or she really had touched down in another world.  
那晚的夜色如同刚开采的石油一样在窗外流淌；她试图在脑海中固定那些细节。她从未记得在国外过的第一个夜晚——仿佛海拔的变化让她失去了平衡，或者她真的降落在了另一个世界。  
The restaurant, she would not remember later, was actually called Kool Runnings.  
她后来不会记得，那家餐厅实际上叫做 Kool Runnings。

Back at the hotel bar, the bartender told them the lamentable story of Irn‑Bru. It had once been sweeter; now it was less sweet. There were petitions, and people hoarding it by the case in their flats.  
回到酒店酒吧，酒保告诉他们 Irn-Bru 的悲惨故事。它曾经更甜；现在不那么甜了。有人提出请愿，人们把一箱一箱地囤积在自己的公寓里。  
She ordered a can from him to make friends, for she saw no other way of doing it. Scottish bartenders had been given the opposite instructions to American ones. The taste, a pink electrocution of the tongue, was indescribable—and there was a version that was more so?  
她从他那里买了一罐来交朋友，因为她看不到其他的方法。苏格兰酒保得到的指示与美国酒保相反。味道，一种粉色的电击舌头，难以描述——而且还有一种味道更甚？  
She decided that if she were presented with a petition she would sign it. And also support Scottish independence, if that was correct. Maybe, now that they were friends, she could ask the bartender. But “Tea... with ice?” her mother asked him, and the chance was gone.  
她决定，如果有人递给她请愿书，她会签字。而且，如果那是正确的，她也会支持苏格兰独立。也许，既然他们现在是朋友，她可以问酒保。但“茶……加冰？”她母亲问他，机会就这样消失了。

And so that was Loch Ness? She followed her sister through the ruins of the castle that overlooked it, taking pictures of her in empty windows. Her gold hair whipped. Her pink lipstick, drawn far beyond the outline of her mouth, smiled without her. People had really lived here.  
所以那就是尼斯湖？她跟着姐姐穿过俯瞰它的城堡废墟，在空无一人的窗户里给她拍照。她的金发在风中飞舞。她的粉色口红，画出了嘴唇轮廓之外的唇线，在她不在时微笑着。这里真的住过人。  
Could you still surprise someone’s breathing near the ceiling, as you could upstairs in her sister’s house? How long did that last?  
你还能像在她姐姐家楼上那样，在靠近天花板的地方让人屏住呼吸吗？那能持续多久？

Her husband rubbed his hands. Finally, a country where the women were wearing enough cloth, and where the wind persisted in exposing the tips of their ears. He had turned to pure itinerary: his mind was full of mileage, national parks, famed distilleries, tallest peaks.  
她丈夫搓了搓手。终于到了一个女性穿着足够蔽体的地方，而且风还总是吹得她们耳朵尖都露了出来。他完全沉浸在了行程规划中：脑子里全是里程数、国家公园、著名的酒厂、最高的山峰。  
At this point he believed he was Scottish—everywhere they went they saw bald heads that looked like his. “One of me!” he would cry, pointing them out. A country full of thousands, and she had reached out and taken one. She fondled the mica stone.  
到了这个地步，他觉得自己都像苏格兰人了——他们走到哪里都能看到和他长着一样光头的当地人。“又一个我！”他会指着他们喊道。一个满是成千上万人的国家，而她却伸出手，选中了一个。她抚摸着那块云母石。  
Maybe the soul was just that dearness nestled in the center of the body, like a chosen pebble in the palm of a hand. When you held someone, it was that dearness you felt, that chosenness.  
也许灵魂就是那样一种珍宝，安放在身体的中心，就像掌心里握着的一颗被选中的鹅卵石。当你拥抱某个人时，感受到的就是那种珍宝，那种被选中的感觉。

And he was dear. He would sniff the air and stop the car and say, “Macbeth lived here.” He knew where there was Red Bull and where there might be ice—three cubes of it—for her mother.  
他很亲切。他会嗅嗅空气，然后停车，说：“麦克白住在这里。”他知道哪里有红牛，哪里可能有冰块——三块——给她母亲。 %%  
In the car, they played a game of Would You Rather, except they misremembered it as I Would Never. This limited things somewhat. Her husband won easily. “I would NEVER do that!” he kept shouting, and then put down another point for himself.  
在车里，他们玩了一个“你宁愿”的游戏，但他们记成了“我绝不会”。这多少限制了些选择。她的丈夫轻松获胜。“我绝不会做那件事！”他不停地喊道，然后又为自己记下一分。 %%

He had done all this to rinse her sister’s mind of pain. Pain was one of the things he could not stand, along with Muppets and receipts, which were endocrine disrupters. “No, thank you,” he said to the man at the gas station when he purchased four cans of the less sweet Irn‑Bru.  
他做这一切是为了洗去她妹妹心中的痛苦。痛苦是他无法忍受的东西之一，还有木偶和收据，这些都是内分泌干扰物。“不，谢谢，”他在加油站对那个男人说，当时他买了四罐不那么甜的爱尔兰布鲁。 %%  
“Keep moving,” he told them all, in his long, striding body. One foot in front of the other, or die.  
“继续前进，”他告诉他们所有人，用他修长而大步的身躯说道。一步接一步，否则就得死。

She did what she always did in a car: looked out the window, trusted, and let herself be carried along. The easiest life to imagine was the life of the postman. The easiest life to imagine was the life of the man who ran the ferry.  
她在车里总是做同样的事情：望向窗外，信任，然后让自己随风而行。最容易想象的生活是邮递员的生活。最容易想象的生活是渡轮船长的生活。  
The easiest life to imagine was that of a child, in the castle that sat on the shore of Loch Ness, where the water was full, it was true, of little slipping necks. You could just grab on.  
最容易想象的生活是一个孩子的，他在尼斯湖岸边的城堡里，那里的水确实充满了小蛇颈。你只需紧紧抓住。

They took turns reading aloud from Wikipedia: about fighting hares, the symbology of thistles, which time of year the heather bloomed. The list of historical guys who actually believed in fairies was pretty long, she told them on the way to Glenbrittle.  
他们轮流大声朗读维基百科上的内容：关于打猎野兔，蓟的象征意义，石楠花何时盛开。她告诉他们，历史上真正相信仙女的人名单相当长，这是她在去格兰布里特的路上说的。  
Her husband would have to update her entry to say that she had joined it.  
她的丈夫必须更新她的资料，说她已经加入了。

The Fairy Pools had been poured down. The walk up was like a slow exploration of the skeleton of an animal, whose life was reënacted here and there by plunges of crystal water.  
小仙潭的水已经流干了。向上走就像缓慢探索一只动物 skeleton 的骨架，水晶般的水流在这里和那里重现着它的生命。  
There were natural bridges, caves heaped with uniform gray treasure, keyholes of bloodstone‑colored water, raindrops on the lens, trees clinging to cliffs, the falls too fast for cameras, like fairies, and the long natural stairs that her sister climbed.  
有天然桥梁，堆满均匀灰色宝藏的洞穴，血石色水的钥匙孔，镜头上的雨滴，紧贴悬崖的树木，对相机来说太快的水帘，像小仙女一样，还有她妹妹爬上的长长的天然楼梯。  
People spoke every language. Europeans, who knew which laws to disregard, were stripping off their clothes and swimming. You could pose with a Red Bull next to the tallest waterfall and make it seem like you were peeing. A perfect place.  
人们说着各种语言。欧洲人知道哪些法律可以无视，他们脱掉衣服在游泳。你可以站在最高的瀑布旁边，旁边放着一瓶红牛，看起来像你正在尿尿。一个完美的地方。

They drank the water. Her husband sat on the edge of the deepest pool, which touched the center of the earth, and scooped it into a survivalist water filter he had bought on the internet.  
他们喝了水。她的丈夫坐在最深的水池边缘，这个水池触及地球的中心，他用在网上买的生存者水过滤器舀起了水。  
“This will let you drink water from ANYWHERE, in any situation,” he had told her intensely when it arrived in the mail. More and more of these situations were arising.  
“这会让你在任何地方、任何情况下都能喝水，”当它寄到时，他激动地对她说。越来越多的这种情况出现了。  
As a child, she had watched Kevin Costner drink his own freshly distilled piss in “Waterworld” and just assumed it was something she would have to do as an adult, for the world would be different.  
作为孩子，她看过凯文·科斯特纳在《水世界》中喝自己新鲜蒸馏的尿，她只是以为作为成年人她也会这么做，因为世界会不同。  
They all drank a long swallow of the cool clear water, which was somehow inflected with the word “green.” It went clear into the center of her, through the hagstone hole and the natural arch, plunging down the stairs of living rock. Inside her, Europeans stripped and splashed.  
他们都喝了一大口清凉透明的水，这水不知为何带着“绿色”的意味。它清澈地流过她的中心，穿过蛤石洞和天然拱门，顺着活石阶流下。在她体内，欧洲人脱衣嬉戏。  
“Now we are refreshed,” he said. “Now we can go on.”  
“现在我们恢复了精神，”他说。“现在我们可以继续前行了。”

Twenty pictures in her photo roll later, her sister’s phone disappeared. Her black‑and‑white scarf and her rose‑gold phone, with the Child’s whole short life on it. It had been in the hospital with them, in her right hand, always.  
在她的相册里，二十张照片之后，她妹妹的手机不见了。她的黑白围巾和玫瑰金色的手机，上面记录着孩子短暂的一生。它曾和他们一起在医院里，一直握在她的右手。  
It had been what the next phone would not be, a warm eyewitness. Her sister was holding it in one picture, and then she wasn’t, a bald mountain behind her, her face closed, impassable; there and then gone.  
那将是下一部手机无法做到的，一个温暖的目击者。在一张照片中，她妹妹手持手机，然后就不见了，背后是一座光秃秃的山，她的脸紧闭，表情冷漠；就在那里消失了。

“I can find it,” she told her sister, desperate. She always had. Five dollars in a parking lot, when that was real money.  
“我能找到它，”她对妹妹说，语气绝望。她一直都能。停车场里五美元，那时候那才是真钱。

But there had been a switch. When she went up, the pools had looked one way, but when she came down again, searching for the rose‑gold phone, they looked another way—as if they were a story that needed to be told in order, from the beginning, without leaving anything out.  
但情况发生了变化。她上去时，池塘看起来一个样子，但当她再次下来，寻找玫瑰金手机时，它们看起来又不一样了——仿佛它们是一个需要按顺序讲述的故事，从头开始，不遗漏任何细节。  
Maybe this place was like the world—you could travel through it only once. She rolled her ankle on the rocks. An hour passed, and then another. “As soon as we get back in cellphone range,” her husband kept telling them, “we can just call...” But no one listened.  
也许这个地方就像世界——你只能一次通过它。她脚踝在石头上扭伤了。一个小时过去了，然后又是另一个小时。“只要我们一回到手机信号范围内，”她丈夫不停地对他们说，“我们就可以直接打电话……”但没有人听。  
Her sister’s sere hair went among the grasses; her head was full of the Child.  
她妹妹干枯的头发混在草丛中；她的头里充满了孩子。

“We shouldn’t have drunk the water,” they would say later. It had angered the fairies—no, not angered. They had simply demanded something in exchange. But as soon as they saw what was on the phone—the face, the flicking motion—they’d know it was too much.  
“我们本来就不应该喝那水，”他们后来会说。那激怒了仙女——不，不是激怒，她们只是要求交换一些东西。但一旦她们看到电话上的东西——面容，摆动的动作——她们就会知道这太过分了。  
They would give it back and keep only the scarf, which was just from Target.

巨人般的东西在她上方起伏，而她显得如此渺小。就在那里：她身后有一堆松散的图案，还有那玫瑰金手机的闪光。

“I can’t believe we got it back,” her sister said softly, spreading two fingers apart to zoom in.  
妹妹轻声说道：“我真的不敢相信我们把它找回来了，”她伸开两个手指放大镜头。  
When they finally made it into range, and called the number—she had to be the one to call, her husband was driving, her sister couldn’t speak, and her mother’s jeans were wet—someone actually answered, as her husband all along had promised would happen.  
当他们终于进入通话范围，并拨打了号码——她必须打电话，因为她的丈夫在开车，妹妹无法说话，而母亲的牛仔裤湿了——有人真的接了电话，正如她丈夫一直承诺的那样会发生。  
“I have it,” the woman said. “It’s safe.” She was driving it to the police station in Portree at that moment—the Child on her lap, with a triangle of face showing. “I love you,” she said simply to the woman on the phone, who understood her perfectly.  
“我有它，”女人说，“它很安全。”她当时正在开车将它送往波特里警察局——孩子坐在她的腿上，露出三角形的脸。“我爱你，”她简单地对电话那头的女人说，那个女人完全理解她。

“An American would never,” her husband said, happy. He had scored another point in the game.  
“一个美国人绝不会这样做，”她的丈夫高兴地说。他在游戏中又赢了一分。

But her breath would not stay inside her, as if it were pushing the wind. _I begged them to give it back, and they did_. There is an exchange, she thought. Something passes between this life and the next that allows you to be here for a while; the Fairy Pools don’t look the same on the way down as on the way up; and you walk through this world only once.  
但她的呼吸无法留在体内，就像它在推动风一样。我请求他们把它还给我，他们做到了。她心想，有一种交换。某种东西在这辈子和下一辈子之间传递，让你能在这里待一段时间；仙女池在下山时和上山时看起来不一样；而你只走过这个世界一次。  
She had drunk the coldest water in the world and thrown it up again green. No one could say she hadn’t really been here.  
她喝过世界上最冷的水，又吐了出来，绿色。没有人可以说她真的没有来过这里。

In her head she signed the petition for Scottish independence; in her head she signed the petition for Irn‑Bru to be sweet again. In her head she stacked a cairn. In her head she lifted the hard heart out of a grave.  
在她脑海里，她签署了苏格兰独立请愿书；在她脑海里，她签署了让爱尔兰布鲁再次变甜的请愿书。在她脑海里，她堆砌了一座石堆。在她脑海里，她从坟墓中抬出了一颗坚硬的心脏。  
The last thing she would do, before she left Scotland, was to stumble out of her hotel, in a straight line like a sleepwalker, and set the stone with the ring of mica in the knothole of a tree—not understanding why she was doing it, crying when she realized it was gone.  
在离开苏格兰之前，她最后要做的事，就是像梦游者一样直线踉跄地走出她的酒店，并将带有云母环的石头放在树的节孔中——不明白自己为何这样做，直到意识到石头不见了才哭泣。  
She walked through the world. The exchange rate stood. Everyone must pay. ♦  
她行走在世间。汇率依然。每个人都必须支付。♦
