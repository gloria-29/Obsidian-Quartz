There are awards for the year’s best films but not for its best [TikTok](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/infinite-scroll/what-happened-when-an-extremely-offline-person-tried-tiktok) videos. That’s too bad, since 2024 yielded several tiny masterpieces. From @yojairyjaimee, a flawless, minute-long re-creation of some bizarre 2009 stage patter by [Kanye West](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2024/06/17/kanye-west-tadao-ando-beach-house-malibu) (who now goes by Ye). From @accountwashackedwith50m, twelve seconds of chocolate-covered strawberries, filmed from the vantage of a saxophonist in an R. &amp; B. band.  
有年度最佳电影奖，但没有年度最佳 TikTok 视频奖。这真是遗憾，因为 2024 年产生了几部小巧的杰作。从@yojairyjaimee 那里，有一个完美的、一分钟长的 Kanye West（现在用 Ye 作为名字）2009 年舞台表演怪诞内容的重制。从@accountwashackedwith50m 那里，有十二秒的巧克力覆盖草莓的视频，是从一支 R&amp;B 乐队的 saxophonist 的视角拍摄的。  
From @notkenna, seven seconds of a dog made to look, with preposterously low-budget effects, as if it were flying on a broomstick. Such Internet gems are what the poet Patricia Lockwood has called “the sapphires of the instant”; each catches the light in a strange, hypnotic way.  
从@notkenna 发来的内容，展示了一段以极其低预算效果制作的七秒狗狗视频，使其看起来像是骑着扫帚飞行。诗人帕特里夏·洛伍德曾将这样的互联网珍宝称为“瞬时的蓝宝石”；每一段都以一种奇异而催眠的方式捕捉着光线。

Just don’t stare too long. If every video is a starburst of expression, an extended TikTok session is fireworks in your face for hours. That can’t be healthy, can it? In 2010, the technology writer Nicholas Carr presciently raised this concern in [“The Shallows: What the Internet Is Doing to Our Brains,”](https://www.amazon.com/Shallows-What-Internet-Doing-Brains/dp/0393357821/ref&#61;sr_1_1?crid&#61;1HS1D3S4Y1L10&amp;dib&#61;eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.AfyorzVhrGfRLFsNWZjMe2w6--h0uUq1G6KpGOr_bL2MucZEulEFxqD6-3xS7fmGbDfKyw8_kS4Ta-xE7VFylFjFdim_ObON3XlcrAHNUnI.ZjVBDMLVGB3F3-jzOkXT9jYvvjjgIkwXXI-QUJCly24&amp;dib_tag&#61;se&amp;keywords&#61;%E2%80%9CThe%20Shallows:%20What%20the%20Internet%20Is%20Doing%20to%20Our%20Brains%22&amp;qid&#61;1737064087&amp;sprefix&#61;the%20shallows%20what%20the%20internet%20is%20doing%20to%20our%20brains%20,aps,103&amp;sr&#61;8-1) a Pulitzer Prize finalist. “What the Net seems to be doing,” Carr wrote, “is chipping away my capacity for concentration and contemplation.” He recounted his increased difficulty reading longer works.  
不要盯着看太长时间。如果每个视频都是表达的火花，那么长时间使用 TikTok 就像是几个小时的烟花在你眼前绽放。这难道对健康没有影响吗？2010 年，科技作家尼古拉斯·卡尔在《浅薄：互联网如何改变我们的大脑》一书中敏锐地提出了这一担忧，该书是普利策奖的入围作品。“网络似乎在做的是削弱我集中注意力和深思的能力，”卡尔写道。他还提到了自己阅读长篇作品时越来越难的情况。  
He wrote of a highly accomplished philosophy student—indeed, a Rhodes Scholar—who didn’t read books at all but gleaned what he could from Google. That student, Carr ominously asserted, “seems more the rule than the exception.”  
他描述了一位学识渊博的哲学研究生——确实是一位罗德学者——此人根本不读书，而是从谷歌那里获取他能获取的所有知识。卡尔回味深长地断言，这位学生“似乎已经成为常态而非例外”。

Carr set off an avalanche. [“Indistractable,”](https://www.amazon.com/Indistractable-Control-Your-Attention-Choose/dp/1526610205/ref&#61;tmm_pap_swatch_0?_encoding&#61;UTF8&amp;dib_tag&#61;se&amp;dib&#61;eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.m7880szKCZkyL9d4nOSb1COwP3reDHlVhX2dFQoCkUAYoGT7U2_pdBzECzzVE5p9Y4Cm0U30sDmayjDjd9SR14BlljeUv-rDtSo0yrRCl6jsRjzPVP7LkPJeJev82XnzMPpexxX3AePXGLAOfHV5cSShjJiY4thPVfIBJHp6KxxrEJyTZiSsUoCtv5WuTCJffkHunE5C94xJgWZM9SjUYEbhShJhheVv8PAcmiJH2UI.pZFKxpEyXNQMWQqjTIbIO6k86jwWCrAQisUWvRyAEVs&amp;qid&#61;1737064142&amp;sr&#61;8-1) Johann Hari’s [“Stolen Focus,”](https://www.amazon.com/Stolen-Focus-Attention-Think-Deeply/dp/0593138538/ref&#61;sr_1_1?crid&#61;2XSEOXRIH9S7Q&amp;dib&#61;eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.8xqz-3wrzuKHOwE7Bp8rCLQIViJSNxUtYgyZagtcHV5Op7i8M-J_zmRQ3i1OTXVnatU3Hdwj2NChxgQCWqM2EIkV2z1h4cWHOQBliLIZcEgiwXD3-1Bj4y5EX4jldwGJC-3GUCgWHUQokuorTdXablrZFpsJT0wmKkyeR8rvvehBGTRR3CWgDLNApM3ADnSyA97D-G_QOKZ9XcxNasJGkKShyz6mKdLvurYLDp6pqeo.ilq_QgyEmIWjOhSa32rOZl0Wy67o24Aljgog8-DAQpY&amp;dib_tag&#61;se&amp;keywords&#61;%E2%80%9CStolen%20Focus,%E2%80%9D&amp;qid&#61;1737064164&amp;sprefix&#61;stolen%20focus,%20,aps,119&amp;sr&#61;8-1) Cal Newport’s [“Deep Work,”](https://www.amazon.com/Deep-Work-Focused-Success-Distracted/dp/0349413681/ref&#61;tmm_pap_swatch_0?_encoding&#61;UTF8&amp;dib_tag&#61;se&amp;dib&#61;eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.PXMpydhpHrw_mkWIg2IShhBdVnGWw1S4vf9V_NF25OV5t0EX2ERjSjhR8r1KB0IEqfxZIaX_btbHUw2GVQqzmICO-2xdv6eTNk7Z_hABJYA8LdgRkRKrnKrSh9w1rbTix3uSBUqWb6fpDRzYxoLhqL_iYb3CJbX1QQHXQ967ZRYeMiK779F_MyEsiWfSYEjbk8vtbT5JDRaBrCZOCgaI7tcFWPveCWHdQhz5A39qRHA.6w-z0aHgCtn6ei4dWSZmq84XKfv1EeF3Sn2jdD8vIA8&amp;qid&#61;1737064183&amp;sr&#61;8-1) and Jenny Odell’s [“How to Do Nothing.”](https://www.amazon.com/How-Do-Nothing-Resisting-Attention/dp/1612198554/ref&#61;sr_1_1?crid&#61;1A83RSESCWS2V&amp;dib&#61;eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.pIvExgq05O75l4p0DQ7xwJmIAyIcB-QK-NZ1xWFvsnEsM7VG-GL_WF9UjN3XZ6bwIMNsDCUGvFVCvapfu6_-tdawK4OYSRMt9jiBRceqQELtHWTDrGQ9mQMcXqvfo8rVM6KVfFEmqdCdxso9wvZi1KnkFv5mGzTUH769YgmC5-YdDGMe1zml9h48tb-wFdHUBOTwBdfh9bwYKVi_OP14R2erX9ZSZ9roLrhaYImYXN4.8S7r4wM9lAgUWwqJkptVqt0aW4TGo_ehJesUd5G3HcU&amp;dib_tag&#61;se&amp;keywords&#61;%E2%80%9CHow%20to%20Do%20Nothing.%E2%80%9D&amp;qid&#61;1737064209&amp;sprefix&#61;how%20to%20do%20nothing.%20,aps,149&amp;sr&#61;8-1) Carr himself has a new book, [“Superbloom,”](https://www.amazon.com/Superbloom-Technologies-Connection-Tear-Apart/dp/1324064617/ref&#61;sr_1_1?crid&#61;LK2Y1OR0O1BC&amp;dib&#61;eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.rJSyPxRy_q-dhCbRpyT4nBaYBEsmY8dCI-ZeejcnF8uLN39hPJD6DRD5QvP3skG7f7e_nDzsNvX7apJ_RpO1xUrTY-TMkXod4wD70SrQmUnmkXhGAbMF4o9FRCVQB8puwEQ9d0Y-fgIFh9aPdExSJL6J-2goPInntLS0LVDLFU1_q63ak703QFI157Y6TUOpqexS_3iqnU_qEjRsPBcOzbRSQT-EBtPSWo1nNp7NB0D-eR7oS0P9dzQc_9h1wlmh5U9kyj39vGQILpYw8bSMY_kqM7ZPseW20b6OXQeTr3JXE02KB02WJTE_rc2O_koukrwjsWSfTGGk3NRZRW5b8jwc3NQGTjODhpoTbhXbIjfX9Xf87_fi1GYWXENgQHt0DEgrkHO29P_e_dIUrj24NyRbK6X3sy4QGvrQPI5AJQFPM9FAdPUJkeVOuOxC05OP.N3LvuGBmCmv-4vlhe3-mvmLFvNRFNVSGAFbFEzLL4ZY&amp;dib_tag&#61;se&amp;keywords&#61;%E2%80%9CSuperbloom,%E2%80%9D&amp;qid&#61;1737064227&amp;sprefix&#61;superbloom,%20,aps,122&amp;sr&#61;8-1) about not only distraction but all the psychological harms of the Internet. We’ve suffered a “fragmentation of consciousness,” Carr writes, our world having been “rendered incomprehensible by information.”  
卡罗引发了一场雪崩。许多关于我们受损注意力的作品包括尼尔·埃亚尔的《不可分心》、约翰·哈里的《被盗的专注》、卡尔·纽波特的《深度工作》以及詹妮·奥德尔的《无所事事》。卡罗自己也有一本新书《超级绽放》，书中不仅讨论了分心，还探讨了互联网带来的所有心理危害。卡罗写道，我们经历了“意识的碎片化”，我们的世界因信息而变得“难以理解”。

Read one of these books and you’re unnerved. But read two more and the skeptical imp within you awakens. Haven’t critics freaked out about the brain-scrambling power of everything from pianofortes to brightly colored posters?  
读其中一本书你会感到不安。但再读两本后，你内心的怀疑就会觉醒。难道批评家们没有对从钢琴到鲜艳海报等各种事物的脑部扰乱能力感到震惊吗？  
Isn’t there, in fact, a long section in Plato’s Phaedrus in which Socrates argues that writing will wreck people’s memories?  
事实上，在柏拉图的《斐德罗》中有一大段，苏格拉底 argue that writing will wreck people’s memories？苏格拉底认为书写会破坏人们的记忆力？

I’m particularly fond of a hand-wringing essay by Nathaniel Hawthorne, from 1843. Hawthorne warns of the arrival of a technology so powerful that those born after it will lose the capacity for mature conversation.  
我特别喜欢纳撒尼尔·霍桑于 1843 年写的一篇焦虑的文章。霍桑警告说，有一种技术如此强大，以至于在它之后出生的人将失去进行成熟对话的能力。  
They will seek separate corners rather than common spaces, he prophesies. Their discussions will devolve into acrid debates, and “all mortal intercourse” will be “chilled with a fatal frost.” Hawthorne’s worry? The replacement of the open fireplace by the iron stove.  
他预言他们会寻求单独的空间而不是共同的区域。他们的讨论将退化为尖酸的辩论，“所有的人际交往”将被“致命的寒冰”所冰冻。霍桑的担忧？开放的火炉被铁炉取代。

It’s true that we’ve raised alarms over things that in retrospect seem mild, the Carr-hort responds, but how much solace should we take in that? Today’s digital forms are obviously more addictive than their predecessors.  
确实，我们曾对事后看来似乎微不足道的事情发出警报，卡尔回应说，但我们因此能获得多少安慰呢？今天的数字形式显然比其前身更具成瘾性。  
You can even read previous grumbling as a measure of how bad things have become. Perhaps critics were correct to see danger in, say, television. If it now appears benign, that just shows how much worse current media is.  
你可以将过去的抱怨视为情况恶化程度的指标。或许批评者正确地看到了电视带来的危险，如果现在看来电视已经变得无害，这反而表明当前的媒体状况更加糟糕。

It’s been fifteen years since Carr’s “The Shallows.” Now we have what is perhaps the most sophisticated contribution to the genre, [“The Sirens’ Call,”](https://www.amazon.com/Sirens-Call-Attention-Endangered-Resource/dp/0593653114/ref&#61;sr_1_1?crid&#61;1VI98S47QJ1NB&amp;dib&#61;eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.auCXzbbFVAbVz389OIr7nlzhx7js_d7-Pikak_P0uaxmmIreOBOvsKygDOo5QMuTBAIsVWqFgejsva7gffL9ST_xzX8fZ8cJ5b5j3-xAXNZtAaUlEQB-KgJKnqo8WvfZV1pNPEXFmzEP3ekIoeU86wDI2gAb4yecgbCSbjgIAeFJdRxkkd4LztWMxXss1u_jOyAXIqapka2Dtb4yTqIn500t_58M7uJSVSg2VPbTTJI.DdXUrE1G9JYpMbTB2mgmkAURyHv6tKjli7V9R1PAQ7M&amp;dib_tag&#61;se&amp;keywords&#61;%E2%80%9CThe%20Sirens%E2%80%99%20Call,%E2%80%9D&amp;qid&#61;1737064279&amp;sprefix&#61;,aps,120&amp;sr&#61;8-1) by Chris Hayes, an MSNBC anchor. Hayes acknowledges the long history of such panics. Some seem laughable in hindsight, he concedes, like one in the nineteen-fifties about comic books. Yet others seem prophetic, like the early warnings about smoking.  
自 Carr 的《浅薄》以来已经过去了十五年。现在我们有了这一类型中最 sophisticated 的贡献，“Sirens’ Call”（ sirens 的呼唤），作者是 MSNBC 主播克里斯·海耶斯。海耶斯承认这种恐慌有着漫长的历史。他承认，有些恐慌在 hindsight 看起来很可笑，比如二十世纪五十年代关于漫画书的恐慌。但还有一些则显得先知先觉，比如对吸烟早期警告的预言。  
“Is the development of a global, ubiquitous, chronically connected social media world more like comic books or cigarettes?” Hayes asks.  
“全球、普及、慢性连接的社交媒体世界的发展更像漫画书还是香烟？”海斯问道。

Great question. If we take the skeptics seriously, how much of the catastrophist’s argument stands? Enough, Hayes feels, that we should be gravely concerned.  
great question. 如果我们认真对待怀疑论者，灾难论者的论点中有多少站得住脚？海耶斯认为，这足以让我们严重关切。  
“We have a country full of megaphones, a crushing wall of sound, the swirling lights of a 24/7 casino blinking at us, all part of a system minutely engineered to take our attention away from us for profit,” he writes.  
“我们有一个充满扩音器的国家，一个压倒性的声音墙，一个 24 小时不停闪烁的赌场灯光在吸引着我们，所有这些都是一个精细设计的系统的一部分，目的是为了盈利而让我们分心，”他写道。  
Thinking clearly and conversing reasonably under these conditions is “like trying to meditate in a strip club.” The case he makes is thoughtful, informed, and disquieting. But is it convincing?  
在这些条件下清晰思考并合理交流“就像在 strip club 里冥想一样。”他所提出的观点是深思熟虑、有根据的，但令人不安。但这够有说服力吗？

History is littered with lamentations about distraction. Swirling lights and strippers are not a new problem. What’s important to note about bygone debates on the subject, though, is that they truly were debates.  
历史充满了对分心的哀叹。闪烁的灯光和舞者并不是新问题。不过，需要注意的是，过去关于这一问题的讨论确实是真正的辩论。  
Not everyone felt the sky was falling, and the dissenters raised pertinent questions. Is it, in fact, good to pay attention? Whose purposes does it serve?  
并非每个人认为世界末日即将来临，持反对意见的人提出了相关的问题：真的有必要关注吗？这又是为了什么目的？

Such questions came up in the eighteenth century with the rise of a disruptive new commodity: the novel. Although today’s critics rue our inability to get through long novels, such books were once widely regarded as the intellectual equivalent of junk food.  
这样的问题在十八世纪随着一种颠覆性的新商品——小说的兴起而出现。尽管今天的批评家懊悔我们无法读完长篇小说，但当时的这类书籍曾被广泛认为是智力上的垃圾食品。  
“They fix attention so deeply, and afford so lively a pleasure, that the mind, once accustomed to them, cannot submit to the painful task of serious study,” the Anglican priest Vicesimus Knox complained.  
“他们如此专注，带来的快乐如此生动，以至于一旦习惯了这些，心灵便无法忍受严肃学习的痛苦任务，”英国圣公会牧师维切斯卢斯·诺克斯抱怨道。  
Thomas Jefferson warned that once readers fell under the spell of novels—“this mass of trash”—they would lose patience for “wholsome reading.” They’d suffer from “bloated imagination, sickly judgement, and disgust toward all the real business of life.”  
托马斯·杰斐逊警告说，一旦读者被小说——“这一堆垃圾”——所吸引，他们就会失去对“有益阅读”的耐心。“他们将遭受想象力膨胀、判断力衰退以及对生活中所有实际事务的厌恶。”

Popular writers took a different view, as the English professor Natalie M. Phillips explains in her book [“Distraction.”](https://www.amazon.com/Distraction-Natalie-M-Phillips-ebook/dp/B01LW8WQ5X/ref&#61;sr_1_1?crid&#61;3HK0QJ6IPDVZ3&amp;dib&#61;eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.N6GAzSMQOhjgZqCNGdr70w.tpjjBHnn1uIUKiryc-XcD7Ic-a9xKudw4Bhx6ClSfcM&amp;dib_tag&#61;se&amp;keywords&#61;%E2%80%9CDistraction%22%20natalie%20m%20phillips&amp;qid&#61;1737064313&amp;sprefix&#61;distraction%20natalie%20m%20phillip,aps,103&amp;sr&#61;8-1) They wondered if unstraying attention was healthy. Maybe the mind required a little leaping around to do its work. “The Rambler” (1750-52) and “The Idler” (1758-60), two essay series by [Samuel Johnson,](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2008/12/08/man-of-fetters) exulted in such mental wandering. Johnson was constantly picking up books and just as constantly putting them down. When a friend asked whether Johnson had actually finished a book he claimed to have “looked into,” he replied, “No, Sir, do _you_ read books _through_?”  
流行作家们持有不同的观点，正如英语教授娜塔莉·M·菲利普斯在她的书《分心》中解释的那样，他们怀疑不偏不倚的注意力是否健康。或许，大脑需要一些跳跃式的思考来完成工作。“闲散者”（1750-52 年）和“散心者”（1758-60 年），萨缪尔·约翰逊的两篇散文系列，颂扬了这种精神上的漫游。约翰逊不断地拿起书，也同样不断地放下。当一位朋友问他是否真的读完了声称读过的“浏览”过的书时，他回答说：“不，先生，你读书读完整本书吗？”

As the mascot of multifocality, Phillips presents Tristram Shandy, the hero of Laurence Sterne’s [“The Life and Opinions of Tristram Shandy, Gentleman,”](https://www.amazon.com/Opinions-Tristram-Gentleman-Penguin-Classics/dp/0141439777/ref&#61;sr_1_1?crid&#61;1WMY1CX7JNF2B&amp;dib&#61;eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.89r3mwO2octY1JzO0AouBbXrUCreq0oOCsPkZh86m0wNReB3sQbYc4uPSacRIj0TudUBaev2tO05bBojih2S-p87wQ3dbRKsaTmCyKjKrxpP8mM_T0SSueFeo6p_JvbSAEfLPYYvWixevJJib_GEomcRccqUGpyf0GwxN6j0ytjJpsZnSjOcB3kd_ndtW3TvGO_uAJln3KDSvyeAeW_KrtPiPxd9QJwX7F7HLKvhOB4.YVVjStqjDxEug58CYyTXcdyXnJ3emIhA5VvPfOJp8gg&amp;dib_tag&#61;se&amp;keywords&#61;%E2%80%9CThe%20Life%20and%20Opinions%20of%20Tristram%20Shandy,%20Gentleman,%E2%80%9D&amp;qid&#61;1737064364&amp;sprefix&#61;,aps,125&amp;sr&#61;8-1) published between 1759 and 1767. The novel starts with Tristram’s conception. His mother’s sudden interjection—“Pray, my dear, have you not forgot to wind up the clock?”—at the moment of his father’s sexual climax leaves Tristram congenitally scatterbrained.  
作为多焦性的吉祥物，菲利普斯呈现了劳伦斯·斯特恩的《项狄传》中的主人公项狄。这部小说发表于 1759 年至 1767 年。小说以项狄的出生为开端。在他母亲突然插话——“亲爱的，请问你忘记上发条了吗？”——的那一刻，正好是他父亲性高潮的时候，项狄因此天生健忘。  
Even his name is the product of broken attention. It was supposed to be Trismegistus, but the maid tasked with telling the curate got distracted and forgot all but the first syllable.  
他的名字本身就是一种注意力缺失的产物。本应是 Trismegistus，但负责告诉神甫的女仆分心了，只记住了第一个音节。  
Tristram relates this tale of woe in a tangle of digressions, punctuated with breathless dashes.  
特里斯坦在冗长的旁枝逸出中讲述这个悲惨的故事，其间夹杂着急促的破折号。

In nine distracted volumes, Tristram never manages to narrate his life. Yet readers found his rollicking thoughts captivating. Perhaps they also found them liberating, Phillips suggests, given the tendency of traditional authorities to demand unwavering focus.  
在九卷散漫的著作中，特里斯坦从未成功叙述过自己的生活。然而，读者们却被他生动的思想所吸引。菲利普斯暗示，也许他们还从中找到了解放感，毕竟传统权威倾向于要求人们保持毫不动摇的专注。  
“What is requisite for joining in prayer in a right manner?” a widely used Anglican catechism asked. “Close attention without wandering.”  
“以正确的方式参与祷告需要什么？”一本常用的安立甘教义问答集问道。“全神贯注而不走神。”

Samuel Johnson’s dictionary noted that “to attend” had multiple meanings. The first, to focus on, was related to the second—to wait on, as a servant. A recent history of attention in the nineteenth-century United States, Caleb Smith’s [“Thoreau’s Axe,”](https://www.amazon.com/Thoreaus-Axe-Distraction-Discipline-American/dp/0691214778/ref&#61;sr_1_1?crid&#61;2VC844PPJ9JPN&amp;dib&#61;eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.0FoQj_L3trl8_Ej-7irnuQ27621YnABcWvneGfjc-cOel5msdC-5q3tHk56RBKhJ.TcGWuKgItAlGLQtdzUPCCaRMOQN9K4X-lggQJPJDob0&amp;dib_tag&#61;se&amp;keywords&#61;%E2%80%9CThoreau%E2%80%99s%20Axe,%E2%80%9D&amp;qid&#61;1737064389&amp;sprefix&#61;thoreau%20s%20axe,%20,aps,117&amp;sr&#61;8-1) draws out this point clearly. Across centuries, thinkers have sought to fend off distraction. But the loudest calls to attention have been directed toward subordinates, schoolchildren, and women. “Atten-_TION_!” military commanders shout at their men to get them to stand straight. The arts of attention are a form of self-discipline, but they’re also ways to discipline others.  
萨缪尔·约翰逊的词典指出，“attend”这个词有多个含义。第一个含义，专注于，与第二个含义——作为仆人等待有关。卡莱布·史密斯的《梭罗的斧头》一书对 19 世纪美国注意力的历史进行了阐述，清晰地说明了这一点。从世纪之交起，思想家们一直在努力抵御分心。但对注意力最响亮的呼唤往往针对的是下属、学童和女性。“注意！”军事指挥官向他们的士兵喊道，让他们站得笔直。注意力的艺术是一种自我约束的方式，但同时也是对他人进行约束的方式。

By the nineteenth century, some had grown wary of the intense forms of concentration that industrial life demanded. The psychiatrist Jean-Étienne Dominique Esquirol introduced a new diagnosis, “monomania,” which was doled out with the faddishness that A.D.H.D. is today.  
到十九世纪，有些人对工业生活所要求的强烈集中注意力形式感到厌倦。精神病学家让-埃蒂安·多米尼克·埃西里奥引入了一个新的诊断，“单狂症”，这种诊断的使用方式与当今多动症障碍（A.D.H.D.）的使用方式一样随波逐流。  
Esquirol felt it to be the characteristic disorder of modernity. Herman Melville made it central to [“Moby-Dick,”](https://www.amazon.com/Moby-Dick-Whale-Penguin-Classics-Deluxe/dp/0143105957/ref&#61;sr_1_1_sspa?crid&#61;2IB052C6R5C70&amp;dib&#61;eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.U1RLU2eAXvlcFCi29yN8dQKenjIRmoOC0tasVXAXYeEI9wqvuccVXRBIJZ79GyxFrXcvA5SxKDyK6u2xdDWdjRTkXa3M3wgWGmgrB6R0CrBTkRnTzXSe9_xVNUlOZpndvpLrf4BMetz6u1eGwKGv6OT_ltzzCrC6-_u8VRaMh5PlLAn9_-8lnzW1Mgtk8nJu6FXYe9-ER1xE35m2eAvJEBirT55YIX8C_wwAwT2liFM.bGkI2q-LO2U_GZUoszIiT1E9aPpi_S_JAZBaLmNtZi0&amp;dib_tag&#61;se&amp;keywords&#61;%E2%80%9CMoby-Dick,%E2%80%9D&amp;qid&#61;1737064408&amp;sprefix&#61;,aps,108&amp;sr&#61;8-1-spons&amp;sp_csd&#61;d2lkZ2V0TmFtZT1zcF9hdGY&amp;psc&#61;1) in which Captain Ahab’s fixation on a white whale brings ruin. Hypnosis, an intense form of focus, became an object of widespread concern.  
Esquirol 认为这是现代性的特征性紊乱。赫尔曼·梅尔维尔在《白鲸记》中将其置于中心位置，书中船长亚哈对白鲸的痴迷带来了灾祸。催眠，一种强烈的集中状态，引起了广泛的关注。

It was Paul Lafargue, [Karl Marx’s](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2016/10/10/karl-marx-yesterday-and-today) Cuban-born son-in-law, who rolled this trepidation about attention into a political program.  
它是卡爾·马克思的出生在古巴的女婿保罗·拉法格，将这种对注意力的担忧转化为了一份政治纲领。  
(His essays have been reissued recently by New York Review Books.) Focussing on one’s work and suppressing one’s natural instincts, Lafargue argued, in the eighteen-eighties, was no virtue. It was, rather, to “play the part of the machine” on behalf of one’s own oppressors.  
(他的散文最近由纽约评论书籍重新发行。) 拉法格在十八世纪八十年代认为，专注于工作并抑制自然本能并不是一种美德。相反，这是“为自己的压迫者扮演机器的角色”。  
Revolutionary consciousness meant asserting “the right to be lazy,” Lafargue insisted. Workers of the world, relax.  
革命的意识意味着主张“拥有懒惰的权利”，拉法格坚持认为。全世界的工人，放松一下。

One daydreams of a Lafarguean resistance, in which the youth are recruited with samizdat copies of “Tristram Shandy.” But would they read it?  
有一天梦想着一种拉法格式的抵抗，在这种抵抗中，年轻人用私下复制的《特里斯坦·席恩德》招募。但他们会读吗？  
I assign my college students about half of what I was assigned as an undergraduate twenty-odd years ago, and many professors have felt the need for similar scaling back.  
我给大学学生布置的作业大约是我二十年前作为本科生时的一半，许多教授也有类似的减少负担的需求。  
“I have been teaching in small liberal arts colleges for over 15 years now, and in the past five years, it’s as though someone flipped a switch,” the theologian Adam Kotsko writes.  
“我现在已经在小型文理学院教学超过 15 年了，而在过去五年里，仿佛有人开关了某个东西，”神学家亚当·科特科写道。  
“Students are intimidated by anything over 10 pages and seem to walk away from readings of as little as 20 pages with no real understanding.”  
学生们对超过 10 页的内容感到 intimidated，并且似乎对只有 20 页左右的阅读材料也没有真正的理解。

Whatever thoughts past writers have had about the virtues of attention, pessimists would argue that the problem is different now. It’s as if we’re not reading books so much as the books are reading us.  
无论过去作家们对注意力的优点有何看法，悲观主义者会认为现在的问题不同了。仿佛我们不是在阅读书籍，而是书籍在阅读我们。  
TikTok is particularly adept at this; you just scroll and the app learns—from your behavior, plus perhaps other information harvested from your phone—about what will keep you hooked.  
TikTok 在这方面的能力特别强；你只需要滚动，应用程序就会根据你的行为以及其他可能从你的手机收集到的信息来了解什么能让你保持兴趣。  
“I wake up in cold sweats every so often thinking, What did we bring to the world?” Tony Fadell, a co-developer of the iPhone, has said.  
托尼·法德尔，iPhone 共同开发者曾说：“我时不时地会在冷汗中醒来思考，我们为这个世界带来了什么？”

As a baseline, Chris Hayes points to [Abraham Lincoln’s](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2020/09/28/why-we-keep-reinventing-abraham-lincoln) debates with Stephen A. Douglas, in the eighteen-fifties: three-hour exchanges of orations about a momentous topic, slavery.  
作为基准，克里斯·海耶斯提到了 1850 年代亚伯拉罕·林肯与斯蒂芬·阿·道格拉斯的辩论：关于一个重大话题——奴隶制的长达三小时的演讲交流。  
He marvels at how complex and layered the speeches were, stuffed with “parenthetical and nested clauses, with ideas that are previewed at the beginning of a sentence, left for a bit, and then returned to later.” He imagines what “sheer stamina of focus” Lincoln and Douglas’s audiences must have possessed.  
他惊叹于这些演讲多么复杂和层次分明，充满了“嵌套从句”，句首预示思想，稍后再回头提及。他想象林肯和道格拉斯的听众必须具备多么惊人的专注力。

Those audiences were large. Would voters flock to something similar today? Not likely, Hayes says. Information now comes in “ever-shorter little bites,” and “focus is harder and harder to sustain.” Hayes has seen this firsthand.  
那些观众人数很多。今天选民会涌向类似的东西吗？海斯说不太可能。现在信息以“越来越短的小块”形式出现，“集中注意力也越来越难”。海斯亲身经历了这一点。  
His illuminating backstage account of cable news describes thoughtful journalists debasing themselves in their scramble to retain straying viewers. Garish graphics, loud voices, quick topic changes, and titillating stories—it’s like jangling keys to lure a dog.  
他的后台描述揭示了有思想的记者为了留住流失的观众，在有线新闻中自贬身价。花哨的图形、响亮的声音、快速的话题转换和耸人听闻的故事——这就像用响亮的钥匙声来引诱一条狗。  
The more viewers get their news from apps, the harder television producers have to shake those keys.  
观众从应用程序获取新闻越多，电视制片人敲键子就越困难。
