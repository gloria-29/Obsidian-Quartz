Road Warrior  公路战士
&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;&#61;

After fifty years, Gloria Steinem is still at the forefront of the feminist cause.

By [Jane Kramer](https://www.newyorker.com/contributors/jane-kramer)

October 12, 2015

![Image 33: “Im an entrepreneur of social change” Steinem says. “I talk. I write. I tell stories. I want to do justice to the women...](https://github.com/gloria-29/OB-assets/releases/download/img-ec/ecad2017651ded044e6f9d0a3d37016832ae1d5ccf913da223608e006bb4a135.jpg)

“I’m an entrepreneur of social change,” Steinem says. “I talk. I write. I tell stories. I want to do justice to the women I meet.”Photograph by Inez and Vinoodh for The New Yorker  
“我是一个社会变革的创业者，”斯坦姆说。“我说话，我写作，我讲故事。我想公正地对待我遇到的女性。”Inez 和 Vinoodh 为《纽约客》摄影

One day in the fall of 1997, Gloria Steinem was unpacking a carry-on bag that in the course of a few weeks had seen the inside of more airplane overhead bins than most travellers’ do in a year, and, as she tells the story, that was when she knew it was time to write a book about her life on the road, rallying women to the fight for equal rights.  
1997 年秋天的一天，格洛丽亚·斯泰纳姆正在整理一个手提包，这个手提包在短短几周内已经比大多数旅客一年内看到的飞机行李舱还要多，正如她讲述的故事，那时她知道是时候写一本书关于她在路上的生活，动员女性为平等权利而战了。  
Steinem was sixty-three then.  
斯坦因当时 63 岁。  
She had been travelling for more than thirty years, speaking, advising, fund-raising, organizing, testifying, demonstrating, educating, campaigning, and, in the process, introducing millions of girls and women to the feminist cause—and during that time she had also founded and presided over the magazine _Ms._, written books, published and edited collections, and, through the Ms. Foundation, which she and three friends of the magazine established, nurtured the talent of generations of younger feminists.  
她旅行了三十多年，演讲、提供建议、筹集资金、组织活动、作证、示威、教育、开展运动，在这个过程中，她还向数百万的女孩和妇女介绍了女权主义事业——在此期间，她还创立并主持了《Ms.》杂志，撰写书籍，出版和编辑文集，并通过她与杂志的三位朋友共同创立的 Ms.基金会，培养了几代年轻女权主义者的才能。  
But she had never stopped travelling, and she wasn’t about to now, with a road book planned. “I’ve spent more time on the road than not,” she told me this past summer.  
但她从未停止过旅行，现在有了路线图，她更不会停下。 “我大部分时间都在路上，”她去年夏天告诉我。  
“It’s been the most important part of my life—and a big antidote to the idea that there is ‘one’ American people.”  
这是我生命中最重要的部分——也是对抗“有一个统一的美国人民”这一观念的强效解毒剂。

Steinem finished the book in February this year, or, as she puts it, “seventeen deadlines late,” and in March she celebrated her eighty-first birthday, with a small dinner cooked by a group of friends. “A relief!” she told me. “My eightieth birthday had gone on for a year.  
斯坦因今年二月完成了这本书，或者，用她的话说，“晚了十七个期限”，三月份她庆祝了她的八十一岁生日，有一群朋友为她准备的小晚餐。“松了一口气！”她告诉我。“我的八十岁生日持续了一年。”  
People were starting to think that the movement began with me and, worse, was going to end with me.” It wasn’t the first time this had happened.  
人们开始认为这场运动是从我开始的，更糟糕的是，它也将以我结束。”这种情况并不是第一次发生了。  
Steinem’s decennials, marked by her enduringly beautiful face, have been a source of fascination (and huge spikes in feminist fund-raising) since she turned forty and a clueless reporter remarked, by way of a compliment, “Oh, you don’t look forty”—to which she replied, “This is what forty looks like.  
斯坦姆的十年，以她永恒美丽的面孔为标志，自从她四十岁时一个无知的记者出于恭维说“哦，你看起来不像四十岁”——她回答说：“这就是四十岁的样子。”——以来，一直是一个令人着迷的源泉（以及女性主义筹款活动的大幅飙升）。  
We’ve been lying for so long, who would know?” Every ten years since then, that face, with its iconic curtain of long, straight hair falling from a center part, begins to appear on magazine covers, television screens, dorm posters, and even T-shirts: this is what fifty looks like, what sixty looks like, this is seventy.  
我们撒谎了这么久，谁会知道？”从那时起，每十年，那张脸，带着标志性的长直发从中间分开垂下，开始出现在杂志封面、电视屏幕、宿舍海报甚至 T 恤上：这就是五十岁的样子，这就是六十岁的样子，这就是七十岁的样子。  
Her eightieth-birthday marathon kicked off with a benefit in Philadelphia, and, while she spent her actual birthday on an elephant in Botswana, she spent the rest of the year “using myself” to promote the cause and the work of other feminists—and to finally finish her book.  
她的八十岁生日马拉松在费城以一场慈善活动拉开帷幕，尽管她在博茨瓦纳的犀牛上度过了真正的生日，但她花了一整年“利用自己”来推广事业和其他女权主义者的工作——并最终完成了她的书。  
“I was out of excuses,” she told me. “Embarrassing!”  
“我已经没有借口了，”她对我说。“太尴尬了！”

Toward the end of May, Steinem, her galleys corrected and delivered, boarded a plane to Beijing, where thirty-one peace activists—among them two Nobel laureates—from thirteen countries were gathering to fly to North Korea on a peace mission.  
五月底，斯特恩在样书校对和交付后，登上了飞往北京的飞机，在那里，来自 13 个国家的 31 位和平活动家——其中包括两位诺贝尔奖获得者——正在聚集，准备飞往朝鲜进行和平使命。  
Their intention, as Steinem explained it, was to cross the DMZ into South Korea, “standing in” for the Korean women on both sides of a longitudinal line they had been forbidden to cross since the war there ended, in 1953.  
他们的意图，正如斯坦因解释的那样，是穿越非军事区进入韩国，“代替”那些自 1953 年战争结束以来被禁止穿越经度线的朝鲜半岛两边的朝鲜女性。  
“We were hoping to walk the entire DMZ, because we were all in white, wearing peace scarves,” she said. “But they put us on a bus instead. While we were still in Beijing, a friend called and asked what I was doing. I said, ‘I’m being a parody of myself.’ True.  
我们本来希望走遍整个非军事区，因为我们都是白色的，戴着和平围巾，”她说。“但他们把我们放在了公交车上。当我们还在北京时，一个朋友打电话问我正在做什么。我说，‘我在自嘲。’真的。  
But I learned an enormous amount—most of all, that those years of isolation and hostility didn’t work. North Korea is the most controlling place I’ve ever been. The day we crossed the DMZ was the longest day of my life. In every way.”  
但我学到了大量的东西——最重要的是，那些年的孤立和敌意并没有奏效。朝鲜是我去过最控制的地方。我们越过非军事区的日子是我一生中最漫长的日子。在各方面。”

Twelve days later, Steinem was in Vermont, giving the commencement address at Bennington College, and on her way home she e-mailed to say that she was ready for conversation.  
十二天后，斯坦姆在佛蒙特州，在本宁顿学院发表毕业典礼演讲，在回家的路上，她通过电子邮件表示她准备好进行对话了。  
“I’ve got a slow few weeks—plenty of time to talk,” she said when I joined her that afternoon, in the garden of the Upper East Side brownstone where she has lived since the late sixties, when she was a rent-controlled tenant, and now owns the first two floors.  
我有一段慢节奏的几周——有很多时间聊天，”当我那天下午加入她时，她说，她自六十年代末以来一直住在上东区的褐色石屋花园里，当时她是受租金控制的租户，现在拥有前两层。  
“Well, slow for me,” she added, given that in a few days she would be flying to Alaska to speak to an audience of three thousand at the University of Alaska’s Fairbanks campus.  
嗯，对我来说有点慢，她补充道，考虑到几天后她将飞往阿拉斯加，在阿拉斯加大学的费尔班克斯校区向三千名听众发表演讲。

She had leaped at the invitation. Alaska was the one state she had never visited, and college campuses, she said, had always been “the single largest slice of my travelling pie”—laboratories of social activism and, in the end, social change. Typically, her schedule was packed.  
她接受了邀请。阿拉斯加是她从未访问过的唯一一个州，她说，大学校园一直是“我旅行蛋糕中最大的一块”——社会活动的实验室，最终，也是社会变革的实验室。通常，她的日程安排得很满。  
Among other things, she would be paying a visit to the city’s “victim-services shelter,” the Interior Alaska Center for Non-Violent Living, where more than half the residents were Native Alaskan women from isolated settlements, many with their children.  
她还将访问该市的“受害者服务庇护所”，即阿拉斯加内陆非暴力生活中心，那里超过一半的居民是来自偏远定居点的原住民阿拉斯加妇女，许多人带着他们的孩子。  
She would also be lunching with ten well-heeled white women, who had bid to join her for an “intimate” Fabulous Feminist Fundraiser.  
她还将与十位富有的白人女性共进午餐，她们竞相加入她的一个“亲密”的杰出女性筹款活动。  
And she would be arguing the case for choice at a big Fairbanks co-operative that was under attack from a conservative borough assemblyman for condoning murder by stocking an issue of _Ms._ with an article about abortion.  
她将在一家受到保守区议员攻击的大费尔班克斯合作社为选择权辩护，该议员因在《Ms.》杂志上刊登关于堕胎的文章而纵容谋杀。

“The best part was the shelter, listening to those amazing women,” Steinem told me when she got home. “For them—women in trouble, in need, terrified, beaten, suicidal women, women with kids at risk—the question is where do you go when you’ve got no _way_ to go, no roads, no cars, no way out. The violence rate in Alaska is high, and at first the shelter was secret. Now there’s always a state trooper on guard.”  
最好的部分是庇护所，听着那些令人惊叹的女性，”当她回到家时，斯坦姆告诉我。“对于她们——陷入困境、需要帮助、感到恐惧、遭受打击、有自杀倾向的女性，有处于危险中的孩子的女性——问题是当你无路可走、没有道路、没有汽车、没有出路时，你该去哪里。阿拉斯加的暴力率很高，起初庇护所是保密的。现在总有一名州警察在守卫。”

I asked Steinem what she thought she could do for those women, and she said, “I’m a media worker, in the parlance of the nineteen-thirties. It’s what I do. I’m an entrepreneur of social change. I raise money. I talk. I write. I tell stories.  
我问斯坦因她认为她能为那些女性做些什么，她说：“我是一个媒体工作者，用二十世纪三十年代的说法。这就是我做的事。我是一个社会变革的企业家。我筹集资金。我说话。我写作。我讲故事。”  
I want to do justice to the women I meet, to tell their stories. There was one young woman who asked me, ‘How do you stand up for yourself when you have no right to stand up for yourself?’ No one had ever told her that she had that right.  
我想公正地对待我遇到的女性，讲述她们的故事。有一位年轻女性问我：“当你没有权利为自己站出来时，你如何为自己辩护？”没有人曾告诉她她有这个权利。  
The first time she tried, her husband had said no, she had no rights. I want to reach women like her.”  
她第一次尝试时，她的丈夫拒绝了，她没有权利。我想接触像她这样的女性。”

没有什么比在错误的队伍中等待更糟糕的了。

Steinem is not a theorist, or even much interested in theory. “Feminist theory came from feminist activism—it wasn’t the other way around,” she said.  
斯坦因不是理论家，甚至对理论并不感兴趣。“女权主义理论源于女权主义运动——并非反过来，”她说。  
“I accept that important theorists like Judith Butler may arrive at enlightening conclusions, but theory can be exclusionary, and that’s not my path. My path is to open the door to this house, to get out of the world I know, and to experience new worlds, new voices.  
我接受像朱迪思·巴特勒这样的重要理论家可能会得出启迪性的结论，但理论可能具有排他性，这不是我的道路。我的道路是打开这所房子的门，走出我所知的世界，去体验新的世界，新的声音。  
It’s making connections, and using myself to listen, because you can’t empower women without listening to _their_ stories. It’s why I was going to call my book ‘America—As If Everyone Matters.’ I liked that title—the irony in it—but I changed my mind after three black women founded the group Black Lives Matter.  
它正在建立联系，并利用我来倾听，因为如果不听她们的故事，就无法赋权女性。这就是我本来打算把我的书命名为《美国——如果每个人都重要》的原因。我喜欢那个标题——其中的讽刺意味——但在三位黑人女性创立了“黑人的命也是命”组织后，我改变了主意。  
I didn’t want to detract from that powerful name or to suggest in any way that my ‘irony’ was a comment on it. Somebody at the publisher’s suggested ‘Memoir.’ I thought, That’s such an élite word. Wrong!  
我不想削弱那个强有力的名字，也不想以任何方式暗示我的“讽刺”是对它的评论。出版社的某人建议用“回忆录”。我想，那是一个如此精英的词。错了！  
Finally, we settled on ‘My Life on the Road.’ ” (Steinem, who doesn’t drive, says, “Well, not quite ‘on,’ more like ‘above.’ ”) It comes out this month.  
最后，我们决定使用《我的公路生活》。”（斯泰因姆，她不开车，说：“嗯，不是完全的‘在’，更像是‘在……之上’。”）这本书将在这个月出版。

**Video:** Gloria Steinem on why she doesn’t drive.

Steinem has a mantra that she says she lives by. She calls it “Ask the Turtle,” because it involves a turtle she rescued—or thought she had—on a geology-class field trip to the Connecticut River Valley in the spring of her freshman year at Smith.  
斯坦因有一句她自称是生活信条的箴言。她称之为“问乌龟”，因为它涉及她在史密斯大学大一春季地质课野外考察中救出——或者她以为救出——的一只乌龟。  
“I found a mud turtle on the riverbank, up by the asphalt road,” she told me. “A big snapping turtle, more than a foot long, but I picked it up—carefully—and lugged it down to the river and slipped it in. The professor saw me just as the turtle disappeared in the water.  
我在河岸上发现了一只泥龟，就在沥青路上，”她告诉我。“一只大钳子龟，超过一英尺长，但我小心地把它捡起来——然后把它拖到河边，放进了水里。就在龟消失在水中的时候，教授看到了我。”  
He said that the turtle had been making its way to dry land for a reason—in order to lay its eggs—and that now it was going to take that turtle months more to lay them.  
他说，海龟之所以要爬向陆地，是有原因的——为了产卵——而现在，这只海龟还需要几个月的时间来产卵。  
It was a lesson I learned to apply to people a few years later, in India—though I didn’t realize it then—when I was going from village to village with Gandhian women organizers, listening to them ask, ‘Tell us your stories. You’ve lived them, you’re the experts.’ ”  
这是一堂我几年后在印度应用于人们的课程——尽管我当时并没有意识到——当我与甘地主义女性组织者一起从一个村庄走到另一个村庄时，我听着她们说：“告诉我们你的故事。你已经经历过，你是专家。”

Steinem comes from Toledo, Ohio, which, as she said one night, standing in her kitchen scrutinizing take-out menus, explains why she won’t eat sushi or rare meat, “like New Yorkers do.” She doesn’t blame Toledo, although her loyalty was seriously tested when the city presented her with a “Son of Ohio” plaque.  
斯坦因来自俄亥俄州的托莱多，正如她在一个晚上站在厨房里仔细查看外卖菜单时所说，这解释了她为什么不会像纽约人那样吃寿司或生肉，“她不怪托莱多，尽管当这座城市向她颁发‘俄亥俄之子’奖牌时，她的忠诚受到了严峻考验。”  
But the road itself was something her father, Leo Steinem, taught her.  
但这条路本身是她父亲，利奥·斯坦姆，教给她的。  
Leo was the black-sheep son of a German-Jewish Toledo family—his father was a businessman and his mother a prominent local suffragist, the first woman to be elected to the Toledo Board of Education—which is to say that he was not enchanted by his parents’ comfortably progressive bourgeois Toledo life.  
利奥是德国犹太裔托莱多家庭的“问题儿子”——他的父亲是一位商人，母亲是一位杰出的当地女权主义者，也是第一位被选入托莱多教育局委员会的女性——也就是说，他对父母那舒适进步的资产阶级托莱多生活并不着迷。

“He was the real gypsy, not me,” Steinem said. “He was kind, loving, funny, but never in one place for long, which meant that we weren’t, either.” The longest times were summers in southern Michigan, where he had bought farmland.  
他是真正的吉普赛人，不是我，”斯坦姆说。“他善良、爱护、幽默，但从不长时间待在一个地方，这意味着我们也不是。”最长的时间是在密歇根南部的夏天，他在那里买了农田。  
He built a small resort on it, with a lakeside dance pavilion, and booked the bands that were playing the summer circuit. But as soon as the season ended he would pack his wife, Ruth, and their two girls (Steinem’s sister was nine years older) into a trailer.  
他在那里建了一个小度假村，有一个湖边舞厅，并预订了在夏季巡回演出中的乐队。但一旦季节结束，他就会把他的妻子露丝和他们的两个女儿（斯坦因的姐姐比她大九岁）打包进拖车里。  
For the rest of the year, the family moved slowly south, with Leo stopping in every town they passed, buying and selling and trading antiques, a peripatetic dealer.  
这一年剩下的时间里，这个家庭缓缓地向南移动，利奥在每个经过的城镇都停下来，买卖和交易古董，成了一个游走四方的古董商。  
Steinem’s first full year of school anywhere was “maybe the seventh grade,” but by then she was reading a book a day—“Librarians saved my life,” she says—and had witnessed her parents’ mournful separation.  
斯坦因姆在任何地方上学的第一年可能是“七年级”，但那时她已经每天读一本书——“图书管理员救了我的命，”她说——并目睹了父母悲伤的分离。

It had been years in coming. When Steinem was born, her mother—the daughter of working-class Protestant parents and, when she met Leo, a successful young journalist—had already suffered one severe breakdown and spent nearly two years in a sanitarium.  
它已经酝酿多年。当斯坦姆出生时，她的母亲——一个工人阶级的福音派父母的女儿，在遇见利奥时，是一位成功的年轻记者——已经经历了一次严重的崩溃，并在疗养院里度过了近两年。  
By the time they divorced, Ruth was addicted to sodium pentothal—“the same drug they fed to Virginia Woolf and Sylvia Plath,” Steinem says—and Leo was living out of a car in California, visiting once a year.  
在他们离婚时，露丝已经对戊巴比妥钠上瘾——“和给弗吉尼亚·伍尔夫和西尔维亚·普拉斯喂的同一药物，”斯坦姆说——而利奥则在加利福尼亚靠一辆车生活，每年只回来一次。

Steinem, who lived alone with her mother from the age of ten until she left for college, says that, as a girl, she used to think that her mother was crazy.  
斯坦因，从十岁起与母亲独自生活直到她上大学，她说，作为一个女孩，她曾经认为她的母亲疯了。  
“It was only much later, when I began to understand how unjust the position of women was in this country, that I knew my mother had never been ‘ill,’ as the doctors claimed. It was that her spirit had been broken.  
后来我才逐渐明白，在这个国家，女性的地位是多么的不公正，这时我才意识到，我的母亲并非如医生所说的那样‘生病’，而是她的精神已经崩溃。  
Until then, I had always worried that I might have inherited something—that I’d start disappearing into the street in my nightgown, the way she had.”  
直到那时，我总是担心自己可能继承了什么——我会像她一样，穿着睡衣消失在街上。”

It isn’t surprising that Steinem had what she calls “a Hollywood vision of school.” She dreamed of becoming a dancer and maybe, eventually, a Rockette. “I imagined myself, with some impracticality, as dancing my way out of Toledo,” she told me.  
斯坦因拥有她所说的“好莱坞式的学校愿景”并不令人惊讶。她梦想成为一名舞者，也许最终成为一名火箭女郎。“我幻想着自己，带着一些不切实际的想法，通过跳舞离开托莱多，”她告诉我。  
By the time she started high school, she had mastered tap dancing while twirling a baton, as well as dancing down a flight of stairs, like a Busby Berkeley showgirl, playing “Anchors Aweigh” on ankle bells.  
到她上高中时，她已经掌握了在转着指挥棒的同时跳踢踏舞，还能像 Busby Berkeley 的女演员一样，在楼梯上跳舞，脚踝上挂着铃铛演奏《Anchors Aweigh》。  
And though she balked at “sinking so low as to put taps on toe shoes”—in late-forties Toledo, the ultimate female talent test—dancing remains on her list of life’s fulfilling pleasures (along with organizing and great sex).  
尽管她反对“堕落到给舞鞋装上踏脚”——在四十年代末的托莱多，这是终极的女性才能测试——但跳舞仍然是她生活中满足感清单上的一项（还有组织和美好的性爱）。  
She has been spotted dancing in elevators and office corridors, and even, for a few months in the nineteen-nineties, at the old Roseland Ballroom, taking tango lessons. “It’s a sick, authoritarian dance, but I loved it,” she says.  
她曾在电梯和办公室走廊里被看到跳舞，甚至在 20 世纪 90 年代的一段时间里，在老罗斯兰舞厅学习探戈。“这是一种病态的、权威的舞蹈，但我喜欢它，”她说。

I once asked Steinem what the real-estate developer and publisher Mort Zuckerman—a man I had seen, years earlier, signalling her to light his cigar—was doing on her otherwise egalitarian list of former lovers, which includes, most enduringly, Franklin Thomas, who for seventeen years was the head of the Ford Foundation (“the longtime love of my life, and best friend”), along with the great alto saxophonist and composer of “Take Five,” Paul Desmond (“a close friend and a short romance”), the director Mike Nichols (“more like three or four years of a smart date”), the Ford Administration’s Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights, Stan Pottinger (“We’d been together long enough, so we stopped the affair”), and the Olympic decathlete and actor Rafer Johnson, who helped tackle Sirhan Sirhan, moments after he shot Bobby Kennedy (“I have lasting respect for him”).  
我曾问斯坦姆，那位房地产开发商和出版商莫特·祖克曼——多年前我曾见过他向她示意点燃雪茄——在他那本否则平等的前情人名单上做了什么，这个名单上包括，最持久的是富兰克林·托马斯，他在福特基金会担任了十七年的负责人（“我一生中的长期爱人，也是最好的朋友”），还有伟大的低音萨克斯风演奏家和作曲家“Take Five”的保罗·德塞蒙德（“一个亲密的朋友和短暂的恋情”），导演迈克·尼科尔斯（“更像是三四年聪明的约会”），福特政府民权助理司法部长斯坦·波特宁格（“我们在一起的时间足够长了，所以我们停止了婚外情”），以及奥运十项全能运动员和演员拉费尔·约翰逊，他在枪击罗伯特·肯尼迪后立即帮助制服了西尔汉（“我对他的持久尊重”）。  
She replied, “Well, Mort’s moved to the right now, but he was funny, and he _loved_ to dance.”  
她回答说：“嗯，莫特现在往右边移动了，但他很风趣，而且他喜欢跳舞。”

“My father gave new meaning to the term ‘financially irresponsible,’ and after years of it my mom crashed,” Steinem told the actress and director Christine Lahti one night this summer.  
我的父亲给“不负责任”这个词赋予了新的含义，而经过多年的这种情况，我妈妈崩溃了，”斯坦姆在去年夏天的一个晚上告诉女演员和导演克里斯汀·拉希蒂。  
Lahti was debating putting together a theatre piece based on stories about the women in her own family, and the two friends went out to dinner and traded reminiscences about “the unlived lives of our moms.” Once, when Steinem was visiting, her mother told her, “Your sister just got a fur coat and didn’t have to pay for it”—meaning that her sister was married and Steinem, who was not, had to buy her own coats.  
拉赫蒂在考虑创作一部以她家族中女性故事为基础的戏剧作品，两位朋友外出吃饭，回忆起“我们妈妈们未度过的生活”。有一次，当斯坦姆来访时，她的母亲告诉她：“你姐姐刚刚买了一件皮大衣，不用付钱”——这意味着她的姐姐已经结婚，而斯坦姆没有，不得不自己买大衣。  
“My mother wasn’t criticizing me—she was _advising_ me,” Steinem told Lahti, laughing.  
我的母亲不是在批评我——她是在给我建议，”斯坦姆对拉希说，笑着。

The women talked for a long time about the kind of liberation that comes from laughing—Steinem having watched six female comics perform at the Gotham Comedy Club the night before.  
女性们长时间地谈论了来自笑声的解放——斯泰因在前一天晚上观看了在哥谭喜剧俱乐部表演的六位女性喜剧演员。  
The show was called Sisters of Comedy, and the impresario was the young feminist producer and writer Agunda Okeyo, who, at the time, was staying in Steinem’s guest room. “The power of laughter—that’s power!” she said.  
喜剧姐妹，制作人兼编剧是年轻的女性主义者奥肯约，当时她住在斯坦因的客房。“笑声的力量——那就是力量！”她说。  
“It’s our only free emotion, the one that nobody can control.”  
这是我们的唯一自由情感，是无人能控制的那一种。

“One of the hard things for me, starting out as an actress, was to laugh,” Lahti said.  
对我来说，作为一个演员开始，最难的事情之一就是笑，”拉蒂说。  
“To find a visual memory or image that let me laugh.” Today, it might be the look on her father’s face—he was a surgeon, partial to the ubiquitous male adage of his generation “Why buy the whole cow if you can get the milk free?”—if, as a teen-age girl, she had come back with the feminist retort “Why buy the whole pig just to get one little sausage?”  
要找到一个能让我笑的视觉记忆或图像。今天，可能是她父亲脸上的表情——他是一位外科医生，喜欢他那一代男性普遍的谚语：“为什么买一头整牛，如果你可以免费得到牛奶？”——如果作为一个少女，她回敬了一句女权主义的反驳：“为什么为了得到一小段香肠就要买一头整猪？”

Lahti married in her thirties and has three children.  
拉赫蒂三十多岁时结婚，有三个孩子。  
Steinem married for the first time in her mid-sixties, inherited three stepchildren (among them the actor Christian Bale), and was widowed three years later, when her husband, David Bale, died of brain cancer, at the age of sixty-two.  
斯坦姆在六十多岁时第一次结婚，继承了三个继子女（其中包括演员克里斯蒂安·贝尔），三年后，她的丈夫大卫·贝尔因脑癌去世，享年六十二岁。  
Bale was a South African-born British businessman and environmentalist. They met when he walked up to her at a Los Angeles Voters for Choice benefit. It was a happy marriage, “a green-card marriage, because we would have been together anyway,” Steinem told me.  
巴莱是一位南非出生的英国商人和环保主义者。他们是在一次洛杉矶“选民选择”慈善晚会上相遇的。这是一段幸福的婚姻，“一张绿卡婚姻，因为我们无论如何都会在一起，”斯坦姆告诉我。  
She says that caring for him that last year, when he was ill and “needed someone to help him out of life, and I needed someone to force me to live in the present,” had actually helped her “expiate the pain of my old terrors”—the terrors of caring for her mother when she was too young to understand or cope.  
她说，去年在他生病时照顾他，“需要有人帮助他走出生活，而我需要有人强迫我活在当下”，实际上帮助她“赎回我过去的恐惧之痛”——在她太小无法理解或应对时照顾母亲的恐惧。

Steinem has compared marriage to slavery law in this country. As a young woman, she fled one brief, ill-advised engagement. And, in her early forties, she amiably dissolved a second, to Robert Benton, who went on to write and direct “Kramer vs.  
斯坦因将婚姻比作这个国家的奴隶法。作为一名年轻女性，她逃离了一段短暂、不明智的订婚。而在她四十年代初，她友好地解除了与罗伯特·伯顿的第二段关系，罗伯特·伯顿后来撰写并导演了《克莱默诉克莱默》。  
Kramer.” “Neither of us was really sure we wanted to marry, so we took it in steps. The first was to do the blood tests and get the license. We did. The second was for him to buy the new suit. He did. The third was for me to buy the dress.  
克莱默。”我们俩其实都不确定是否真的想要结婚，所以我们一步步来。第一步是做血检和领取执照。我们做到了。第二步是他买新西装。他做到了。第三步是我买婚纱。  
I never got to the dress, I just couldn’t do it, and the marriage license expired.”  
我从没穿上婚纱，我就是做不到，而且结婚证也过期了。”

Four years after David Bale died, a reporter from Pakistan asked Steinem why she had changed her mind about marriage. “I didn’t change,” she told him. “Marriage changed. We spent thirty years in the United States changing the marriage laws.  
四年后，大卫·贝勒去世，一位巴基斯坦记者问斯坦因为什么她改变了对婚姻的看法。“我没有改变，”她告诉他。“婚姻改变了。我们在美国花了三十年改变婚姻法律。”  
If I had married when I was supposed to get married, I would have lost my name, my legal residence, my credit rating, many of my civil rights. That’s not true anymore. It’s possible to make an equal marriage.”  
如果我按计划结婚，我会失去我的名字、我的合法居所、我的信用评分、许多我的公民权利。这不再是事实。现在可以拥有平等的婚姻。

Steinem’s archives are at Smith.  
斯坦因档案在史密斯处。  
She is devoted to the college, which was founded, in the eighteen-seventies, with an endowment from Sophia Smith, a Massachusetts woman with a family fortune at her disposal and a strong desire to provide young women with what she described in her will as the “means and facilities” for higher education equal to any available to young men.  
她致力于这所大学，该大学成立于 19 世纪 70 年代，由马萨诸塞州的一位名叫索菲亚·史密斯的女人捐赠基金建立，她拥有家族财富，并强烈希望为年轻女性提供她在其遗嘱中所描述的“手段和设施”，以获得与年轻男性同等的高等教育。  
In Steinem’s senior year, Chester Bowles, who would shortly be named Ambassador to India, came to speak.  
在斯坦姆的毕业班上，即将被任命为印度大使的切斯特·鲍尔斯前来演讲。  
He was so taken by the students’ response that he donated his lecture fee of two thousand dollars—serious money, in those days, when a year’s room, board, and tuition at Smith cost about half that—to help finance a year in India for two seniors after graduation.  
他如此被学生的反应所打动，以至于他捐赠了他的两千美元讲座费——在那个年代，这可是笔大钱，因为当时在史密斯学院一年的住宿、餐饮和学费大约是这笔钱的一半——以帮助资助两名毕业生在毕业后去印度的一年。  
Steinem got one of the fellowships.  
斯坦姆获得了一项奖学金。

“The first organizing I did was in India, though I didn’t realize it then, only that I wanted to stay,” she told me.  
“我第一次组织活动是在印度，尽管当时我没有意识到，只是想留下来，”她告诉我。  
She stayed two years, having talked the press office at Pan Am into free tickets with the promise of “writing something that would make people want to fly to India. I had figured out how to do that.  
她待了两年，通过向泛美航空的公关部门承诺“写些能让人想飞往印度的东西”，说服他们免费提供机票。我已经想出了如何做到这一点。  
My writing was what I’d depended on to get through exams. I’d make up a great quote and attribute it to an important thinker. The professor would be impressed.”  
我的写作是我依赖通过考试的东西。我会编造一个伟大的引言，并把它归功于一个重要的思想家。教授会印象深刻。”

Steinem’s oldest friend from India, the feminist organizer and economist Devaki Jain, flew to New York this summer to spend a couple of weeks in Steinem’s guest room, going over the manuscript of an autobiography that Steinem had been urging her to write.  
斯坦因在印度的最老朋友，女权主义组织者和经济学家德瓦基·贾因，今年夏天飞往纽约，在斯坦因的客房里度过几周，审阅斯坦因一直敦促她撰写的自传手稿。  
They had met in Delhi at the beginning of 1957, when Jain, just home on a break from Oxford, was doing research for a Gandhian co-operative union. “We became friends so fast, and with such ease, and we had a wonderful time,” Jain told me. “But Gloria was already inspired.  
他们在 1957 年初在德里相遇，当时贾因刚从牛津假期回家，正在为一个甘地主义合作社联盟进行研究。“我们很快就成了朋友，相处得非常融洽，我们度过了美好的时光，”贾因告诉我。“但格洛丽亚已经受到了启发。”  
Like Gandhi, she walked the road. She had the humility to do that. It was what she wanted.”  
像甘地一样，她走在路上。她有那份谦卑去做那样的事。这就是她想要的。”

Jain, who was one of the first women in India to use the word “feminist,” lost touch with Steinem after she went home. In 1974, they reconnected in New York, and a few years later Jain arranged for Steinem to return to India.  
贾因，印度最早使用“女权主义者”一词的女性之一，在斯泰因回家后失去了联系。1974 年，他们在纽约重新联系，几年后贾因安排斯泰因重返印度。  
It was on that trip, Steinem told me—“and only after the great conflagration of feminist consciousness in America”—that she understood how much those first two years had shaped her.  
在那次旅行中，斯坦姆告诉我——“而且只有在美国女性主义意识的大觉醒之后”——她才意识到前两年对她影响有多大。

For one thing, she had quickly learned to eschew the car-with-driver distance preserved by many Westerners in India then, and, instead, to embrace the “dormitory on wheels” of rickety buses and the third-class women-only carriages of local trains—“leaving behind all my possessions except a cup, a comb, and the sari I had on” and discovering groups of women of all ages, sharing food and stories with other women they had just met.  
首先，她很快学会了摒弃当时许多印度西方人保留的“有司机驾驶的汽车”距离，相反，她选择了摇摇欲坠的公共汽车上的“车轮上的宿舍”和当地火车上的三等女性车厢——“除了一个杯子、一把梳子和身上穿的莎丽，我把所有东西都留在了身后”并发现了各个年龄段的女性的群体，她们与刚刚认识的其他女性分享食物和故事。  
She had trekked to villages cordoned off by the government because of caste riots, and watched, at night, as the villagers emerged from mud huts to sit in circles, lit by kerosene lamps, and tell their stories of burnings, murders, thefts, and rapes, “with fear and trauma that needed no translation” but with the relief that came from talking and being heard.  
她曾徒步前往政府因种姓冲突而封锁的村庄，夜晚时分，看到村民们从泥屋中走出，围坐在一起，点着煤油灯，讲述他们遭受的纵火、谋杀、盗窃和强奸的故事，“带着无需翻译的恐惧和创伤”，但同时也感受到了通过交谈和被倾听带来的慰藉。  
In her road book, she calls it “the first time I witnessed the ancient and modern magic of talking circles, those groups in which anyone may speak in turn, everyone must listen, and consensus is more important than time.”  
在她的旅行手册中，她称之为“我第一次见证古代与现代的谈话圈魔法，这些群体中任何人都可以依次发言，每个人都必须倾听，共识比时间更重要。”

“Boy, when you need someone to say goodbye to his family forever so that you can fake your own death and assume his identity to escape from Chechen mobsters—that’s when you find out who your real friends are!”  
男孩，当你需要有人永远告别家人，这样你才能假装自己死亡并假扮他的身份逃离车臣匪徒——那时你才会知道谁才是你的真朋友！

When Steinem returned from India, she started making the rounds of magazines and newspapers in New York, looking for a reporting job. It was not an experience that she would recommend.  
斯坦因从印度回来后，她开始在纽约的杂志和报纸上寻找报道工作。这不是她会推荐的经历。  
In those days, the news side of the business was a men’s club, and its prerogatives were rarely challenged. “We need a reporter, not a pretty girl” is how she remembers being shown the door by an editor at _Life_. She made her way freelancing. Her work from those days was direct, accessible, and often very funny, and in 1963 an editor at the magazine _Show_ turned her loose on the Playboy Club.  
那些日子里，新闻部门是个男人的俱乐部，其特权很少受到挑战。“我们需要一名记者，而不是一个漂亮女孩”，她记得自己被《生活》杂志的编辑拒之门外。她开始做自由职业者。她那时的作品直接、易接近，常常非常有趣，1963 年，《展示》杂志的编辑让她在花花公子俱乐部自由发挥。  
She went undercover as “Marie Catherine Ochs”—her grandmother’s name—applied to become a Playboy Bunny, got the job, and began her investigation into a world of puerile male fantasy, keeping a journal in which she recorded in deadpan detail her eleven days of Bunny life, from the physical exam and the pushup, waist-crunching costume to the sleazy, oblivious men who would tweak her cottontail whenever she passed their table with a tray of drinks.  
她化名为“玛丽·凯瑟琳·奥克斯”——她祖母的名字——申请成为花花公子兔女郎，得到了这份工作，并开始调查一个幼稚的男性幻想世界，她在日记中用平淡的笔触记录了她作为兔女郎的 11 天生活，从体检和俯卧撑、腰部挤压的服装到那些粗俗、无知的男人，每当她端着饮料托盘经过他们的桌子时，他们就会摆弄她的兔毛。  
The piece she produced made her famous.  
她创作的作品使她成名。

“The rest of the Playboy story has two parts,” she told me. “First came the ‘looks’ attacks. I was accused of being noticed because of my ‘beauty.’ Well, let me tell you something from the bottom of my soul.  
“《花花公子》故事的其余部分分为两部分，”她告诉我。“首先是对我外貌的攻击。我被指责因为我的‘美貌’而受到关注。好吧，让我从心底告诉你一些事情。”  
When I was young, in high school, in college, I was considered a pretty girl. But beautiful?  
当我年轻的时候，在高中，在大学，我被认为是挺漂亮的女孩。但美吗？  
I wasn’t considered beautiful until the women’s movement.” She laughed and said, “The threshold was lower then!” The second part was that most of the attacks came from men—furious that an undercover Bunny had invaded a male playground, and that people were reading what she wrote and, worse, laughing at them.  
我不被认为漂亮，直到妇女运动。”她笑了，说，“那时候门槛更低！”第二点是，大多数攻击来自男人——他们愤怒地发现一个潜伏的兔女郎入侵了男性游乐场，而且人们正在阅读她所写的内容，更糟糕的是，还在嘲笑他们。  
(“Men fear ridicule the way women fear violence,” she says.) If those men thought of feminism at all, it was as a historic blip that began and ended with the “suffragettes”—who in fact campaigned and crusaded for eighty-some years before American women won voting rights, in 1920.  
“男人害怕嘲笑，就像女人害怕暴力，”她说。如果那些男人对女权主义有所思考，那也只是作为一个始于“女权运动者”并结束于 1920 年美国女性赢得选举权的历史上的小插曲——实际上，她们在那时已经为争取选举权进行了大约八十年的运动和斗争。

The struggle for women’s suffrage is known today in the movement as “first wave” feminism, though it’s unlikely that the kind of man who, nearly fifty years ago, paid for the privilege of tweaking cottontails at a Playboy Club had ever heard the term, let alone knew that a second wave was on the way.  
妇女参政权斗争今天在运动中被称为“第一波”女权主义，尽管几乎五十年前，为在花花公子俱乐部摆弄兔女郎而付费的那种男人可能从未听说过这个术语，更不用说知道第二波即将到来。  
Not even Steinem knew that at the time.  
甚至斯坦姆当时也不知道。  
What she knew was that there was something wrong in those waning days of America’s postwar suburban idyll, in which happily entitled men rushed home from work on their commuter trains to wives who greeted them at the door, holding out a perfectly calibrated Martini.  
她知道的是，在战后美国郊区田园诗般的日子即将结束的那几天里，那些快乐地享有特权的男人从通勤火车上匆匆赶回家，门前的妻子们迎接他们，递上一杯完美调制的马提尼酒，其中似乎有些不对劲。  
In those days, feminism was best known as the refuge and revenge of ugly women—women who couldn’t find a man to marry them.  
那些日子里，女权主义最出名的是丑女的避难所和复仇之地——那些找不到男人结婚的女人。

I asked Steinem how she had dealt with the attacks, and she allowed that, at first, it wasn’t easy. “Other women in the movement helped me enormously, but there was one old woman in particular. I was giving a talk and the ‘looks’ thing came up.  
我问斯坦因如何应对攻击，她表示起初并不容易。“运动中的其他女性给了我巨大的帮助，但有一位特别的老妇人。我正在做一场演讲，提到了‘外观’这个问题。”  
Before I could answer, she stood up and said, ‘It’s important for someone who could play the game, and win, to say, The game isn’t worth shit!’ I was so grateful to her for understanding that I could use who _I_ was to say who _we_ were and what we represent.”  
在我回答之前，她站起来说：“对于能玩游戏并赢得比赛的人来说，说‘这游戏一钱不值！’很重要。”我很感激她理解，我可以利用自己的身份来说明我们是谁，我们代表什么。”

By then, of course, the feminist movement was undeniably resurgent. The National Organization for Women dates from 1966. La Raza Unida, one of the first political parties with a feminist agenda, was founded by Mexican-Americans in 1970.  
那时，当然，女权运动无疑正在复兴。全国妇女组织成立于 1966 年。拉西亚统一党，是第一个具有女权主义议程的政治党之一，由墨西哥裔美国人于 1970 年创立。  
Black women’s groups had long been active, particularly in the South, fighting the double discrimination of race and gender; women artists’ and women writers’ collectives were springing up in California, demanding a place in museums and on publishers’ lists; and, in the big cities of the East, students and young working women were meeting in dorms and walkups for consciousness-raising sessions, talking about their lives and trying to figure out how to change them.  
黑人女性团体一直很活跃，尤其是在南方，她们在种族和性别双重歧视的斗争中；加利福尼亚州涌现出女性艺术家和女性作家团体，要求在博物馆和出版商的名单上占有一席之地；而在东部的各大城市，学生和年轻职业女性在宿舍和公寓里举行意识提升会议，谈论她们的生活，试图弄清楚如何改变它们。  
Steinem was savvy enough to understand that the thorny gift of brains and beauty had made her attractive to the mass media and an unexpectedly useful servant of the cause.  
斯坦姆足够聪明，明白大脑和美貌这个棘手的礼物使她成为大众媒体的宠儿，并意外地成为事业的有用仆人。

In England, en route to India at the beginning of 1957, Steinem discovered that she was pregnant by her erstwhile fiancé.  
在英国，1957 年初前往印度的途中，斯坦姆发现她前未婚夫使她怀孕了。  
She ended the pregnancy when a doctor, at considerable risk to himself, referred her for an abortion, in a country where, as in the United States, abortions were still illegal when the life of the mother was not at stake.  
她在一个国家终止了怀孕，这个国家与美国一样，当母亲的生命不处于危险时，堕胎仍然是非法的。一位医生冒着相当大的风险将她推荐进行堕胎。  
The doctor, knowing only that she “had broken an engagement at home to seek an unknown fate,” exacted two promises from Steinem: “First, you will not tell anyone my name.  
医生只知道她“为了寻求未知的命运而违背了家里的婚约”，从斯坦因那里得到了两个承诺：“首先，你不要告诉任何人我的名字。”  
Second, you will do what you want to do with your life.” For nearly ten years, nobody, not even her closest friends, knew that she had had an abortion.  
其次，你会做你想要在生活中做的事情。”近十年来，没有人知道她曾经做过人流，甚至她的密友也不知道。

In 1968, Clay Felker, the former features editor of _Esquire_, founded _New York_ and invited Steinem to join his editorial board. “There was Clay, Pete Hamill, Jimmy Breslin, Tom Wolfe, a few other guys, and me,” she said.  
1968 年，前《时尚先生》特稿编辑克莱·费尔克创立了《纽约客》，并邀请斯坦因加入他的编辑委员会。“有克莱、皮特·汉密尔顿、吉米·布雷斯林、汤姆·沃尔夫，几个其他人和我，”她说。  
Four years earlier, she had covered one of her first political campaigns, when Bobby Kennedy was running for senator in New York (an experience she still sums up as sharing a taxi with Gay Talese and Saul Bellow, to whom Talese said, “You know how every year there’s a pretty girl who comes to New York and pretends to be a writer.  
四年前，她报道了她第一次的政治竞选活动，当时鲍比·肯尼迪在纽约竞选参议员（她仍然将这次经历总结为与盖·塔莱斯和索尔·贝娄一起乘坐出租车，塔莱斯对他们说：“你知道每年都有一个漂亮的女孩来到纽约假装自己是作家。”  
Well, Gloria is this year’s pretty girl”).  
嗯，格洛丽亚是今年的美女）。

“I was doing politics, but even at the magazine I was still the girl writer,” she said, “and the guys there, whom I loved, their advice about feminism was, ‘Don’t get involved with those crazy women.’ I thought, These guys are my friends, and they don’t know who I am, because I haven’t said.  
“我从事政治活动，但在杂志社我依然是那个女孩作家，”她说，“那里的那些我爱的人，他们对女性主义的建议是：‘别和那些疯狂的女人搅和在一起。’我想，这些人是我的朋友，但他们不了解我，因为我还没有说。”  
So I covered an abortion-rights speak-out at Judson Memorial Church, in Greenwich Village. Courtesy of that meeting, I learned that one in three American women had needed an abortion at some point in her life. The question was: Why is this illegal?  
所以我报道了在格林威治村朱迪森纪念教堂举行的堕胎权发声会。感谢那次会议，我了解到三分之一的美国女性在其一生中的某个时刻需要堕胎。问题是：为什么这是非法的？  
I could see from my response to the meeting, and the response of the others, that we as women needed to talk about ourselves.” The article that Steinem wrote that week counts among the first mainstream reporting on the women’s movement.  
我从对会议的回应以及他人的回应中看出，我们作为女性需要谈论我们自己。”斯坦姆那周所写的文章被认为是关于妇女运动的首次主流报道之一。

There are certain “mothers” of second-wave feminism, and who they are depends largely on whom you ask. If you ask an academic, she might say Simone de Beauvoir, whose book “The Second Sex” was published in French in 1949 (and in English four years later).  
某些“母亲级”人物是第二波女权主义的，他们是谁很大程度上取决于你问的是谁。如果你问一个学者，她可能会说西蒙娜·德·波伏娃，她的书《第二性》于 1949 年在法国出版（并在四年后的英文版）。  
If you ask a middle-class suburban housewife of the postwar generation, she is likely to credit Betty Friedan, whose “The Feminine Mystique” appeared in 1963.  
如果你问战后一代的中产阶级郊区家庭主妇，她可能会把功劳归功于贝蒂·弗里丹，她的《女性的神秘》于 1963 年出版。  
But if you ask American feminists who came of age in the seventies they will as often as not tell you that the movement came to political life when Gloria Steinem first said, “If it’s not good for all women, it’s not good for any living thing.”  
但是如果你问在七十年代成年的美国女权主义者，他们往往会告诉你，当格洛丽亚·斯泰纳姆第一次说，“如果对所有的女性都不好，那么对任何生物都不好”时，运动才开始进入政治生活。

Beauvoir brought a philosophical and biological history of “female” to the table, lived and travelled with the philandering Jean-Paul Sartre, and was not much given to the cause or company of other women.  
波伏娃将“女性”的哲学和生物历史带到了桌面上，与风流倜傥的让-保尔·萨特一起生活旅行，并不太关注其他女性的事业或陪伴。  
Friedan brought the career ambitions of white, well-to-do women like her, craved the spotlight, and eventually was written off by much of the movement as a “glass-ceiling feminist.” But what Steinem brought, as a writer, an organizer, and an activist, was the then radical conviction that gender, race, class, age, and ethnicity were all targets of inequality, and belonged together in any over-arching struggle for human and civil rights.  
弗里丹带来了像她这样的白人、富裕女性的职业抱负，渴望成为焦点，最终被运动中的许多人视为“玻璃天花板女权主义者”。但斯坦因作为作家、组织者和活动家带来的，是当时激进的信念，即性别、种族、阶级、年龄和民族都是不平等的目标，它们在任何关于人权和公民权利的总体斗争中都属于一体。

In December, 1971, a forty-page preview issue of what would become _Ms._ appeared as a supplement in the year-end issue of _New York_. (“Ms.” was a status-free form of address dating from the seventeenth century.  
1971 年 12 月，《Ms.》杂志的四十页预览版作为年终特刊的增刊出现在《纽约》杂志上。（“Ms.”是一种自 17 世纪以来无阶级的称呼方式。）  
In 1971, the New York Democratic congresswoman and take-no-prisoners feminist firebrand Bella Abzug introduced the bill that made it legal.) In return for those forty pages, Felker paid for the printing and publishing of the first issue, which went on the stands, at a hundred and forty pages, in January.  
1971 年，纽约民主党女国会议员、不妥协的女权主义斗士贝拉·阿布祖引入了使该法案合法化的法案。作为回报，费尔克为这四十页支付了印刷和出版第一期的费用，该期杂志在一百四十页，于 1 月上市。  
“Clay had the sense to know that he couldn’t start a women’s magazine,” Steinem said. “He said—I mean this! He said, ‘If more women were imported from the islands, you wouldn’t need a feminist movement, since it’s really all about child care.’ ”  
克莱伊有常识，知道他不能创办一本女性杂志，”斯坦姆说。“他说——我是认真的！他说，‘如果从岛屿上引进更多女性，你们就不需要女权运动了，因为那其实全是关于育儿的问题。’”

The cover of the _Ms._ preview issue was a housewife “goddess,” painted bright blue (the color of Krishna).  
该期《Ms.》预览版的封面是一位家庭主妇“女神”，画成明亮的蓝色（克莉须那的颜色）。  
She had a baby glowing in her belly and eight arms, each one holding up a tool of her unpaid trade: an iron, a steering wheel, a mirror, a ringing phone, a clock, a duster, a frying pan, and a typewriter.  
她腹中孕育着婴儿，八只手臂各自举起她未领薪水的职业工具：一把铁锤、一个方向盘、一面镜子、一部响铃的电话、一个时钟、一块抹布、一个煎锅和一台打字机。  
The features included Jane O’Reilly’s instant classic “The Housewife’s Moment of Truth”; Steinem’s essay “On Sisterhood”; a feminist evaluation of the Presidential candidates that year; and a declaration, “We Have Had Abortions,” that was signed by Steinem and fifty-two other well-known women, among them Nora Ephron, Lee Grant, Lillian Hellman, Billie Jean King, and Anaïs Nin.  
该期特色包括简·奥雷利的即时经典之作《家庭主妇的真相时刻》；斯坦姆的论文《关于姐妹情谊》；当年总统候选人的女权主义评估；以及一份由斯坦姆和五十二位知名女性（包括诺拉·艾芙隆、李·格兰特、莉莉安·赫尔曼、比利·简·金和安娜伊斯·宁）签署的宣言：“我们曾经堕过胎”。  
The issue sold out in eight days. Within a few years, there were half a million subscribers.  
该问题在八天内售罄。几年后，有五十万订阅者。

Once a month, Steinem spends an evening with a few old friends from the magazine. She calls those evenings “our group-therapy sessions,” and by the time I joined them one night, she was sharing desserts with Joanne Edgar and Suzanne Braun Levine.  
每月一次，斯坦姆会和几位杂志的老朋友共度一个晚上。她把这些晚上称为“我们的团体治疗会议”，在我加入他们那一晚，她已经和乔安妮·埃加德和苏珊·布朗·莱文一起分享甜点了。

Levine was the editor of _Ms._ for its first seventeen years. She arrived there a magazine veteran. “I was lucky,” she told me. “I was the right age and had the right amount of experience, and I came with a whole color-coded production chart.  
莱文是《Ms.》杂志的编辑，担任了前十七年。她到那里时已经是一位杂志老手。“我很幸运，”她告诉我。“我正处于合适的年龄，拥有适量的经验，而且我带来了一份完整的彩色编码生产图表。”  
I arrived at the office for my interview, and right away Gloria brought me coffee. I never looked back.” Edgar, a founding editor, had met Steinem in the summer of ’71, shortly before the long-anticipated Equal Rights Amendment passed the House of Representatives.  
我到达办公室参加面试，格洛丽亚立刻给我端来咖啡。我从未回头。”爱德华，一位创始人编辑，在 1971 年夏天遇到了斯坦姆，就在备受期待的平等权利修正案在众议院通过之前不久。  
(It passed the Senate in the spring, only to be defeated, a few years later, when a state-by-state referendum fell three states short of the number required to become law.) Bella Abzug, rallying America’s feminists to fight with their votes, had helped put together the National Women’s Political Caucus that summer.  
（该法案在春季获得参议院通过，但几年后，在州际 referendum 中因三票之差未能成为法律。）贝拉·阿布祖（Bella Abzug）动员美国女权主义者通过投票进行斗争，帮助在那年夏天组建了全国妇女政治联盟。  
Steinem, a founding member, travelled the country, organizing and speaking, and Edgar, a Southerner, was dispatched to Mississippi and Missouri to work on political campaigns.  
斯坦姆，一位创始人，走遍全国，组织和演讲，而来自南方的埃德加则被派往密西西比州和密苏里州参与政治活动。  
Back in New York, after the elections, she joined the ranks of feminists with mattress privileges in the loft that Steinem had built above her dining table and went to work at _Ms._  
回到纽约，选举之后，她加入了在斯坦姆在她餐桌上方建造的阁楼里拥有床垫特权的女权主义者行列，并在《女士》杂志开始了工作。

A few minutes into dessert, the women began reminiscing about _Ms._ “before the Australians”—meaning the Australian media group that took over the magazine during a slump in 1987.  
几分钟的甜点时间后，女人们开始回忆起“在澳大利亚人之前”——指的是在 1987 年杂志陷入低谷时接管该杂志的澳大利亚媒体集团。  
Two years later, a group of American feminists was able to buy it back, and eventually Steinem helped form a foundation to keep the magazine in print, ad-free, as a monthly.  
两年后，一群美国女权主义者能够将其回购，最终斯坦姆帮助成立了一个基金会，以保持该杂志印刷、无广告的形式作为月刊。  
It is a quarterly now, and its moment is in some ways past, but the message it sent was clearly heard, and today mass women’s magazines like _Cosmopolitan_ vet political candidates for their stands on women’s issues, and groups like Emily’s List raise money to rank and support feminists among those candidates.  
这是一份季刊，它的时刻在某些方面已经过去，但它传达的信息显然被听到了，如今像《大都会》这样的大众女性杂志会审查政治候选人在女性问题上的立场，而像艾米莉名单这样的团体则为排名和支持这些候选人中的女权主义者筹集资金。

In the early nineties, Steinem decided that something important was missing in the peripatetic life that she describes as “the mixture of freedom and insecurity I needed.” By then, she owned two floors of the brownstone, but the only part that felt like home to her, and not a way station and movement hostel, was her desk and the garden off her study—and that was thanks to a green-thumbed reporter named Irene Kubota Neves, who had interviewed her, years earlier, for _People_, and, lacking a garden of her own to love, adopted Steinem’s.  
20 世纪 90 年代初，斯坦姆决定，在她描述的“我需要的自由与不安全感的混合”这种游牧生活中，缺少了某些重要东西。那时，她已经拥有了那栋褐色石屋的两层，但对她来说，唯一感觉像家的是她的办公桌和书房外的花园——这要归功于一位名叫伊丽娜·库博塔·内韦斯的园艺爱好者记者，她多年前曾为《人物》杂志采访过她，由于自己没有花园可以爱护，便收养了斯坦姆的花园。

The result was that Steinem went shopping. She started collecting furniture and fabrics.  
结果斯坦姆去购物了。她开始收集家具和布料。  
She bought frames for the photographs, pictures, poems, and letters that document the stages of her life on the road, including an almost comically deranged Christmas letter sent in 1971 to friends and family by a right-wing second cousin whom she had met four times, when she was in her teens.  
她为照片、图画、诗歌和信件买了相框，这些信件记录了她路上生活的各个阶段，包括 1971 年她十几岁时，一位她见过四次、右翼的堂兄寄给朋友和家人的几乎滑稽疯狂的圣诞信。  
After wishing everyone a Merry Christmas, the cousin digressed, accusing Steinem, who at the time was raising money for the Angela Davis defense fund, of letting herself be “brainwashed” into endorsing such an “un-American” cause as the Black Panthers.  
在向每个人祝福圣诞快乐之后，这位表兄转移了话题，指责斯坦姆，当时她正在为安吉拉·戴维斯辩护基金筹集资金，让自己被“洗脑”，支持如此“非美国化”的事业，即黑豹党。  
“Gloria is the first of our name to depart from the American principles as to help our sworn enemies, so we must repudiate her pronouncements and disclaim the cousinship. Love and kisses.” Steinem told me, “I was thrilled to get it. Suppose he had liked me?”  
格洛丽亚是我们名字中第一个背离美国原则去帮助我们的誓言敌人的，因此我们必须否定她的声明并否认我们之间的关系。爱与吻。” 斯坦姆告诉我，“我收到这封信时非常激动。他是不是喜欢我？”

The walls of her living room are painted a warm, deep Indian yellow.  
她的客厅墙壁被涂成温暖、深沉的印度黄色。  
There are pillows everywhere, kilims on the floor, and an old wooden fireplace, with a couple of painted wooden figures, carved like mermaids—“I think from an Indian carrousel,” Steinem told me—lying on the mantelpiece, and, hanging between them, a long, thick, heavily decorated black tunic from Afghanistan, or maybe Kashmir.  
到处都是枕头，地板上有地毯，还有一个古老的木制壁炉，上面摆放着几个画有木制人物，雕刻得像美人鱼——“我想是从印度旋转木马上来的，”斯坦因告诉我——它们躺在壁炉架上，它们之间挂着一件长长的、厚重的、装饰华丽的黑色长袍，来自阿富汗，或者可能是克什米尔。  
The room is cozy and crowded. When it gets too crowded, Steinem gives something away. “It’s how I redecorate,” she says.  
房间舒适而拥挤。当它变得过于拥挤时，斯坦姆会放弃一些东西。“这就是我重新装饰的方式，”她说。

Posters cover the walls of the front hall—a time line of the movement as it has changed during the course of her life.  
海报覆盖了前厅的墙壁——记录了运动在她一生中的变化历程。  
For years, Steinem made it a rule never to lecture or organize unless a woman of color was invited with her—a woman who could reach women whose problems of discrimination were far greater than her own—and also for courage, because she is prone to stagefright.  
多年来，斯坦因规定除非有色人种女性被邀请与她一起演讲或组织活动——一个能够触及那些歧视问题远比她自身更大的女性——而且也因为勇气，因为她容易有舞台恐惧症。  
(“Ironic,” she told me.  
“讽刺，”她对我说。  
“A media worker who loses her saliva before a talk and has to carry a mouth spray in her bag.”) There are posters of Steinem on the road with Dorothy Pitman-Hughes, the African-American activist who had opened a multiracial feminist day-care center in New York, and, later in the seventies, with the great black activist Flo Kennedy (who brought down the house one night when a redneck in the audience demanded to know if she and Steinem were lesbians, and she replied, “Are _you_ my alternative?”). And there are posters from fund-raising galas of the past few decades, when feminism became a fashionable liberal cause, and women with money began sitting on movement boards.  
一位在演讲前失去唾液并需要在包里携带口喷的媒体工作者。路上有斯坦姆和非洲裔美国人活动家多萝西·皮特曼-休斯的宣传海报，她曾在纽约开设了一个多族裔女权主义日托中心，还有在七十年代后期与伟大的黑人活动家弗洛·肯尼迪（有一次在观众中一个乡巴佬要求知道她是否和斯坦姆是同性恋，她回答说：“你是我的替代品吗？”）的宣传海报。还有过去几十年筹款盛会的海报，当时女权主义成为时尚的左翼事业，有钱的女性开始坐在运动委员会上。

我知道我们没做成什么，但会议就是为了这个。

Steinem welcomed them all—the rich, the celebrities, the climbers for the cause. She was a radical but, consciously, never an outsider.  
斯坦因欢迎了所有人——富人、名人、为事业攀登的人。她是一个激进分子，但自觉从未是局外人。  
She enjoyed the world where she plied her trade as an entrepreneur of social change, and, with her mouth spray at hand, she had long since mastered the subterfuges of talking truth to power. You could call it consciousness-raising—on a wider canvas.  
她享受着作为社会变革企业家从事贸易的世界，并且，手握喷雾器，她早已掌握了向权力说真话的诡计。你可以称之为意识提升——在一个更广阔的画布上。  
When she went to Los Angeles to speak at a big Equality Now benefit last fall, she told me how much she was looking forward to a meeting with the honchos at Creative Artists Agency the next day.  
当她去年秋天去洛杉矶参加一个大型“平等现在”慈善活动时，她告诉我她多么期待第二天与创意艺术家代理公司的负责人会面。  
They were going to discuss the status of women in the film industry—their comparatively low numbers, their discrepant salaries—and it didn’t matter if those women were movie stars or grips, or if her meeting required a large application of charm, dazzle, and good humor.  
他们打算讨论电影行业中女性的地位——她们相对较少的数量，她们不一致的薪水——无论这些女性是电影明星还是场工，或者如果她的会议需要大量运用魅力、耀眼和幽默感，这都无关紧要。  
She was ready for that.  
她准备好了。

For Steinem, the “conflagration of consciousness” that transformed second-wave feminism into a national movement occurred on November 18, 1977, when Bella Abzug—having persuaded Congress to authorize, and fund, a National Women’s Conference, in Houston—took the stage.  
对于斯坦姆来说，将第二波女权主义转变为全国性运动的“意识烈火”发生在 1977 年 11 月 18 日，当时贝拉·阿布祖说服国会授权并资助在休斯顿举行的一次全国妇女大会，并走上了舞台。  
It was an occasion that Steinem describes in her new book, beginning with the sentence “This conference may take the prize as the most important event nobody knows about.” Steinem had already spent nearly a year organizing with Abzug, her congressional colleagues Patsy Mink and Shirley Chisholm, and a commission, appointed by President Carter, asking other American women what they, as women, wanted.  
这是斯坦姆在她新书中所描述的一次场合，从句子“这次会议可能成为无人知晓的最重要事件，并获得奖项。”开始。斯坦姆已经与阿布茨、她的国会同事帕特西·明克和雪莉·奇莎姆，以及卡特总统任命的一个委员会一起，花费了近一年的时间来组织，询问其他美国女性，作为女性，她们想要什么。  
She had travelled, state by state, to insure a more or less consistent process for the election of Houston delegates, and discovered, to her shock, the extent to which grass-roots groups of ultra-conservative women were prepared to co-opt that process—or, as she puts it, “Success can be as disastrous as failure—and it almost was.”  
她走遍各个州，以确保选举休斯顿代表的过程尽可能一致，结果让她震惊地发现，极端保守的女性草根组织准备如何利用这一过程——或者，正如她所说，“成功可能和失败一样灾难性——它几乎就是这样。”

Two thousand official delegates came to the conference to discuss and vote on twenty-six different planks, from child care and lesbian rights, to foreign policy, and they were joined by eighteen thousand observers, making it, Steinem says, “probably the most geographically, racially, and economically representative body this nation has ever seen.”  
两千名正式代表参加了会议，讨论和投票了二十六个不同议题，从儿童保育和女同性恋权利到外交政策，他们还得到了一万八千名观察员的加入，正如斯坦姆所说，“可能是这个国家有史以来在地理、种族和经济上最具代表性的团体。”

Steinem describes her “surprise duty” at the conference as that of a scribe, since she had been asked by the various caucuses of women of color to collect and coördinate both their particular and their shared concerns and put them together in a plank that would replace the “so-called Minority Women’s Plank” submitted by various state conferences.  
斯坦因将她在会议上的“意外职责”描述为一名书记员，因为她被各色人种女性小组请求收集和协调她们各自和共同关心的问题，并将它们汇总成一个纲领，以取代各州会议提交的“所谓的少数族裔女性纲领”。  
What Steinem doesn’t describe is the extent of the women’s trust in her to do that. She had fought in solidarity with these women for years. “There is no competition of tears in feminism,” she once told me.  
斯坦因没有描述的是女性对她做那件事的信任程度。她与这些女性团结战斗了多年。“在女权主义中，没有眼泪的竞争，”她曾告诉我。  
“If you’ve suffered discrimination, you’re sensitive to it on every level. I learned feminism largely from black women. Women of color basically invented feminism.”  
如果你遭受过歧视，你会在每个层面上对它敏感。我主要从黑人女性那里学习女性主义。有色人种女性基本上发明了女性主义。

The women of color who came as delegates to Houston were scattered across the country. They had never met as a group before. “It was the first time I realized that being a writer was also being an activist,” Steinem told me.  
有色人种的女性代表们散布在全国各地。她们以前从未作为一个团体见过面。“这是我第一次意识到，成为一名作家也是成为一名活动家，”斯坦姆告诉我。  
The African-American women raised “the umbrella issues of racism and poverty.” The Asian-Americans added language barriers, sweatshops, and isolation.  
非洲裔美国女性提出了“种族主义和贫困的普遍问题。”亚裔美国人增加了语言障碍、血汗工厂和孤立。  
The Chicana women added the ever-present fear of deportation—and of having to leave their children behind to be brought up by strangers.  
奇卡诺女性增加了始终存在的被驱逐出境的恐惧——以及不得不将孩子留给陌生人抚养的恐惧。  
But Steinem says that nothing prepared her for the Native American women, who wanted to protect their languages and their culture, and to reclaim something of the tribal sovereignty guaranteed by the treaties that, as often as not, had betrayed them.  
但斯坦姆表示，没有任何事情为她准备迎接美洲原住民妇女，她们想要保护自己的语言和文化，并收回由条约保证的部落主权，而这些条约往往背叛了她们。  
They had one of the toughest jobs in Houston: to educate the only country we have, as one of the delegates put it, to the fact that we are also here.  
他们拥有休斯顿最艰巨的工作之一：正如一位代表所说，教育我们唯一的乡村，让我们认识到我们也在这里。

The conference was a green light for millions of American women, including liberal Republican women—an endangered species now.  
会议为数百万人，包括自由派共和党女性——现在是一种濒危物种——发出了绿灯。  
(Betty Ford, who had campaigned for the E.R.A. and been a strong supporter of abortion rights, spoke there, along with Lady Bird Johnson and Rosalynn Carter.) The N.W.C. delivered a political program that included equal employment, equal pay, and, crucially, full reproductive rights.  
（贝蒂·福特曾为平等权利修正案（E.R.A.）奔走呼号，并坚定支持堕胎权，她在那里发表了讲话，与 Lady Bird Johnson 和 Rosalynn Carter 一同出席。全国妇女委员会（N.W.C.）提出了一项政治计划，包括平等就业、平等薪酬，以及至关重要的全面生育权利。）

In the decades since then, feminists here and abroad have travelled, connected, and discovered that, in a new “world economy” of labor migration, immigration, and capital exploitation, women everywhere are at risk.  
自那时起，国内外女权主义者四处旅行、建立联系，发现在一个新的“世界经济体”中，劳动力流动、移民和资本剥削，世界各地女性都面临风险。  
The Global Fund for Women was founded by three Palo Alto feminists in 1987 and Equality Now in 1992, by Jessica Neuwirth, an American lawyer who had worked for Amnesty International; Navi Pillay, a South African lawyer; and Feryal Gharahi, an Iranian women’s-rights lawyer.  
全球妇女基金由三位帕洛阿尔托女权主义者于 1987 年创立，以及平等现在组织于 1992 年，由杰西卡·纽沃斯（一位曾为国际特赦组织工作的美国律师）、纳维·皮拉伊（一位南非律师）和费瑞尔·加哈里（一位伊朗女权律师）共同创立。  
A year later, the Iraqi-American activist Zainab Salbi put together Women for Women International. The issues for those groups now involve the kinds of violence against women that cross borders and increase as wars proliferate and the gaps in wealth widen.  
一年后，伊拉克裔美国活动家扎因巴·萨尔比组建了“国际女性之声”。这些团体现在面临的问题包括跨越国界的针对女性的暴力行为，随着战争的蔓延和贫富差距的扩大而加剧。

In America, sex trafficking is said to be as high today as in any other country. Honor killings and forced marriages have been reported.  
在美国，据说性贩卖的规模今天与其他任何国家一样高。有报道说发生了荣誉谋杀和强迫婚姻。  
Female genital mutilation, which affects ninety per cent of girls and women in countries like Somalia and Egypt, is now practiced within the diaspora here, despite a ban dating from the Clinton Presidency.  
女性割礼，影响索马里和埃及等国家 90%的女孩和妇女，尽管自克林顿总统时期以来就有禁令，但现在在海外侨民中仍在实行。  
According to Yasmeen Hassan, the global executive director of Equality Now, and the author of the first book on domestic violence to appear in Pakistan, more than five hundred thousand girls and women in the United States either are at risk of having F.G.M. or have already had it.  
根据 Yasmeen Hassan，平等现在全球执行董事、也是巴基斯坦首部关于家庭暴力的书籍的作者，美国有超过五十万名女孩和妇女面临割礼的风险，或者已经接受了割礼。

Steinem’s friends say that she can spot a strong feminist like Hassan from a helicopter, the way Sarah Palin claims she can spot a moose.  
斯坦因的朋友说她可以从直升机上认出像哈桑这样的强势女权主义者，就像莎拉·佩林声称她可以从直升机上认出一头驼鹿一样。  
It’s part of Steinem’s organizational agenda, and it can happen anywhere—on the street, or in a restaurant, or in line at a movie, when a stranger comes up to her and they begin to talk. It can even happen on the telephone.  
这是斯坦因组织议程的一部分，它可能发生在任何地方——在街上，在餐厅里，或者在电影院排队时，当陌生人走到她面前开始交谈。甚至可能发生在电话中。  
Pamela Shifman was a young white American working in South Africa, four years after apartheid ended, as a legal adviser to the parliamentary women’s caucus of the African National Congress, when someone in her office said, “This woman from your country, someone named Gloria Steinem, keeps calling.  
帕梅拉·希夫曼是一位年轻的白人美国人在南非工作，在种族隔离结束后的四年里，她作为非洲国民大会议会女议员小组的法律顾问，当时她办公室里有人说：“这个来自你们国家、名叫格洛丽亚·斯泰纳姆的女人一直在打电话。”  
She wants to be helpful, so here’s her phone number.” Shifman called. “We’d never met, but Gloria was coming to South Africa for a conference, and she said, ‘I can stay on. Just tell me for how long, and let me do whatever you need me for,’ ” Shifman said. “She stayed a week.  
她想要帮忙，所以这是她的电话号码。”Shifman 打电话说。“我们从未见过面，但 Gloria 要来南非参加一个会议，她说：‘我可以留下来。告诉我你需要我待多久，让我做你需要我做的任何事情。’”Shifman 说。“她待了一个星期。”  
She worked with me on organizing strategies, and went to meetings with me, from morning to night. The only thing she asked of me was to take her to see the Rain Queen of the Balobedu”—whose job was to make rain, and to carry and pass on the oral history of her people.  
她和我一起制定组织策略，从早到晚和我参加会议。她唯一要求我做的事情就是带她去看巴洛贝杜的雨后女王”——她的工作是降雨，并携带和传承她人民的口头历史。  
“Gloria had a ring, from her friend Wilma Mankiller, the Chief of the Cherokee Nation, to give to the queen, and she did.”  
格洛丽亚有一枚戒指，是她的朋友切罗基族首领威尔玛·曼基勒送给她的，用来送给女王，她确实送了。

Steinem was determined that I speak with other women who, like Shifman, came of age during the eighties and nineties and are known in the movement as “third wave” feminists. Amy Richards was the first. “The smartest person I know,” Steinem said.  
斯坦因姆决心要和我交谈其他女性，她们像希夫曼一样，在八十年代和九十年代成长起来，在运动中被称为“第三波”女权主义者。艾米·理查兹是第一个。“我所认识的最聪明的人，”斯坦因姆说。  
Richards went to work for Steinem as a Barnard intern and now works alongside her on nearly every project, while at the same time, with a partner, writing books, running a young-feminist speakers bureau called Soapbox, and shepherding groups of college students through an intensive week of consultations and tough encounters at a Feminist Boot Camp.  
理查兹曾作为巴纳德学院的实习生为斯坦姆工作，现在几乎在每个项目上都与她并肩工作，同时，与一位合作伙伴合著书籍，运营一个名为“肥皂箱”的年轻女权主义演讲局，并带领大学生小组在女权主义训练营中度过一周密集的咨询和艰难的遭遇。

One night, Steinem crossed Central Park to visit Jessica Neuwirth, the later second-wave feminist who co-founded Equality Now but who, in the spirit of the third wave, had launched an online feminist nonprofit called Donor Direct Action, designed to connect donors with the grass-roots groups they support abroad.  
一天晚上，斯坦因穿过中央公园去拜访杰西卡·纽瓦 irth，她是后来的第二波女权主义者，共同创立了“平等现在”组织，但在第三波精神的影响下，她发起了一个名为“捐赠者直接行动”的在线女权主义非营利组织，旨在将捐赠者与他们在海外的支持草根组织联系起来。  
Neuwirth was grappling with a crisis involving one of her most at-risk recipient groups—a highly visible women’s-rights organization in Libya, whose director, Salwa Bugaighis, a fearless lawyer, had just been murdered by five assassins in her home, in Benghazi; Bugaighis’s husband had been abducted and her sister threatened that she was “next.”  
纽维尔特正面临着一场危机，涉及她最易受伤害的受助群体之一——一个在利比亚非常显眼的妇女权利组织，该组织的负责人萨拉瓦·布加伊希斯，一位无畏的律师，刚刚在她家中被五名刺客谋杀；布加伊希斯的丈夫被绑架，她的妹妹威胁说她是“下一个”。

Neuwirth had also invited Navi Pillay over and she wanted Steinem and Pillay—until last year, the U.N. High Commissioner for Human Rights—to help talk her through her choices and advise her on what to do to avoid exposing any successors to Bugaighis to the same risks.  
纽维尔特还邀请了纳维·皮拉伊，她希望斯坦姆和皮拉伊——直到去年，联合国人权事务高级专员——帮助她通过她的选择，并就如何避免将任何接替布加伊西的人暴露于相同风险提供建议。  
Steinem pointed out that every feminist active in Libya knew them, and, by being active, had chosen to take them on. Pillay listened, reserving judgment. Ten days later, Neuwirth introduced her donors to the Salwa Fund, using a video made by Bugaighis before her death.  
斯坦姆指出，每个在利比亚活跃的女权主义者都认识他们，通过积极参与，他们选择了接受他们。皮莱倾听，保留判断。十天后，纽沃斯向她的捐助者介绍了萨拉瓦基金，使用的是布加伊希斯在她去世前制作的视频。  
The video was now called “Rest in Peace, Salwa.” And the crisis remains unresolved.  
视频现名为“愿沙拉瓦安息。”危机仍未解决。

Ai-jen Poo, the advocate for domestic workers and caregivers who won a MacArthur “genius award” last year, was another woman on Steinem’s list. Poo had started organizing as a student, and at twenty-two, with a grant from the Ms. Foundation, she began to organize domestic workers nationally—ninety per cent of them minority women—into associations.  
艾金·普，去年获得麦克阿瑟“天才奖”的国内工人和护理工作者倡导者，是斯坦姆名单上的另一位女性。普从学生时代就开始组织活动，22 岁时，在 Ms.基金会的一项资助下，她开始在全国范围内组织国内工人——其中 90%是少数族裔女性——成立协会。  
Today, at forty-one, she is the director of the National Domestic Workers Alliance, with forty-seven affiliates across the country, representing fifteen thousand members.  
今天，41 岁的她成为了全国家庭服务员联盟的主任，该联盟在全国有 47 个分支机构，代表着一万五千名成员。  
Poo once described Steinem to me as the feminist “who broke down all the silos of separation”—a second-wave feminist with a third-wave commitment to collective leadership and voice. “A lot of people talk about ‘network theory’ now,” she said.  
波伏娃曾这样描述斯坦因：“她是打破所有隔阂的女权主义者”——一位第二波女权主义者，对第三波集体领导和发声有着承诺。“现在很多人都在谈论‘网络理论’，”她说。  
“It’s become an all-feminist strategy. But Gloria was always dedicated to lifting other women up, to sharing leadership with them. It was never about herself. And, because of that, for young feminists, it’s the new norm.”  
它已成为一种全女性主义策略。但格洛丽亚始终致力于提升其他女性，与他们分享领导权。这从未关乎她自己。正因为如此，对于年轻的女性主义者来说，这成为了新的常态。

This was evident when three of those younger feminists arrived at Steinem’s house one night for a few hours of vegetarian take-out, catching up, and sharing stories.  
这在一群年轻女权主义者深夜来到斯坦姆的家，花几个小时吃素食外卖、聊天和分享故事时表现得尤为明显。  
Salamishah Tillet, a professor of English and African Studies at the University of Pennsylvania, came with her sister Scheherazade, an artist-in-residence at the Art Institute of Chicago.  
萨利马什·蒂莱特，宾夕法尼亚大学英语和非洲研究教授，与她妹妹、芝加哥艺术学院驻院艺术家谢赫拉扎德一同前来。

Scheherazade tucked herself into the corner of a couch.  
舍赫拉兹德蜷缩在沙发的一角。  
Pamela Shifman, who is now the executive director of NoVo Foundation, one of America’s largest nonprofits for girls and women (it was founded by Warren Buffett’s son Peter and Peter’s wife, Jennifer), opted for the floor, having made the shortest commute, from Brooklyn.  
帕梅拉·希夫曼，现在是美国最大的非营利组织之一 NoVo 基金会（由沃伦·巴菲特之子彼得及其妻子詹妮弗创立）的执行董事，选择了地面层，因为她从布鲁克林通勤距离最短。  
And the playwright and performer Sarah Jones, best known for her Tony Award-winning multi-character piece “Bridge &amp; Tunnel,” joined us, by way of a Skype video stream, from the West Village, where she was in jet-lag meltdown after three weeks in Europe, interviewing prostitutes for a new show she was developing called “Sell/Buy/Date.”  
戏剧家和表演者莎拉·琼斯，因她的托尼奖获奖多角色作品《桥与隧道》而闻名，通过 Skype 视频流从西村加入我们，她在欧洲度过三周时间采访妓女，为即将开发的新剧《买卖约会》做准备，此时她正处于时差反应崩溃中。

Jones had spent some of her trip sitting in a red-lit storefront window in Amsterdam, soliciting. “I wanted to have this experience,” she told her friends at Steinem’s. But it was the prostitutes themselves—“the politics” of their indoctrination—who unsettled her.

The women responded to Jones with some preoccupations of their own.  
女性们对琼斯回应了一些她们自己的忧虑。  
Salamishah, who had been raped herself, talked about rape victims, who become “super invisible,” even and especially to themselves, and asked the group, “How do you intervene without using the language of pathology?” Scheherazade asked, “How do we create” a new language?  
萨拉米沙，自己也曾遭受过强奸，她谈论了成为“超级隐形”的强奸受害者，甚至尤其是对自己而言，并问小组：“你们如何在不使用病理学语言的情况下介入？”舍赫拉扎德问道：“我们如何创造一种新的语言？”  
Steinem quoted the runaway slave and abolitionist Harriet Tubman—who, on forays into the South, freed hundreds of other slaves—saying, “I could have saved thousands if only they knew they were slaves.” And Shifman talked about the women who used to be housed at the Bayview Correctional Facility, in Manhattan, across from Chelsea Piers.  
斯坦姆引用了逃跑的奴隶和废奴主义者哈丽特·塔布曼——她在南方远征中解放了数百名其他奴隶——说：“如果他们知道自己是奴隶，我本可以拯救成千上万的人。” 而希夫曼谈到了曾经被安置在曼哈顿贝维尤矫正设施中的女性，该设施位于切尔西码头对面。  
Bayview, a notoriously cruel women’s prison, was closed three years ago, and its prisoners were transferred.  
贝维尤，一所臭名昭著的残酷女子监狱，三年前被关闭，囚犯被转移。  
Shifman said that at one point the prison—its women scarred from puberty by the inextricable triad of poverty, prostitution, and drugs—had the highest percentage of staff sexual misconduct of any prison in the United States.  
石曼表示，在某个时期，这家监狱——那里的女性因贫困、卖淫和毒品这不可分割的三重奏而早早地经历了青春期——是美国任何监狱中工作人员性不当行为比例最高的。

The conversation was wide-ranging, but by the end of the evening no one, including Jones, knew what, exactly, to make of that week in Amsterdam.  
对话内容广泛，但到晚上结束时，包括琼斯在内，没有人确切知道该如何看待在阿姆斯特丹的那一周。

It’s safe to say that for feminists in their twenties—think “fourth wave” feminists—social media has put an expiration date on many of the old certainties.  
可以说，对于二十多岁的女权主义者——即所谓的“第四波”女权主义者——社交媒体给许多旧有的确定性标上了保质期。  
The Internet effect has arguably been paradoxical, something that is at once concentrating and diluting the political energy and solidarity of the women’s movement, leaving young women free to confront new issues in necessarily new ways.  
互联网效应可以说是矛盾的，它既集中又稀释了妇女运动的政冶能量和团结，让年轻女性能够以必要的新方式自由面对新问题。

Those issues—sex-work issues, race issues, sexual-assault issues, police-brutality issues, even transgender issues and language-identity issues—have in fact always been movement issues and, in particular, Steinem’s issues.  
这些问题——性工作问题、种族问题、性侵犯问题、警察暴力问题，甚至跨性别问题和语言身份问题——实际上一直是运动问题，尤其是斯坦姆的问题。  
She worked hard to get lesbian rights included in the platform of the N.W.C. in 1977. (Even Friedan, who called lesbians “the lavender menace,” voted for it.) And her record on racism and police brutality has been unimpeachable.  
她努力争取在 1977 年将女同性恋权利纳入 N.W.C.的平台上。（即使是称女同性恋为“紫罗兰威胁”的弗里丹，也投了赞成票。）她在种族主义和警察暴力方面的记录无可挑剔。  
(“Violence in any patriarchy begins at home, in the family,” she told me.)  
“任何父权制中的暴力都始于家庭，始于家庭，”她告诉我。

She likes to point out that today’s generational byword, “intersectionality,” was in fact coined in the late eighties, by the African-American law professor and race theorist Kimberlé Crenshaw, who used it to describe interconnected forms of discrimination, whose consequences each woman has to balance and negotiate, and feminists have to acknowledge and understand.  
她喜欢指出，今天这一代人的流行语“交叉性”实际上是在 20 世纪 80 年代末由非裔美国法学教授和种族理论家金伯利·克伦肖创造的，她用它来描述相互关联的歧视形式，每个女性都必须平衡和协商其后果，而女权主义者必须承认和理解。

Steinem readily acknowledges that the Internet has been revolutionary in giving feminists—particularly black and gay feminists, who in the past were largely unheard by the mainstream media—a voice, and that some important young feminists are now emerging through platforms like Twitter.  
斯坦因 readily acknowledges that the Internet has been revolutionary in giving feminists—particularly black and gay feminists, who in the past were largely unheard by the mainstream media—a voice, and that some important young feminists are now emerging through platforms like Twitter. 斯坦因 readily acknowledges that the Internet has been revolutionary in giving feminists—尤其是黑人女权和同性恋女权主义者，他们在过去很大程度上被主流媒体忽视——一个声音，并且一些重要的年轻女权主义者现在正通过像推特这样的平台崭露头角。  
But she is wary about calling the Internet effect unequivocally “democratizing,” as many of those feminists claim.  
但她对将互联网效应明确称为“民主化”持谨慎态度，正如许多女权主义者所主张的那样。

You have to ask where a lot of these posts about our so-called divisions on issues like race and gender come from. What’s the context? Who’s arguing?

这是人们在网上彼此如此敌对的部分原因，尤其是女性，经常遭受网络骚扰。”

Two debates that have played out online particularly trouble Steinem.  
两次在线辩论尤其让斯坦姆感到困扰。  
One involves the idea of prostitution as sex work—a legitimate trade that women can decide to practice, and which should be protected through legal regulation, as advocates argue, rather than one that women are forced into, as Steinem is inclined to believe.  
一种观点认为卖淫是性工作——一种女性可以选择从事的合法贸易，应该通过法律规范来保护，正如倡导者所主张的，而不是像斯坦姆倾向于相信的那样，是女性被迫进入的。  
“The word ‘work’ can be double-edged,” she told me.  
“‘工作’这个词有两面性，”她告诉我。  
“The problem of legitimizing the sex trade as work, the way it’s done in Germany, for example, is that ‘work’ has consequences, one being that you are required to do just that—work—and if you refuse to accept a client, or to do something a client wants, you can be fired, or worse.” Then, there’s the matter of choice.  
性交易合法化作为工作的问题，例如在德国的做法，问题在于“工作”有后果，其中之一就是你必须做那件事——工作——如果你拒绝接受客户，或者做客户想要的事情，你可能会被解雇，或者更糟。然后，还有选择的问题。  
Steinem finds it unlikely that anyone actually chooses to be a sex worker, certainly not when she’s twelve years old—roughly the average age of entry into prostitution—or when the rate of trauma and injury among prostitutes is comparable to that of soldiers in wartime, or when what qualifies as consent, in legal terms, may not be consensual at all but enforced.  
斯坦姆认为，没有人会真正选择成为性工作者，当她 12 岁时当然不会——这大约是进入卖淫的平均年龄——或者当妓女中的创伤和伤害率与战时士兵相当时，或者当法律意义上的同意可能根本不是自愿的，而是被迫的时。  
There are exceptions, she says: “Women who make their own arrangements, privately and directly, with a client, and whose only worry is likely to be a big bill from the I.R.S.”

上个月末，Sarah Jones 在一场满座的预览会上介绍了她工作的“买卖/日期”版本，扮演了十四个角色，包括男性和女性，每个人都有不同的故事和视角，讲述他们在性交易中的经历。

在预览之后，斯坦因与她一起上台进行对话。

Sex traffickers, sex-tour managers, brothel owners, and pimps—the people selling the bodies of men, women, and children for their own profit—are arrested. Their clients are fined.  
人口贩子、性旅游经理、妓院老板和皮条客——这些人为了自己的利益出售男人、女人和儿童的肉体——被逮捕。他们的客户被罚款。

The other battle that troubles Steinem began in 2008, when Hillary Clinton and Barack Obama were competing for the Democratic Presidential nomination.  
2008 年，希拉里·克林顿和巴拉克·奥巴马为争夺民主党总统提名而展开的另一场战斗开始困扰斯坦姆。  
Steinem does not officially join political campaigns, on principle, “because if you work on a campaign anything you say reflects on your candidate, and I want—no, I need—to be free to disagree.” But over the years she has stumped independently for countless candidates, as she did for Clinton in the run-up to the Democratic Convention that year (and hopes to do again in the run-up to 2016).  
斯坦姆姆原则上不正式加入政治竞选活动，“因为如果你为竞选活动工作，你说的任何话都会反映在你的候选人身上，而我——不，我需要——有自由表达不同意见。”但多年来，她独立地为无数候选人助选，就像她在那年民主党大会前夕为克林顿助选一样（并希望在 2016 年的前夕再次这样做）。  
Early in the campaign, she had written an Op-Ed in the _Times,_ which the editors entitled, to her surprise and distress, “Women Are Never Front-Runners.”  
竞选初期，她曾在《时代》杂志上发表了一篇社论，编辑们将其标题定为让她惊讶和痛苦：“女性永远不会成为领跑者。”

In it, she presented a hypothetical female Obama, with all of Obama’s characteristics and credentials but the Y chromosome—her point being that, at that moment in the country’s history, the combination of “black” and “woman” still amounted to a kind of double negative and would be deadly at the polls.

在她看来，性别是更大的障碍。“实际上，我认为他们两个都没有机会，但如果有人能成功，那将是奥巴马，”她告诉我。“事实上，我说我们需要的是八年克林顿总统和八年奥巴马总统。”

Mainstream media paid modest attention to Steinem’s piece. Online, however, it fed a divisive debate that, according to Rebecca Traister, the author of a book about the 2008 campaign, “Big Girls Don’t Cry,” was simmering beneath the surface.  
主流媒体对斯坦姆的文章给予了适度关注。然而，在网上，它引发了一场分裂性的辩论，据《2008 年竞选》一书的作者丽贝卡·特雷斯特所说，这场辩论在表面之下暗流涌动。  
Steinem was attacked for being a privileged white woman out of touch with the times.  
斯坦姆因被视为一个脱离时代的特权白人女性而受到攻击。

In August this year, Daunasia Yancey and Julius Jones, two young activists from Black Lives Matter, had a backstage confrontation with Hillary Clinton that was taped, and the video made its way online.  
今年八月，黑人的命也是命运动中的两位年轻活动家 Daunasia Yancey 和 Julius Jones 与希拉里·克林顿在后台发生了冲突，并被录像，视频随后在网上流传。  
The subject was “three strikes and you’re out,” a habitual-offenders law introduced by Bill Clinton in 1994 and adopted over the years, in various forms, by twenty-four states.  
主题是“三振出局”，这是比尔·克林顿于 1994 年引入的惯犯法，多年来以各种形式被 24 个州采纳。  
Yancey and Jones accused Hillary Clinton of complicity in that law, which, although it was drafted to discourage serious offenders and keep them off the streets, had in fact contributed to mass incarceration of black men, often for minor offenses, swelling a prison population that was already about fifty per cent black.  
杨西和琼斯指责希拉里·克林顿在那项法律中存在共谋，尽管该法律旨在阻止严重犯罪分子并让他们远离街头，但实际上却导致了黑人男性大规模监禁，通常是因为轻微罪行，使得一个本已约 50%黑人人口的监狱人口进一步膨胀。  
At the end of a demonstrably tense exchange, Clinton said, “Look, I don’t believe you change hearts. I believe you change laws, you change the allocation of resources, you change the way systems operate.”  
在一场明显紧张的交流结束后，克林顿说：“听着，我不相信你能改变人心。我相信你能改变法律，你能改变资源的分配，你能改变系统运作的方式。”

我询问了 B.L.M.的三十四岁联合创始人阿丽西亚·加尔萨——她同时也是艾金·普的全国家庭工人联盟特别项目总监——关于那次交流。  
“We have twenty-six chapters nationally, and no one leader speaks for black people,” she said. “But what you saw on that video was the dialectic between policy change and culture change.

I was disappointed in Hillary—to be honest, I was sad to see her admonishing two young people.  
我对希拉里感到失望——说实话，看到她责备两个年轻人，我感到很难过。  
She advocated new laws, when what she should have said is ‘The movement has made this issue front and center and changed my heart.’ Hillary should take notes from Gloria, who has always pushed boundaries around,” Garza, who works closely with Steinem, told me.

格洛丽亚谈到了一个事实——这是她骨子里的感觉——那就是种族问题必须成为女性主义问题。

A few days later, Steinem was in New Jersey, giving a talk that ended—as all her talks have since she marched in a Black Lives Matter demonstration—with B.L.M.’s three principles: “Lead with love. Low ego, high impact.  
几天后，斯坦因在新泽西州，发表了一次演讲，就像她自从参加“黑人的命也是命”示威活动以来所有的演讲一样，以 B.L.M.的三个原则结束：“以爱为领导。低自我，高影响力。”  
Move at the speed of trust.” She also wrote a post on Facebook that read “Trump’s greatest damage to women was to raise sympathy for Carly Fiorina by attacking her appearance. . . .  
以信任的速度前进。”她还发布了一条 Facebook 帖子，内容为“特朗普对女性的最大伤害是通过攻击她的外貌来提升卡莉·菲奥莉娜的同情心……”。  
If you thought Republicans could find no woman more damaging to the diversity and needs of the female half of this country than Sarah Palin, take a good look at Carly Fiorina and what she stands for.” Steinem told me that, given platforms like Fiorina’s and those of virtually all the other Republican Presidential candidates—defunding Planned Parenthood, rescinding reproductive rights, abolishing Obamacare, criminalizing immigration, and, with a big nod to industry, denying or ignoring climate change—the left will have to pull together and win.  
如果你认为共和党找不到比莎拉·佩林更损害这个国家多样性和女性一半需求的女人，那么好好看看卡莉·菲奥莉娜和她所代表的一切。”斯坦因告诉我，鉴于像菲奥莉娜这样的平台以及几乎所有其他共和党总统候选人的立场——削减计划生育资金、撤销生育权利、废除奥巴马医改、将移民刑事化，以及对工业的大幅倾斜，否认或忽视气候变化——左派必须团结起来并取得胜利。

Five years ago, Steinem’s friend Wilma Mankiller died, at the age of sixty-five, with Steinem among the friends and family at her bedside.  
五年前，斯坦姆的朋友威尔玛·曼克勒尔去世，享年六十五岁，斯坦姆在亲友的陪伴下守在她的床边。  
Mankiller had been the first elected chief of the Cherokee Nation, and she and Steinem had been close ever since she joined the board of the Ms. Foundation.  
曼克 iller 曾是切罗基族的第一位当选首领，自从她加入 Ms.基金会董事会以来，她和斯坦姆一直关系密切。  
Over the years, Mankiller had become, for Steinem, a kind of spiritual guide—the way Abzug had been her guide through the corridors of Washington power.  
多年来，曼基勒对斯坦姆来说成了一种精神导师——就像阿布茨如何引导她穿越华盛顿权力的走廊一样。  
It was Mankiller, she says, who continued her education in the “deep history” of matrilineality, and the communal talking circles that expressed it. “We have always started our ‘history’ with when hierarchy, patriarchy, and nationalism started,” Steinem told me.  
曼克 iller 说，是她继续在“深层历史”中学习母系传承和表达这种传承的社区谈话圈。“我们总是从等级制度、父权制和民族主义开始我们的‘历史’，”斯坦姆告诉我。  
“But democracy did not come from Greece. It is much, much older, and it came from women and men together.” She added, “The Iroquois Confederacy had circles of consensus—it was matrilineal.”  
但是民主并非源自希腊。它要古老得多，并且是由男性和女性共同创造的。”她补充说，“易洛魁联盟有共识的圆圈——它是母系传承的。”

A few years before Mankiller died, she and Steinem finished an outline and a preface for a book that they were planning to write together. “It was going to be a brief, practical book, citing moments and practices from the past that could be useful to women now,” Steinem said.  
几年前曼克 iller 去世前，她和斯坦因完成了一本他们计划共同撰写的书的提纲和序言。“那将是一本简短实用的书，引用过去的一些时刻和实践，对现在的女性可能有用，”斯坦因说。  
She intends to finish the book now. “I want to contribute our idea that most of human history was very different from what we have today, with our monotheistic patriarchies and their ‘pyramid’ structures of authority from the top,” she said.  
她打算现在完成这本书。“我想贡献我们的观点，即人类历史的大部分与我们现在的情况非常不同，我们有单一神论父权制及其从上至下的‘金字塔’式权力结构，”她说。  
“Many peoples were—and some still are—not gender-based in their languages. And there was rarely a single chief. There was always a chief for peace, and a different one for war. Their societies were not polarized, and not violence-based.” The jury is out on that.  
许多人种的语言并不是基于性别的。而且很少有一个单一的领袖。他们总是有一个和平领袖，还有一个不同的战争领袖。他们的社会不是两极分化的，也不是基于暴力的。关于这一点，众说纷纭。  
Many archeologists and anthropologists would disagree.  
许多考古学家和人类学家会不同意。  
But, as an organizing principle for Steinem, and for the feminists she has brought together, the evocation of an ancient tradition of talking circles for sharing stories, bridging differences, and coming to acceptable common solutions has been a remarkably effective tool.  
但是，对于斯坦因和她所团结的女权主义者来说，唤起一个古老的谈话圈传统，用于分享故事、弥合分歧并达成可接受的共同解决方案，这已经成为一个非常有效的工具。

One night, I asked Steinem about the future. “I learn, I give,” she told me. “I have the greatest luxury of thinking I might make a difference. I say to myself, ask myself: Can anyone else do what I do? If not, I should keep doing what I can uniquely do.  
一天晚上，我向斯坦姆询问关于未来。她说：“我学习，我给予。”我对自己说：“我能做别人做不到的事情吗？如果不能，我就应该继续做我能独特做到的事情。  
For me, it’s a combination of responsibility and pleasure.  
对我来说，这是一种责任与乐趣的结合。

“People are always asking me, ‘Who will you pass the torch to?’ The question makes me angry. There is no one torch—there are many torches—and I’m using my torch to light other torches.  
人们总是问我：“你将把火炬传给谁？”这个问题让我很生气。没有唯一的火炬——有许多火炬——我正用我的火炬去点亮其他火炬。  
There shouldn’t have been a ‘first’ Gloria Steinem, and there won’t be a last one.” Steinem has placed her house in trust as a place where feminists can meet, work, write, and, if they need to, stay. “I don’t plan to die,” she said, laughing. “I’ll be at home, with those women.  
不应该有一个“第一个”格洛丽亚·斯泰纳姆，也不会有一个“最后一个”。斯泰纳姆已经将她房屋设立为信托基金，作为女权主义者可以聚会、工作、写作，如果需要的话，还可以住宿的地方。“我不打算死，”她笑着说，“我会和那些女人在家里。”  
I’ll live with them here.” Steinem says, sometimes, that her funeral will be a benefit.  
我将和他们在这里生活。”斯坦姆有时说，她的葬礼将是一场慈善活动。

At the beginning of “My Life on the Road,” there is a dedication to John Sharpe, the doctor who helped Steinem in London, fifty-eight years ago. “Dear Dr. Sharpe,” it says.  
在《我的道路生涯》一书的开头，作者向约翰·夏普医生致敬，这位医生五十八年前在伦敦帮助了斯坦姆。书中写道：“亲爱的夏普博士。”  
“I believe you, who knew the law was unjust, would not mind if I say this so long after your death: I’ve done the best I could with my life. This book is for you.” ♦  
我相信你，知道法律是不公正的，你不会介意我在你死后这么久还说这句话：我尽了我所能过好我的生活。这本书是为了你。
