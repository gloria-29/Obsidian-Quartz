[Harold Wallace Ross](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1951/12/15/h-w-ross), who founded _The New Yorker_ a century ago, had a rule that no one should ever write about writers, because writers are boring, except to other writers, and he figured the same was true about editors—only it was more true, because no one should even know an editor’s name. That didn’t stop [William Shawn](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1992/12/28/remembering-mr-shawn), who became the editor of the magazine after Ross’s death, in 1951, from naming one of his kids Wallace, for Ross. It didn’t stop [Ann Beattie](https://www.newyorker.com/contributors/ann-beattie) from naming her car Roger, for her _New Yorker_ editor, [Roger Angell](https://www.newyorker.com/news/postscript/remembering-roger-angell-hall-of-famer). And for all I know there are Chihuahuas and nieces and motorcycles at large named [Bob Gottlieb](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/06/26/remembering-robert-gottlieb-editor-extraordinaire), the magazine’s editor from 1987 to 1992; Lady Evans, the titled name of [Tina Brown](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2017/11/20/how-tina-brown-remixed-the-magazine), its editor from 1992 to 1998; and D.R., for David Remnick, its editor since then. (I once had a tuxedo cat named Shaun, with a “u,” but that came from “Finnegans Wake” and doesn’t count.)  
[哈罗德-华莱士-罗斯](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1951/12/15/h-w-ross)在一个世纪前创办了_《纽约客》_，他有一条原则，那就是任何人都不应该写作家，因为作家是无聊的，除了对其他作家来说。这并没有阻止[威廉-肖恩（William Shawn](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1992/12/28/remembering-mr-shawn)）在1951年罗斯去世后成为该杂志的编辑，为他的一个孩子取名华莱士（Wallace），以纪念罗斯。这也没有阻止[安-比蒂](https://www.newyorker.com/contributors/ann-beattie)为她的_《纽约客》_编辑[罗杰-安格尔](https://www.newyorker.com/news/postscript/remembering-roger-angell-hall-of-famer)给她的汽车取名罗杰。据我所知，还有很多吉娃娃、侄女和摩托车都叫[鲍勃-戈特利布](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/06/26/remembering-robert-gottlieb-editor-extraordinaire)，他是该杂志1987年至1992年的编辑；Lady Evans是[蒂娜-布朗](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2017/11/20/how-tina-brown-remixed-the-magazine)的名字，她是该杂志1992年至1998年的编辑；D.R.是大卫-雷姆尼克的名字，他是该杂志此后的编辑。(我曾经养过一只燕尾服猫，名叫肖恩（Shaun），名字里有个 &quot;u&quot;，但那是来自《芬尼根的守灵夜》，不算数）。

Most editors remain unsung. To be unknown is, ordinarily, to be underestimated. “The only great argument I have against writers, generally speaking, is that many of them deny the function of an editor, and I claim editors are important,” Ross once wrote.  
大多数编辑都默默无闻。默默无闻通常意味着被低估。&quot;罗斯曾写道：&quot;一般来说，我反对作家的唯一理由是，他们中的许多人否认编辑的作用，而我却认为编辑很重要。  
For him, editors were worth more than writers in the way that a great batting coach was worth more than a great batter. “Writers are a dime a dozen,” Ross told [James Thurber](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2003/09/08/the-years-with-thurber). “What I want is an editor.” Writers were children; editors were adults. “I can’t find editors,” Ross fumed.  
对他来说，编辑比作家更有价值，就像一个优秀的击球教练比一个优秀的击球手更有价值一样。&quot;罗斯对詹姆斯-瑟伯（James Thurber）说：&quot;作家是凤毛麟角。&quot;我想要的是一个编辑&quot;。作家是孩子，编辑是成年人。&quot;我找不到编辑，&quot;罗斯气愤地说。  
“Nobody grows up.” (The magazine’s editorial director, Henry Finder, once said of Remnick, “I think he regards the editor’s job as being not crazy,” while, on the other hand, “the writer’s prerogative is to be, perhaps, a little crazy.”) Ross also found it useful—and this was a pretty clever trick—to tell writers who balked at being edited that the more they argued with an editor, the less worthy they were of being published.  
&quot;没有人会长大&quot;。(该杂志的编辑总监亨利-芬德曾这样评价雷姆尼克：&quot;我认为他认为编辑的工作就是不要疯。&quot;而另一方面，&quot;作家的特权就是，也许，有点疯。&quot;）罗斯还发现，告诉那些拒绝被编辑的作家，他们与编辑争论得越多，就越不配发表作品，这一招非常有用，而且非常聪明。罗斯还发现，对那些拒绝接受编辑的作家说，他们与编辑争论得越多，他们就越不值得被出版，这也是一个相当聪明的窍门。  
“The worse the writer is, the more argument; that is the rule,” he informed one very quarrelsome contributor. Stating this rule was an exceptionally effective way of getting a writer to pipe down. Then, too: it happens to be true.  
&quot;他告诉一位非常爱吵架的投稿人：&quot;作者越糟糕，争论就越多；这就是规则。说出这条规则是让作者闭嘴的一个特别有效的方法。然后，还有：这碰巧是真的。  
(I promise that my editor did not write that last sentence—he doesn’t even agree with it.)  
(我保证最后一句话不是我的编辑写的，他甚至都不同意）。

The relationship between an editor and a writer can be as intimate as an affair and as ineffable as a marriage, but it is also likely to involve two perfect strangers warily guarding a precise measure of distance: N95-masked and six feet apart, like pandemic shoppers, or flintlockarmed at ten paces, like eighteenth-century duellists.  
编辑和作家之间的关系可以像婚外情一样亲密，也可以像婚姻一样不可言喻，但也很可能是两个完全陌生的人在小心翼翼地保持着一定的距离：戴着 N95 口罩，相距六英尺，就像大流行病的购物者；或者手持燧发枪，相距十步，就像十八世纪的决斗者。  
“As to your query about coming to the office, we should be delighted to have you, though I should tell you that we aren’t very impressive to look at,” one editor gently warned one of her writers in 1967. S. N. Behrman and his editor, [Katharine White](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1996/02/26/katharine-white-profile-lady-with-a-pencil), worked together for twenty years before daring to use each other’s first names, and that only happened because they were thrown together in grief after Ross’s death. That’s not to say that the relationship is symmetrical. It’s not. It’s as lopsided as unrequited love.  
&quot;至于你关于来办公室的询问，我们很高兴你能来，不过我得告诉你，我们看起来并不令人印象深刻，&quot;1967 年，一位编辑温和地告诫她的一位作家。S. N. 贝尔曼和他的编辑凯瑟琳-怀特[](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1996/02/26/katharine-white-profile-lady-with-a-pencil)一起工作了20年，才敢使用对方的名字，而这只是因为罗斯去世后，他们在悲痛中走到了一起。这并不是说这种关系是对称的。事实并非如此。就像单相思一样一边倒。  
Writers are dizzy about their editors, as twitchy as teen-agers. _I write only for you_, Shawn’s writers used to tell him.  
作家们被编辑搞得晕头转向，像青少年一样神经兮兮。_我只为你写作_，肖恩的作家们经常这样告诉他。  
“In the wildly unlikely event that this Fragment does not meet your publication needs at this time, I would ask that you dispose of it thoroughly and irremediably—some combination of shredder and flame is usually sufficient,” David Foster Wallace wrote to his editor, Deborah Treisman, in 1999.  
&quot;1999年，大卫-福斯特-华莱士（David Foster Wallace）在写给编辑德博拉-特雷斯曼（Deborah Treisman）的信中说：&quot;万一这本《片段》暂时不能满足您的出版需求，我希望您能彻底、不可救药地处理掉它--通常碎纸机和火焰的组合就足够了。  
Steven Millhauser wrote her a limerick (“There once was a woman named Treisman; / Of grammar she was a policeman . . .”). Beattie kept a picture of Angell on her typewriter.  
史蒂文-米尔豪泽（Steven Millhauser）给她写了一首打油诗（&quot;从前有个女人叫特雷斯曼；/她是一名警察......&quot;）。比蒂在她的打字机上保留了一张安格尔的照片。  
Claudia Roth Pierpont once told Dorothy Wickenden, then the magazine’s executive editor, that writing involved “trying to acquire my own ‘internal Dorothy.’ ” Early in Adam Gopnik’s stint as a _New Yorker_ editor, he got a draft of a piece from [John Updike](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2009/02/09/john-updike). It was perfect, so he set it aside. Soon, he got a typewritten postcard from Updike:  
克劳迪娅-罗斯-皮尔庞特曾对时任杂志执行主编的多萝西-威肯登说，写作就是 &quot;努力获得我自己的&#39;内心的多萝西&#39;&quot;。&quot;亚当-高普尼克（Adam Gopnik）在担任_《纽约客》_编辑的初期，他收到了约翰-厄普代克（John Updike）的一篇文章草稿。它非常完美，所以他把它放在一边。不久，他收到了厄普代克寄来的一张打字明信片：

&gt; Dear Adam,  亲爱的亚当  
&gt; The piece recently submitted was  
&gt; 最近提交的作品是  
&gt; a) deemed unacceptable,  a) 认为不可接受、  
&gt; b) in need of significant rewriting,  
&gt; b) 需要大量改写、  
&gt; c) lost behind a radiator.  
&gt; c) 丢失在散热器后面。  
&gt; John  约翰

Updike—even Updike!—had been feverishly awaiting a reply, Gopnik realized. “Was anything wrong with the Auden review?” Updike once queried Shawn, scratching at the editorial door like a cat left outside for the night.  
戈普尼克意识到，厄普代克--甚至是厄普代克！一直在焦急地等待着回复。&quot;奥登的评论有什么问题吗？&quot;有一次，厄普代克问肖恩，他像一只被遗弃在外面过夜的猫一样，在编辑部的门前抓来抓去。  
“There has been an ominous gap since I turned it in.” He wrote constantly, and brilliantly, submitting fiction, poetry, and criticism to the magazine over six decades. He got plenty of rejections, and sometimes, like every other self-loathing writer, he all but asked for them.  
&quot;自从我把它交上去后，就出现了一个不祥的空档。&quot;六十年来，他不断写作，才华横溢，向杂志投递小说、诗歌和评论。他收到了很多退稿，有时，像其他自暴自弃的作家一样，他都会要求退稿。  
“I enclose a disk,” Updike once wrote to Finder, “but if you and Remnick are too let down, I will certainly understand.”  
&quot;我附上一张磁盘，&quot;厄普代克曾在给芬德的信中写道，&quot;但如果你和雷姆尼克太失望，我一定会理解的。&quot;

Writers are needier, every which way—[John O’Hara](https://www.newyorker.com/books/page-turner/the-eavesdroppers-secret-on-john-ohara) once sent Ross a letter that read, “I want more money I want more money I want more money I want more money I want more money I want more money I want more money I want more money I want more money I want more money I want more money”—but editors are desperate for good copy, delivered on deadline.  
At a weekly magazine, and even more so for its clock-tickingly digital version, this desperation can get awfully keen. The editor [Wolcott Gibbs](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1958/08/30/wolcott-gibbs) once sent Dorothy Parker a telegram that read, “_SWELL JAM I’M IN STOP COMMITTING SUICIDE IF NO COPY FROM YOU TODAY_.” Nearly a century later, forwarding to Wickenden an e-mail in which a writer all but admitted that he was never going to finish a long-awaited Profile of Noam Chomsky, Remnick asked, “Does someone have any Advil? Or a pistol?”

The hundredth anniversary of _The New Yorker_ is a lousy excuse for violating three of Harold Ross’s century-old rules: never write about writers, never name editors, and never write about the magazine. From the start, _The New Yorker_ avoided exactly this kind of palaver by making fun of it, at first by inventing a history and a pedigree that the publication never had. “When the magazine was founded in 1867 there were only two subscribers, both of them the Editor,” [Corey Ford wrote](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1925/10/31/xii-the-circulation-problem) in _The New Yorker’s_ first year—not 1867 but 1925—in a series called “The Making of a Magazine: A Tour Through the Vast Organization of The New Yorker,” a parody of the self-puffery that was an everlastingly irritating feature of _The New Yorker’s_ rival _Time_.  
_《纽约客》_创刊一百周年是一个糟糕的借口，因为它违反了哈罗德-罗斯的三条百年老规矩：永远不要写作家，永远不要说出编辑的名字，永远不要写杂志。从一开始，_《纽约客》_就通过取笑来避免这种麻烦，起初，它编造了一个该杂志从未有过的历史和血统。[科里-福特（Corey Ford）在_《纽约客》_创刊第一年--不是1867年，而是1925年--名为 &quot;一本杂志的诞生 &quot;的系列报道中写道：&quot;当杂志在1867年创刊时，只有两个订户，他们都是编辑：这是对_《纽约客》_的竞争对手_《时代》_一直以来令人讨厌的自吹自擂的模仿。](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1925/10/31/xii-the-circulation-problem)  
It was Ford who gave a name to the monocled toff who became the publication’s mascot, Eustace Tilley, and he gave him an even dandier grandfather, Terwilliger Tilley, a fictional founder, who oversaw the delivery of the magazine to ex-President James Buchanan (“an early supporter of the _New Yorker_”), by penny-farthing. Ford’s _Time_\-ese “Making of a Magazine” included a behind-the-scenes look at the editorial work undertaken at “[The Magazine’s Punctuation Farm](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1925/10/17/x-the-magazines-punctuation-farm)”: “The periods are set out in shallow pans under glass in the early Spring, and carefully watered; and after six weeks of sunshine each sends down a tiny root no bigger than a bean (,) which is called a _comma_.” (To be fair, the commas really were a problem. “Commas in _The_ _New Yorker_ fall with the precision of knives in a circus act, outlining the victim,” [E. B. White](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2005/02/14/andy) once remarked. “You have sprinkled commas about all over the pages as though you were putting raisins in a plum-pudding,” Roald Dahl complained, culinarily.)  
是福特为成为该刊物吉祥物的单帽托夫取名为尤斯塔斯-蒂利（Eustace Tilley），还为他取了一个更可爱的祖父--特尔维利格-蒂利（Terwilliger Tilley），他是一个虚构的创始人，负责监督将杂志通过分钱运费送到前总统詹姆斯-布坎南（&quot;_《纽约客》_的早期支持者&quot;）手中。福特的_《时代》_语 &quot;一本杂志的诞生 &quot;包括了&quot;[杂志的标点符号农场](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1925/10/17/x-the-magazines-punctuation-farm)&quot;编辑工作的幕后故事：&quot;初春时节，这些句号被放在玻璃下的浅盘中，并被仔细浇灌；经过六周的阳光照射，每个句号都会长出比豆子（）还小的根，这就是_逗号_&quot;。(公平地说，逗号确实是个问题。&quot;_《__纽约客》_中的逗号就像马戏表演中的刀子一样，准确无误地落下，勾勒出受害者的轮廓，&quot;[E. B.怀特](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2005/02/14/andy)曾经说过。&quot;罗尔德-达尔（Roald Dahl）曾抱怨说：&quot;你把逗号撒得满纸都是，就像在李子布丁里放葡萄干一样。

Ross, a lover of rules, had been much influenced as a cub reporter by Mark Twain’s essay on the literary offenses of James Fenimore Cooper. (“There are nineteen rules governing literary art in the domain of romantic fiction—some say twenty-two,” Twain wrote.  
罗斯是个规则爱好者，作为一名小记者，他深受马克-吐温关于詹姆斯-菲尼摩尔-库珀（James Fenimore Cooper）文学违法行为的文章的影响。(吐温写道：&quot;在浪漫主义小说领域，有十九条文学艺术规则--有人说是二十二条。  
“In ‘Deerslayer,’ Cooper violated eighteen of them.”) Ross brought Twain’s punishing standards to the swank but slipshod literary scene of nineteen-twenties New York City. Notwithstanding Ross’s aversion to making a fetish of the magazine, _New Yorker_ editors soon acquired a well-earned reputation for fussiness, not only about commas but about everything.  
&quot;在《屠鹿者》中，库珀违反了其中的十八条&quot;）。罗斯将吐温的惩罚性标准带到了十九世纪二十年代纽约市奢华但粗疏的文学界。尽管罗斯不喜欢把杂志当儿戏，_《纽约客》_的编辑们很快就赢得了 &quot;小题大做 &quot;的好名声，不仅在逗号上，而且在任何事情上都是如此。  
Ross, who was born in Colorado and had hair like a shaving brush and liked to pass himself off as a rube from the Great American West, as if he ran not a magazine but a chuck wagon, typed out page after page of queries about the pieces he read.  
罗斯出生在科罗拉多州，头发像剃须刷，喜欢把自己伪装成来自美国西部的乡巴佬，好像他经营的不是杂志而是一辆撵车。  
“Is cheese not a dessert,” he’d ask. Or: “Don’t think one wonders at. Think you marvel at and wonder why.” The Profile writer Margaret Case Harriman, whose father owned the Algonquin Hotel (she was born in Room 1206), could not abide the fussing.  
&quot;他会问：&quot;奶酪不是甜点吗？或者：&quot;不要以为你会惊叹。而应该是惊叹，并想知道为什么&quot;。简介作家玛格丽特-凯斯-哈里曼（Margaret Case Harriman）的父亲是阿尔冈昆酒店的老板（她出生在1206房间），她无法忍受这种大惊小怪。  
Ross objected to her use of the word “brood,” complaining that it had become “almost a _New Yorker_ word.” “Thurber broods,” he wrote. “People brood up and down 52nd Street. But does Mr. G. brood? I doubt it.” Harriman thwacked back: “Think A. Lincoln brooded before _New Yorker_ coined word.”  
罗斯反对她使用 &quot;brood &quot;一词，抱怨说这个词 &quot;几乎成了_《纽约客》_的一个词&quot;。&quot;瑟伯耿耿于怀，&quot;他写道。他写道，&quot;52 街上下的人们都耿耿于怀。但先生会耿耿于怀吗？我很怀疑&quot;。哈里曼回击道：&quot;在_《纽约客》_创造出这个词之前，我就认为A. Lincoln曾耿耿于怀&quot;。

Given the chance of such tussles, editors generally try to protect their writers, recasting tempestuous in-house queries in teapottier terms. “Dear Miss Manning,” Katharine White wrote to a poet.  
鉴于有可能发生这样的争吵，编辑们一般都会尽量保护自己的作者，用茶壶里的茶水来重新表达内部的激烈询问。&quot;亲爱的曼宁小姐&quot;，凯瑟琳-怀特写给一位诗人的信。  
“The verse ‘Oysterette’ is amusing enough except that we fear an oyster is not a crustacean. Is he? Perhaps you can make us a little clearer on this point.” A writer who was paying attention could often spot which notes came from whom.  
&quot;诗句&#39;牡蛎&#39;很有趣，只是我们担心牡蛎不是甲壳类动物。是吗？也许你能让我们更清楚一点&quot;。用心的作家往往能发现哪些注释来自谁。  
“I do not think that the remarks jotted down in pencil came from you,” Vladimir Nabokov wrote to White.  
&quot;弗拉基米尔-纳博科夫在写给怀特的信中说：&quot;我不认为用铅笔记下的那些话是你说的。  
“They are not in your style.” In such cases, White, sending out a proof, disarmed her authors: “Please don’t be alarmed by all the marks on it, as these are very few compared to many of our manuscripts and Mr. Ross always works particularly hard on things he likes.” Flattery gets you everywhere.  
&quot;它们不符合你的风格&quot;。在这种情况下，怀特寄出了校样，解除了作者们的疑虑：&quot;请不要对上面的所有标记感到惊慌，因为与我们的许多手稿相比，这些标记很少，而且罗斯先生总是对他喜欢的东西特别用心&quot;。奉承让你无处不在。

Editors suggest stories. “You might keep an eye on the Duke of Gloucester’s wedding in Westminster Abbey,” Conrad Aiken’s editor wrote to him in 1935.

You’ll be pleased to know, or indifferent, that a certain cocky manipulation of character is finally beginning to leave my work.” In 1941, he’d written an early draft of “[Slight Rebellion Off Madison](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1946/12/21/slight-rebellion-off-madison)”—the germ of “Catcher in the Rye”—but, despite promising that the story would appear in 1943, the magazine held on to it until the war ended. “ ‘Bananafish’ looks like better nonsense when it’s one word, don’t you think?” Salinger wrote Lobrano in 1948. Lobrano agreed.  
你会很高兴地知道，或者漠不关心地知道，我的作品中终于开始出现了某种狂妄自大的人物形象&quot;。1941 年，他写下了《麦田外的轻微叛乱》（[Slight Rebellion Off Madison](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1946/12/21/slight-rebellion-off-madison)）的初稿--这是《麦田里的守望者》的萌芽--但是，尽管他承诺这个故事将在 1943 年发表，杂志社还是把它保留到了战争结束。&quot;&#39;Bananafish&#39;是一个单词，看起来更像是废话，你觉得呢？塞林格在 1948 年写信给洛布拉诺。洛布拉诺同意了。  
But he rejected another Salinger story about Holden Caulfield. “We can’t help feeling that this story is too ingenious and ingrown,” Lobrano explained delicately.  
但他拒绝了塞林格另一个关于霍尔登-考尔菲德的故事。&quot;洛布拉诺巧妙地解释道：&quot;我们不禁觉得这个故事过于巧妙，而且不伦不类。

Ross resolved to change the course of the magazine, writing, in March of 1945, to one of the magazine’s foreign correspondents, Janet Flanner, in France, “The war is going to be over and forgotten before any number of real atrocity stories are printed, I’m afraid, unless the New Yorker gets around to doing something.” Meanwhile, the magazine kept on churning out much lighter fare.  
罗斯决心改变杂志的发展方向，1945 年 3 月，他给杂志驻法国的一名外国记者珍妮特-弗兰纳写道：&quot;除非《纽约客》有所行动，否则恐怕在刊出任何真正的暴行故事之前，战争就会结束并被遗忘。与此同时，《纽约客》杂志还在不断推出更轻松的内容。  
“The impression the magazine now gives anyone turning stuff in, is that material will first be completely dismantled, then assembled again in the assembling plant,” E. B.  
&quot;E.B.怀特在那年晚些时候向罗斯抱怨说：&quot;杂志现在给人的印象是，任何人上交的材料都会先被完全拆解，然后再在组装厂组装起来。  
White complained to Ross later that year, as if the Punctuation Farm had become an actual place, somewhere north of Queens.  
怀特在那年晚些时候向罗斯抱怨说，就好像 &quot;标点农场 &quot;变成了一个真实的地方，就在皇后区北部的某个地方。

It was Shawn, though, who proposed printing the entirety of John Hersey’s [account of Hiroshima after the bomb](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1946/08/31/hiroshima) in a single issue of the magazine, one of the best things _The New Yorker_ ever did, aside from publishing the entirety of Rachel Carson’s “[Silent Spring](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1962/06/16/silent-spring-part-1),” in 1962. When Shawn first read Carson’s piece, he called to tell her that it might change the course of history. Carson hung up the phone, collapsed, and wept.  
是肖恩提议在单期杂志上刊登约翰-赫西（John Hersey）的[《广岛原子弹爆炸后》](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1946/08/31/hiroshima)的全部内容，这是_《纽约客》_除了在1962年刊登蕾切尔-卡森（Rachel Carson）的《寂静的春天》之外做得最好的事情之一。当肖恩第一次读到卡森的文章时，他打电话告诉她，这篇文章可能会改变历史的进程。卡森挂了电话，瘫倒在地，哭了起来。

Ross ran a humor magazine; Shawn ran a literary magazine that elevated reporting. In the years of American prosperity that followed the Second World War, the cachet of _The New Yorker_ meant that it was flooded with advertisers. Shawn, needing to fill a swelling magazine’s pages, ran it like a book club, publishing some astonishingly important journalism, from Truman Capote’s “[In Cold Blood](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1965/09/25/in-cold-blood-the-last-to-see-them-alive)” to Richard Rovere’s letters from Washington.  
罗斯经营的是一本幽默杂志；肖恩经营的是一本文学杂志，提升了报道的档次。在第二次世界大战后美国繁荣的岁月里，《纽约客》的知名度意味着广告商蜂拥而至。肖恩需要填满膨胀的杂志版面，他像经营读书俱乐部一样经营《纽约客》，发表了一些令人震惊的重要新闻作品，从杜鲁门-卡波特的《冷血》[](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1965/09/25/in-cold-blood-the-last-to-see-them-alive)到理查德-罗维尔的《华盛顿来信》。  
But he also, especially as time wore on, ran no small number of staggeringly long and often mind-numbingly boring articles about little of consequence, or what Tina Brown took to calling the fifty-thousand-word piece about zinc—articles that, by the end of Shawn’s tenure, were no longer wending through pages of towering ads, avenues through a city of skyscrapers.  
但他也发表了不少令人吃惊的长篇文章，尤其是随着时间的推移，这些文章往往令人头昏脑胀、无聊至极，而且几乎没有什么意义，蒂娜-布朗（Tina Brown）把这些文章称为 &quot;关于锌的五万字文章&quot;--在肖恩任期结束时，这些文章已经不再在高耸入云的广告页中穿行，也不再在摩天大楼林立的城市中穿行。

Shawn referred to his own way of editing as The Tradition. It worked best in person. He would sit behind his big oak desk, and the writer would sit on a leather couch alongside it, both the desk and the couch covered with stacks of manuscripts, the _New Yorker_ veteran Michael J. Arlen told me. Arlen, now ninety-four, started at the magazine in 1956.  
肖恩将自己的剪辑方式称为 &quot;传统&quot;。这种方式当面进行效果最好。_《纽约客》_资深编辑迈克尔-J-阿伦（Michael J. Arlen）告诉我，他会坐在他的大橡木书桌后面，作家则坐在旁边的皮沙发上，书桌和沙发上都堆满了手稿。现年九十四岁的阿伦于 1956 年开始在该杂志工作。  
He and Shawn would go through proofs together, Arlen recounted, “and when something in the writing wasn’t good enough, invariably a soft patch in the middle where some line of reasoning or observation or whatever had gone off the rails (and which one was all too aware of oneself but hoped wouldn’t be noticed), just when we were about to conclude, Shawn would say in his whispery consigliere voice, ‘Why don’t you take these paragraphs downstairs (handing me a page of the proofs) and run it through your typewriter again and see if you don’t like it better?’ ”  
阿伦回忆说，他和肖恩会一起审校稿件，&quot;当稿件中有些地方写得不够好时，中间总会有一块软肋，一些推理、观察或其他方面出了偏差（自己也很清楚，但希望不要被发现）、就在我们准备结束的时候，肖恩会用他那低声下气的顾问口吻说：&#39;你为什么不把这些段落拿到楼下（递给我一页校样），再用你的打字机打一遍，看看你是不是更喜欢了？&#39;&quot;

For out-of-town writers, The Tradition usually required the telephone.  
对于外地作家，《传统》通常需要电话联系。  
Ross typed queries, but Shawn, who had been known to follow the Ross method, once typing up a list of a hundred and seventy-eight queries about a profile of an automaker, preferred to meet face to face or, failing that, to make phone calls or, failing that, to send telegrams. Shawn started working at the magazine in 1933, and became the managing editor six years later.  
罗斯是打字询问，但肖恩也曾效仿罗斯的方法，有一次他打出了一份包含一百七十八条询问的清单，内容是关于一家汽车制造商的简介，他更喜欢面对面交谈，如果不行，就打电话，如果不行，就发电报。肖恩于 1933 年开始在该杂志工作，六年后成为执行主编。  
He was savvy enough to know what future researchers might do with his archive, and therefore made a point of never writing much down. “_PLEASE PHONE ME IF POSSIBLE_,” he telegrammed Rovere, _The New Yorker’s_ Washington correspondent, who had been in the habit of mailing him three-page, single-spaced typewritten letters. In 1953, Shawn telegrammed Lillian Ross: “_EXCITED TODAY TO RECEIVE PIECE AT LAST MORE EXCITED WHEN EYE READ IT PERIOD YOU ARE WRITING BETTER AND WITH MORE AUTHORITY THAN EVER PERIOD IMPATIENT TO SEE SECOND PART BOTH AS EDITOR AND BECAUSE OF SUSPENSE IN STORY AS READER LOVE BILL_.” By then, she was both his writer and his lover. (Their affair lasted until his death, more than three decades later.)  
他很精明，知道未来的研究人员可能会利用他的档案做什么，因此他从不多写。他给_《纽约客》_驻华盛顿记者罗维雷打电报说：&quot;如果可能，请给我打电话。&quot;罗维雷习惯于给他邮寄长达三页、单行距的打字信件。1953 年，肖恩给莉莲-罗斯发电报：&quot;今天终于收到了你的作品，我很激动，当我读到你的作品时，我的眼睛更兴奋了，你写得比以前更好，更有权威性，我迫不及待地想看到第二部分，因为我是编辑，也因为故事中的悬念，因为读者喜欢你的作品。那时，她既是他的作家，也是他的情人。(他们的恋情一直持续到三十多年后他去世）。

Shawn, walking backward on a snowy path in the woods, swept away his footprints with a pine-tree branch. He refused to be interviewed, even after he was attacked in the press. Instead, J. D. Salinger, helped by [Lillian Ross](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2017/10/02/lillian-ross-a-pioneer-of-literary-journalism-has-died-at-ninety-nine), drafted a heated, twenty-three-page defense: “Shawn _single-handed_ actually _invented_ various forms of reporting; found writers who could develop these forms; and encouraged each contributor to create individual expressions of reporting,” Salinger wrote. “Would-be imitators have never been able to catch on to how the New Yorker does it.  
肖恩倒退着走在林中的雪路上，用松树枝扫掉了自己的脚印。他拒绝接受采访，甚至在遭到媒体攻击后也是如此。相反，J.D.塞林格在[莉莲-罗斯](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2017/10/02/lillian-ross-a-pioneer-of-literary-journalism-has-died-at-ninety-nine)的帮助下，起草了一份长达23页的激烈辩护词：&quot;肖恩_单枪匹马_实际上_发明了_各种报道形式；找到了能够发展这些形式的作家；并鼓励每个撰稿人创造出个性化的报道表现形式，&quot;塞林格写道。&quot;那些想模仿《纽约客》的人永远也无法领会《纽约客》是如何做到这一点的。  
(A good, fat part of that ‘how’ is Shawn.)” Shawn, Salinger insisted, had “kept his staggering virtues quiet, or hidden them here, there and everywhere in dozens of writers, artists and editors, or even mysteriously made it seem, to some writers, that their special accomplishments were one hundred percent achieved on their very own.”  
(这个&#39;如何&#39;的一个重要组成部分就是肖恩）&quot;。塞林格坚持认为，肖恩 &quot;对自己惊人的美德缄口不言，或者把它们藏在几十位作家、艺术家和编辑的身上，或者甚至神秘地让一些作家觉得，他们的特殊成就百分之百是他们自己取得的&quot;。

For later editors, little effort was required to leave no trace behind. Phone calls were faster than telegrams, and, especially after the magazine stopped hiring secretaries, they were a lot easier than letters.  
对于后来的编辑来说，要想不留下任何痕迹，几乎不费吹灰之力。电话比电报快，尤其是在杂志社不再雇用秘书之后，电话比信件更方便。  
(Remnick likes to text.) During the Shawn years, the pace of publishing changed, and so did the machinery: phototypesetting, word processors, desktop computers. “Here, at last, are those _TURKEY SEASON_ galleys,” McGrath wrote in 1980 to Alice Munro, who preferred doing business by mail.  
(雷姆尼克喜欢发短信）在肖恩时期，出版业的步伐发生了变化，机器也是如此：照相制版、文字处理器、台式电脑。&quot;麦格拉斯在1980年写给爱丽丝-门罗（Alice Munro）的信中说：&quot;终于有了《土耳其季节》的样书。

&gt; The reason for the delay is that these proofs are the first ever set up by some new photographic process we’re dropping our Linotype machine in favor of. . . .  
&gt; 延误的原因是，这些样稿是我们首次使用某种新的照相工艺制作的，我们将放弃使用 Linotype 机器。. . .  
&gt; These galleys look the same as the old ones—or almost the same—and our production department assumes that nothing else will change, but I don’t believe it for a minute.  
&gt; 这些样张看起来和旧样张一样--或者几乎一样--我们的制作部门认为其他方面不会有什么变化，但我一点也不相信。  
&gt; I’m sure that this is just the first step in a sinister plot, and that before long we will all be wired into a master computer that will write, print, buy, and read the magazine all by itself.  
&gt; 我相信，这只是一个险恶阴谋的第一步，用不了多久，我们都会被连接到一台主计算机上，由它自己来编写、印刷、购买和阅读杂志。

In the nineteen-nineties, _The_ _New Yorker’s_ corporate owner set up a system that, while it lasted, automatically deleted e-mail after a few months. Texts vanish. The editorial trail goes cold.  
十九世纪九十年代，_《纽约客》__的所有者建立了一个系统，该系统在使用几个月后自动删除电子邮件。文本消失了。编辑线索消失。_

Every editor brings in new editors and new writers, new sailcloth and planking for Theseus’ ship. “There is about them all the air of a man who has lost his wife and four children in an outboard motorboat accident,” Thurber complained about an aging staff.  
每一位编辑都会带来新的编辑和新的作家 为忒修斯的船带来新的帆布和木板&quot;瑟伯抱怨说：&quot;他们都像一个在舷外摩托艇事故中失去了妻子和四个孩子的人。  
Under Shawn, the magazine grew more serious. He brought on some of the magazine’s most distinctive and important writers, from [Dwight Macdonald](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2011/09/05/browbeaten?printable&#61;true&amp;currentPage&#61;all) and [James Baldwin](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1998/02/16/the-enemy-within-hilton-als) and John McPhee to Calvin Trillin and Jane Kramer and Jamaica Kincaid. He gave Baldwin an advance to write a series of pieces on Africa. Baldwin went there but never wrote those pieces; he gave Shawn something else instead. _The New Yorker_ published “[Letter from a Region of My Mind](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1962/11/17/letter-from-a-region-in-my-mind),” the heart of “The Fire Next Time,” in 1962.  
在肖恩的领导下，杂志变得更加严肃。他请来了杂志上一些最独特、最重要的作家，从[德怀特-麦克唐纳](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2011/09/05/browbeaten?printable&#61;true&amp;currentPage&#61;all)、[詹姆斯-鲍德温](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1998/02/16/the-enemy-within-hilton-als)和约翰-麦克菲到卡尔文-特里林、简-克莱默和牙买加-金凯德。他给了鲍德温一笔预付款，让他写一系列关于非洲的文章。鲍德温去了那里，但没有写出那些作品；他给了肖恩一些别的东西。_《纽约客》_于1962年发表了《下一次的火焰》的核心--&quot;[来自我心灵某个区域的信](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1962/11/17/letter-from-a-region-in-my-mind)&quot;。

The battles between editors and writers raged on. “This paragraph has been recast and is no longer recognizable as my style,” Nadine Gordimer complained to William Maxwell.  
编辑和作家之间的争斗仍在继续。&quot;纳丁-戈迪默（Nadine Gordimer）向威廉-马克斯韦尔（William Maxwell）抱怨说：&quot;这一段已经被改头换面，再也看不出是我的风格了。  
Charles McGrath once hung up on Alec Wilkinson (the only time he ever hung up on a writer), and let me tell you this: they don’t even agree on what the fight was about. Harold Brodkey railed at Tina Brown for publishing a Critics piece that indicted Bill T.  
查尔斯-麦格拉斯（Charles McGrath）曾经挂过亚历克-威尔金森（Alec Wilkinson）的电话（这是他唯一一次挂断作家的电话），让我告诉你：他们甚至都不知道争吵的原因是什么。哈罗德-布罗基（Harold Brodkey）抨击蒂娜-布朗（Tina Brown）发表了一篇《评论家》文章，控诉比尔-T.  
Jones’s dance “Still / Here,” which the dance critic had refused to see, as “victim art.” Brodkey, dying of _AIDS_, faxed Brown, “Want some more victim art? See you at my funeral.”  
琼斯的舞蹈 &quot;Still / Here&quot;，这位舞蹈评论家拒绝观看，认为这是 &quot;受害者艺术&quot;。布罗基死于艾滋病，他给布朗发传真说：&quot;还想再来点受害者艺术吗？我的葬礼上见。&quot;

Some editors are bullies. “Shawn believed that all writers were children who should be indulged,” Mark Singer, who started at the magazine in 1974, told me, “and Gottlieb believed all writers were children who should not be indulged.” Others are merely unmovable.  
有些编辑是恶霸。&quot;肖恩认为所有的作家都是孩子，应该被纵容，&quot;1974 年开始在杂志工作的马克-辛格告诉我，&quot;戈特利布认为所有的作家都是孩子，不应该被纵容。还有一些人只是不为所动。  
“This purchase is conditional on your acceptance of a considerable amount of editing,” Roger Angell warned Don DeLillo, about [the story](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1971/11/27/game-plan) that became the novel “[End Zone](https://www.amazon.com/End-Zone-Don-DeLillo/dp/0140085688/).” Then, too, some writers are mulish. “I (literally) spent more time and effort restoring what I’d written than writing it,” the film critic [Pauline Kael](https://www.newyorker.com/books/double-take/sunday-reading-the-electrifying-critical-mind-of-pauline-kael) wrote.  
&quot;罗杰-安格尔（Roger Angell）在谈到后来成为小说《末日地带》（[End Zone](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1971/11/27/game-plan)）的[故事](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1971/11/27/game-plan)时警告唐-德里罗（Don DeLillo）说：&quot;你必须接受大量的编辑工作，才有可能买下这本书&quot;。然后，有些作家也是闷闷不乐的。&quot;影评人[保利娜-凯尔](https://www.newyorker.com/books/double-take/sunday-reading-the-electrifying-critical-mind-of-pauline-kael)写道：&quot;我（真的）花了比写作更多的时间和精力来还原我所写的东西。  
“The editors tried to turn me into just what I’d been struggling not to be: a genteel, fuddy-duddy stylist.” Martin Amis once replied to editorial notes sent to him by Deborah Treisman by notifying her that she appeared to be under the mistaken impression that what he had sent her was a draft.  
&quot;编辑们试图把我变成我一直在努力避免成为的那种人：一个风度翩翩、老气横秋的文体家&quot;。马丁-艾米斯曾在回复德博拉-特雷斯曼寄给他的编辑笔记时通知她，她似乎误以为他寄给她的是草稿。

The worst writers can’t stand being edited; the worst editors write for their writers.  
最糟糕的作家无法忍受被编辑；最糟糕的编辑为作家写作。  
(For the record, my editor disagrees with this wild assertion of mine, too.) There’s also a certain kind of editor who wants to take credit for a writer’s work—“This was garbage until I fixed it up”—even when that’s not even a little bit true.  
(我的编辑也不同意我的这一狂妄论断。）还有一种编辑想为作家的作品邀功--&quot;在我修改之前，这就是个垃圾&quot;--哪怕事实并非如此。  
Sometimes, stuck with a handsy editor, you can get your way if you’ve got enough clout.  
有时候，如果你有足够的影响力，就能在编辑的刁难下讨回公道。  
“I am willing and eager to consider fairly and respectfully all suggested cuts and changes and omissions you may want to make, but this series is not subject to any editing whatever without my knowledge and consent,” Thurber wrote to Shawn, as if filing a legal notice.

In a letter to Katharine White, Nabokov drew a line not so much in the proverbial sand as with the underline key on his typewriter: “I shall be very grateful to you if you help me to weed out bad grammar but I do not think I would like my longish sentences clipped too close, or those drawbridges lowered which I have taken such pains to lift.” White later wrote to Updike, “Nabokov’s the best writer in English but sometimes he’s maddening and I do not like what his ego has done to make him so very complex.” Updike once expressed the same kind of exasperation about a Nabokov novel: “There seem to be a lot of hostile parentheses.”  
在写给凯瑟琳-怀特的信中，纳博科夫没有在沙子上划线，而是用打字机上的下划线键划了一条线：&quot;如果你能帮我剔除语法错误，我会非常感激你，但我想我不希望我的长句被剪得太近，也不希望我费尽心机架起的那些吊桥被降低&quot;。怀特后来在给厄普代克的信中说：&quot;纳博科夫是英国最好的作家，但有时他让人抓狂，我不喜欢他的自负让他变得如此复杂&quot;。厄普代克曾对纳博科夫的一部小说表达过同样的愤懑：&quot;似乎有很多充满敌意的括号&quot;

Worse than being over-edited is being unpublished, and a depressing percentage of _The New Yorker’s_ archives—more than twenty-five hundred manuscript boxes at the New York Public Library (welcome to my foxhole!)—consists of letters of rejection, not only to unknown writers but also to well-known writers and to longtime contributors, an archive of agony.

_The New Yorker_ paid its writers terribly—possibly one reason that, early on, it published so many women writers was that it could get away with paying them peanuts—and it did not reliably accept writers’ submissions. There’s a reason that Updike fretted so much.  
_《纽约客》（The New Yorker）_付给作家的稿酬非常高，早期《纽约客》发表了那么多女作家的作品，其中一个原因可能就是它可以不付给她们花生稿酬，而且《纽约客》并不可靠地接受作家的投稿。厄普代克如此焦虑是有原因的。  
Other magazines print most of what their established contributors submit; from the start, _The New Yorker_ refused to do that, rejecting submissions even from its star writers—sometimes for years—leaving many of them, especially fiction writers, in precarious financial straits.  
其他杂志刊登的大多是其知名撰稿人的投稿；而_《纽约客》_从一开始就拒绝这样做，甚至拒绝其明星作家的投稿--有时一拒绝就是好几年，这使得许多明星作家，尤其是小说作家，陷入了岌岌可危的经济困境。

&quot;1930 年，当奥格登-纳什开始向杂志投稿时，凯瑟琳-怀特曾写信给他说：&quot;你很快就会成为我们最喜欢的诗人。

但杂志变了，纳什的能力也减弱了，到了 19 世纪 60 年代，当罗杰-安格尔-怀特的儿子成为纳什的编辑时，情况就不一样了。&quot;1961年，安格尔写信给纳什说：&quot;当我们不得不拒绝奥格登-纳什的诗歌时，这是黑暗的一天。

Shawn was ushered out the door, in 1987, by the magazine’s new owner, the magnate S. I. Newhouse, and replaced by the longtime Knopf editor-in-chief Bob Gottlieb. Loyalists were furious.  
1987年，肖恩被杂志社的新主人--巨头S.I.纽豪斯赶出了家门，取而代之的是长期担任克诺普夫主编的鲍勃-戈特利布。忠实拥护者勃然大怒。
